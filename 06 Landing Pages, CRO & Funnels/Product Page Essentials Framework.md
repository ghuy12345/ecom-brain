# Product Page Essentials Framework

Framework by Josh Omosebi, Seba Agency.

## The idea in one line

Every ecommerce store needs a few product page (PDP) basics in place before anything clever. The ads bring the right person in (see [[Simplifying Meta Ads Structure Framework]]); the PDP has to close them.

## Essential 1: Name the product after its mechanism

The title should spell out the result the product delivers, rather than merely its category.

Instead of the category label "Men's skincare cream", pick a name that states what it does:

- "Anti-wrinkle cream"
- "Acne scar cream"
- "Anti-ageing cream"
- "Skin-repairing cream", where X, Y and Z are the things it repairs

**The payoff:** the benefit registers immediately, and your USP is built into the name.

**How to write it**
1. What result or mechanism does it deliver?
2. For whom, or against which problem?
3. Put that in the name rather than hiding it in the description.

**Test:** show the title alone to a stranger and ask what problem the product addresses; they should answer correctly.

## Essential 2: Build your reviews

Reviews are non-negotiable. Build them in levels.

**Level 1: genuine reviews.** Buyers can usually tell when reviews are fake or written by AI, so post authentic ones the moment they arrive. When the product promises a visible change, such as skincare, you need real people, and AI content is no substitute.

**Level 2: visual reviews.** Add GIFs or short videos.

**Level 3: organic winners as reviews.** With a sizeable organic audience, place your best-performing organic videos, especially review-style ones, on the page. They already have proven engagement, feel relatable, show the transformation buyers want, and act as proof.

**The review ladder**
```
No reviews (fix this first) → genuine written reviews (the baseline) → GIFs or short clips (a step up) → top organic videos (strongest, if you have an organic following)
```

## Essential 3: Social proof, layered and relevant to buyers

**Types:** expert or doctor backing, certifications, celebrity endorsements, press coverage, any other credibility.

**Rule 1: show it.** Every relevant credential belongs on the page; don't bury it.

**Rule 2: relevance to the buyer.** A pet-food brand's backing from a famous startup accelerator means something to backers, not dog owners, so it stays off the PDP.

**The relevance filter:** would a shopper weigh this when deciding? If yes, display it prominently. If it would only interest backers or industry peers, drop it.

## Essential 4: Explain how to use it

Spell out usage on the page, as numbered steps when the process has them. Doing so answers the "am I able to use this, and does it suit me?" hesitation before the buyer reaches checkout.

## The summary formula

**Converting PDP = mechanism-based name + reviews + relevant proof + usage steps + description.**

1. **Title:** the mechanism and the USP both appear in the name.
2. **Reviews:** start with genuine ones, then add video, then organic winners.
3. **Social proof:** include whatever matters to the buyer and leave out the rest.
4. **How to use:** a numbered sequence placed on the page itself.
5. **Description:** handles the essentials.

## Where it appears in the metrics

- **Add-to-cart rate** (adds to cart ÷ landing page views) reflects these basics: about 5 to 10% is healthy, above 10% strong.
- **Landing page view rate** depends on a fast page: aim for 90% or more.

Both are covered in [[Meta Ad Metrics Framework - Part 2]].

## Product page audit checklist

- [ ] Title states the mechanism or result
- [ ] Genuine reviews, no AI reviews
- [ ] Real people for transformation products
- [ ] GIF or short-video reviews
- [ ] Organic winners reused as reviews (if available)
- [ ] All relevant credentials displayed
- [ ] Every proof element passes the relevance filter
- [ ] Usage steps on the page
- [ ] Description handles the essentials
