# Routing Traffic by Awareness Level

## What it is
A routing decision you make before writing any page copy. The awareness levels from Eugene Schwartz's Breakthrough Advertising (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most-aware) tell you what a visitor needs next, and so which page they should arrive on. Page format, which mechanism you lead with, hook style and button wording all flow from that call.

The commercial point: because most stores send every click to a product page, they only really convert people who already know the product or are ready to buy. The largest groups of potential buyers are problem-aware and solution-aware, and they need a pre-sale page that teaches and differentiates before they see an offer.

## When to use it
- Planning a new funnel or adding a traffic source.
- When cold visitors bounce off a product page.
- When retargeting underperforms.
- When deciding whether an ad should point at a collection, a product or a pre-sale page.

## How (steps)
1. **Place the traffic.** Most cold paid traffic already knows it has the problem, so do not spend the page convincing them of it; head toward the answer. Truly unaware audiences are rarely worth chasing, because shifting their beliefs costs too much per sale.
2. **Give each level what it needs:**
   - **Problem-aware:** describe the problem the way they would, deepen it, reveal that a fix exists, show why what they tried before did not work, and present the product as the obvious next step. Format: advertorial or educational listicle. Button tone: an invitation.
   - **Solution-aware:** recognise the options they have already tried, explain where those fall short, introduce your mechanism and what makes it different, and back it with people who had the same history. Format: a listicle built around comparison, a dedicated comparison page, or an advertorial that leans on differentiation. Button tone: "see how it compares".
   - **Product-aware:** open with the deal or the strongest proof, add a guarantee and genuine urgency to beat "maybe later". Format: sales/offer page.
   - **Most-aware:** a clean product or bundle offer, a quick checkout, honest urgency and a simple guarantee.
3. **Route each source:**
   - Cold broad, interest or lookalike audiences, video viewers who did not click, and non-branded problem searches go to a pre-sale page.
   - People who read the pre-sale page and left go to the offer page.
   - People who viewed a product go back to that product page with a stronger offer.
   - Email subscribers go to an offer or product page; past buyers to a product page or a new launch.
   - Branded searches go to the offer page.
   - Organic followers go to either an offer or a pre-sale page.
   - Creator and influencer traffic usually goes to a pre-sale page, often an advertorial.
4. **Match the destination to the ad's promise.** There is no fixed rule between collection and product pages. If the ad shows a range, land on the range; if it features one item, land on that item. A gift-guide ad that drops people on a single product breaks its own promise.
5. **Keep one language end to end.** Ads in one language pointing at a site in another convert badly. Translate the ads and the pages together.

## Example
Hypothetical funnel for a joint-support supplement: a broad cold ad opens with "why stretching hasn't fixed your knee" and links to a listicle explaining the overlooked cause. Readers continue to an offer page with bundles and a guarantee. Anyone who read the listicle but did not buy is retargeted directly to the offer page, not shown the listicle again. Branded searchers skip both and land on the offer. When the brand enters a Spanish-speaking market, it translates the pages as well as the ads.

## Common mistakes
- Pointing cold traffic at a product page.
- Giving product-aware visitors a problem-led page when they just want the deal.
- Giving cold visitors a hard offer before they trust you.
- Retargeting readers to a pre-sale page they have already seen.
- Assuming all cold traffic is unaware.
- A language mismatch between ad and site.
