# Sale Pages, Discount Formats and Savings Display

## What it is
How to run a sale so the savings are obvious at every step. Two decisions matter: the **discount format** (how the reduction is applied) and **savings congruence** (whether the sale message survives from the ad all the way to checkout). A sale that customers have to calculate, or that disappears at checkout, leaks conversions at the worst possible moment.

Shopify lets you discount four things (specific products or collections, buy-X-get-Y deals, the whole order, or shipping) using three formats:
- **Manual code:** the shopper enters it at checkout. Suits cart-recovery emails, creator codes and private or VIP sales. That extra step loses some buyers, and the saving stays hidden until checkout unless a link applies it for them.
- **Automatic discount:** kicks in on any qualifying order and is visible in the cart. Suits store-wide sales. Some full-price buyers will benefit too; during a public sale that is simply good service. Product pages will not display it unless you add custom code.
- **Compare-at markdown:** a crossed-out price and sale tag on the product page. The cart says nothing about savings, it is tedious across a big catalogue, and it cannot be scheduled.

## When to use it
- Planning Black Friday, holiday or seasonal sales.
- When a sale converts worse than expected.
- When deciding whether a holiday offer needs its own page.

## How (steps)
1. **Pick the format.** Default to automatic discounts for sales. Use codes for targeted audiences, ideally delivered by links that pre-apply them. Discount codes have one real advantage over markdowns: checkout displays the saving, which builds trust.
2. **Configure carefully.** For automatic dollar-off discounts, apply once per order (otherwise it applies per item) and set a minimum spend above the discount value. Enter the start date before you save, or the discount goes live at once. Limit how many times creator codes can be used. Allow stacking mainly with shipping discounts.
3. **Run the congruence checklist:**
   - Announcement bar and buttons state the offer.
   - Collection pages repeat it.
   - Product pages show the struck price, name the deal and say when it ends.
   - Cart shows the updated price and total savings.
   - Savings appear again just above the checkout button.
   - Checkout carries the same message.
   The principle: the shopper should never need a calculator. Where evergreen and sale discounts stack, show the combined saving.
4. **Give holiday sales their own page** built around a truly reduced offer, and adapt your proven ads with holiday messaging instead of starting fresh. The discount must be real, not a dressed-up regular price.
5. **Test the sale homepage against a sale landing page** (language, visuals, whether the button goes to a product or a collection) and look for conversion gains compared with the previous week.
6. **Prefer a distinct offer.** Rebranding your evergreen offer for Black Friday works, but a stronger, clearly different offer usually captures more.
7. **Read offer tests carefully.** When a cheaper offer wins with returning visitors but the main offer earns more per session overall, give it more time, then try combining them as tiers (a smaller saving on one unit, a bigger one on three).
8. **With generous margins,** cut bundle prices harder while keeping the single unit appealing to first-time buyers.
9. **After the holiday,** keep a working offer alive by updating the page, not its address, so ad learning is preserved.

## Example
Hypothetical: a candle brand runs 25% off for Black Friday via an automatic discount. The announcement bar, collection banner and product pages all say "25% off everything, ends Monday". The cart shows "You're saving $18.40" above the checkout button, and checkout repeats it. A dedicated sale page with a gift-set offer runs against the sale homepage; the dedicated page wins on revenue per session.

## Common mistakes
- Manual markdowns that leave the cart silent about savings.
- Dollar-off discounts that apply per item by accident.
- Discounts that go live early because no start date was set.
- Fake "sale" prices or countdowns that do not end.
- Judging a Black Friday result as if it were a normal month's baseline.
