# Testing Landing Pages Through Ad Spend

## What it is
Instead of waiting for split-test significance, you attach a new page to ads and let the ad account judge it. The page earns real, profitable sales while it is being tested, and if it beats target you scale it immediately. This is the practical route for most stores, which do not have the order volume for classic split tests.

It is not for very small spend: a tiny page test cannot produce enough sales to read and burns budget you need for finding winning ads. Until you have a proven angle and meaningful spend, send traffic to a simple product page (see [[Landing Pages as a Scaling Lever]]). After that, there are two ways to do it.

**Method A: a dedicated page-test campaign.** A separate ABO campaign (budgets set per ad set, which guarantees each one spends) running a proven ad that links to the new page. Cleaner read, but it costs dedicated budget.

**Method B: inside the main campaign.** Copy proven ads, swap in the new page's link, and add them to the campaign already carrying your budget (usually a CBO) so the platform decides where money goes. More cost-efficient, closer to real conditions, and spend naturally drifts toward the page that converts better.

## When to use it
Once the precondition is met, choose by budget and by what you need from the test:
- **Use Method B (inside the main CBO)** when cost efficiency matters most, when you care more about how the page performs under real delivery than about a pure comparison, and when speed matters. Expect surprises: the page you expected to lose can produce the top spender. Avoid splitting pages this way below roughly $2k/day.
- **Use Method A (a separate ABO)** when you have more budget to dedicate and want a cleaner, isolated read, or when the control is your homepage, whose traffic cannot simply be split by redirect.
- **Either way,** if a page works in a dedicated test after about a week, add it to the main campaign so it runs beside the originals; watch that it does not steal spend from proven ads without earning it.

## How (steps)
**Method A**
1. Create a Sales campaign with manual setup and an obvious name, with campaign budget optimisation switched off.
2. Give each ad set a meaningful budget, roughly $100/day or more as a rough guide; smaller budgets take too long to read.
3. Dynamic creative off. Rebuild the champion ad by hand (same video, primary text and headline), since an existing post cannot just be given a new link.
4. Paste the new page link and check it again after any duplication. Use a "Learn More" button for story or educational pages. Destination: website. Audience: broad.
5. Attribution: 7-day click, so the page is credited only with buyers who actually clicked through.
6. Optional on larger accounts: a "control" ad set with the same ad pointing at the old page, so you compare against a matched control rather than the account in general.
7. QA the ad itself: open the preview on a phone, tap the button, confirm the right page loads.

**Method B**
1. With existing winners: create a page-test ad set in the main campaign holding the top three to five ads (or those best suited to the page), all pointing at the new page.
2. With new creative: build a few concepts, each with a couple of hook or visual variations, for page A, then duplicate each ad with the link changed to page B, so every creative appears on both pages.
3. If you add duplicates into an existing ad set, note it in your log, because page and concept results are read per ad set.

**Offer tests**
Test a new offer as a pair: offer-specific ads (statics that state the offer plainly work well) pointing at an offer-specific page, tested together in the campaign.

Write fresh creative for a new page instead of reusing winners when the page uses a different angle or awareness level; reuse the winner when the page continues the same angle and should be the only variable.

## Example
Hypothetical: at $300/day with no proven angle, a brand tests no pages. At $3,000/day, with one angle carrying the account, it uses Method B: its top four ads pointed at a new listicle in an ad set inside the main CBO. The listicle wins a growing share of spend and lifts AOV. Later, at $10,000/day, it wants a clean read on a radically different advertorial and runs Method A: two ABO ad sets at $200/day, one to the product page and one to the advertorial.

## Common mistakes
- Leaving CBO on in a dedicated test, so spend drifts away and the test starves.
- Not re-checking links after duplicating ads.
- Judging the page on view-through purchases.
- Missing an ad on one of the two pages, which leaves the comparison incomplete.
- Testing pages at very small spend or before an angle is proven.
