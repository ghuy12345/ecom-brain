# Choosing a Pre-Sale Page Type

## What it is
A pre-sale page (also called a pre-lander or bridge page) sits between the ad and the offer page. It does the belief-shifting so the offer page only has to close. There are several formats, and each leans on a different trust mechanism: story, education, third-party authority, comparison, volume of reviews, or science.

Two principles keep the choice in proportion. First, precision beats format: how exactly the content addresses its reader counts for more than which layout you picked; a listicle or advertorial that nails one avatar's pain beats a "correct" format written generically. Second, you usually cannot predict the winner, so having several distinct pages to test is worth more than agonising over the first pick.

## When to use it
- When you have decided a traffic source needs pre-selling (see [[Routing Traffic by Awareness Level]]).
- When an existing pre-sale page has plateaued and you want a different trust mechanism to test against it.

## How (steps)
1. **Decide by awareness level, then by the kind of trust needed.** Use this as a default map:
   - Problem-aware, emotional, complex problem: advertorial (fallback: founder story).
   - Problem- or solution-aware, tangible benefits, need it fast: listicle (fallback: advertorial).
   - Solution-aware in a crowded market with named rivals: comparison page (fallback: listicle).
   - Solution- or product-aware, with hundreds of good reviews: a customer review page (fallback: comparison).
   - Skeptical, health-adjacent buyers: expert authority page (fallback: advertorial).
   - Sophisticated buyers who want the science: science/mechanism page (fallback: expert).
   As a broad rule, advertorials educate and so suit colder, less aware readers; listicles suit readers who already know solutions exist.
2. **When unsure, build a listicle.** It goes up quickly, fits most products and sets a reliable benchmark.
3. **Use hybrids for mixed traffic.** Actual traffic is almost always a mix of levels. Graft one block onto a base type: an advertorial with a comparison table for readers who are moved but still comparing; listicle plus founder portrait; a listicle in which a matching review backs up each reason; expert-led criteria settled by a comparison table; advertorial where an expert, not a founder, delivers the mechanism.
4. **Follow the default section order** for your type (details in [[Advertorial Landing Pages]], [[Listicle Landing Pages]] and [[Alternative Pre-Sale Page Types]]).
5. **Apply the assembly rules every time:**
   - Two or more calls to action, one halfway and one closing, with the closing one the most prominent.
   - Proof sits beside the claim it supports, never pooled at the bottom.
   - Switch between text-heavy and image-heavy sections so neighbouring blocks never look alike.
   - Item, step and review counts come from your content, not a fixed template.
   - Before/after visuals are mandatory for skincare and beauty.
   - No header, footer or navigation on pre-sale pages.
   - Mobile: readable body text (16px and up), buttons big enough to tap (44x44px minimum), nothing that scrolls sideways, and the main button close at hand.
   - A missing image should never block publishing; use a placeholder and replace it later.

## Example
Hypothetical: a collagen brand's traffic is mostly women who have tried other powders. That is solution-aware in a crowded market, so the default is a comparison page with a listicle fallback. Because the brand also has 600 detailed reviews, the team builds a listicle-review hybrid as a second test: five reasons, each proven by a review from someone who switched. Both pages run against the same ads; whichever wins becomes the control for the next round.

## Common mistakes
- Picking a format out of habit rather than from the reader's awareness.
- Expecting the format to do the work. Pouring generic content into a proven template does not create a winner.
- Testing only one page type and concluding pre-sale pages "don't work".
- Leaving store navigation on a pre-sale page, giving readers exits before the offer.
