# Auditing Pages - First Visit, Mobile and Pre-Publish

## What it is
Three complementary audits. The **first-visit audit** confirms the site really communicates what the brand thinks it does. The **mobile break-it audit** hunts for things that are broken, odd or confusing. The **pre-publish checklist** catches the strategic mistakes that cost landing pages the most. Weak conversion frequently comes down to something absent or not working; fixing it is unglamorous and very profitable. (Link, device and checkout QA before launch is covered in [[Landing Page QA Before Launch]].)

## When to use it
- Taking over a store or starting with a new brand (once you know the message it intends to send).
- Before publishing any new landing page.
- Periodically, even on a site you think is optimised; a fresh pass always finds defects.

## How (steps)
**First-visit audit**
1. Use a phone-sized viewport and add an audit section to your research document.
2. Browse as a shopper who knows nothing: homepage, benefit pages, collections, product, cart, and a full checkout with a realistic address so you see the real shipping cost and delivery estimate.
3. File each observation under one of three headings: **questions** a buyer would have that the site never answers; **action items**; **opportunities**, facts that are true but never mentioned (shelf-stable, giftable, an associated expert, packaging).
4. Challenge every claim: what proves it? Unproven claims do not build belief. A benefit you overlooked while browsing is one shoppers overlook as well.
5. Check that best sellers lead the homepage, that attributes shown on the product also appear in headline badges, and that benefit claims are grouped (benefits, diets, problems) rather than a jumbled list. Drop unbelievable or low-fit claims.
6. Cut the ordering explanation down to roughly three steps, show delivery time before checkout, label navigation by where it leads, and give subscription-only pages a one-off purchase option too.

**Mobile break-it audit**
1. Open the store on a real phone in a private window so you get pop-ups like a new visitor.
2. Tap everything interactive (links, arrows, panels, videos, accordions), landing pages included. Write down everything wrong.
3. Hunt for: broken elements and 404s; inconsistent text colours or sizes; text too small for your customers; outdated years, awards or payment badges; incomplete policy information (dispatch time but no delivery time); accidental swipes; add-to-cart going somewhere odd; confusing prices; chat widgets covering content; low-resolution logos; awkward page addresses.
4. Repeat on a smaller phone to catch a checkout button pushed below the fold.

**Pre-publish checklist (the costly mistakes)**
- Structure: cold traffic gets a pre-sale page; the product page only handles warm traffic.
- Content: specific items a competitor could not claim (swapping generic items for product-specific ones reportedly lifted conversions about 40%); smooth transitions into the product; roughly 70% education to 30% selling; customer language.
- Proof: every major claim backed by something, sitting beside it, using a mix of types.
- Visuals: one per item or section; before/after where relevant. Text-only items lose readers; adding visuals is reported to raise engagement about 38%.
- Mobile: text 16px or larger, buttons at least 44x44px, no sideways scrolling, tested on a real phone.
- Sub-headlines: read only them; do they tell the story alone?
- CTA: at least two, journey-oriented, unmissable, saying what happens next. Deep scrolling but few button clicks points to a weak closing call to action.
- Personalisation: one avatar and their language per page.

## Example
Hypothetical: auditing a meal-kit store, the first-visit pass finds the site never says meals are never frozen, a key reason customers choose it, and shows delivery time only at the last checkout step. The mobile pass finds a gallery that swipes when you try to scroll and a 2019 award badge. The pre-publish pass on the new landing page finds eight generic benefit items and one CTA at the very bottom. All three lists go into one prioritised backlog.

## Common mistakes
- Auditing on desktop when most traffic is mobile.
- Auditing before you know what message the brand intends.
- Doing it once and never repeating it.
