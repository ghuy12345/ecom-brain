# Choosing Which Ads Deserve a Landing Page

## What it is
A short research routine that decides which ads get their own page. The rule behind it: build the page to serve an ad that already performs, never the other way round. Each click happened because one particular ad made one particular case, and the page should keep making that case. Starting from a working ad also removes one unknown, because you already know the ad can sell.

## When to use it
- Before you build any new landing page.
- When you have several ads running and need to decide where page effort pays most.
- When an ad clicks well but converts poorly and you suspect the destination.

With no working ad yet, do not build pages: keep producing ads and sending them to a simple product page until one lands. Building a page with no proven angle behind it is the weakest option.

## How (steps)
1. **Set up a repeatable view.** At the ad level in Ads Manager, hide ads with no delivery, rank by spend, and save the columns as a preset.
2. **Top spenders, last 30 days.** Pick the three biggest spenders that also have the lowest CPA or best ROAS. Copy each preview link into your research document.
3. **Proven ads now switched off, last six months.** Same sort. You are looking for ads that once carried the account and might be revived with a better page.
4. **High CTR, weak conversion, last 30 days.** Sort by CTR. Ads with strong click-through but conversion well below the account average are the clearest page opportunity: the ad earns interest, the page loses it.
5. **Best hook rates.** Create a hook-rate column (3-second plays over impressions, as a percentage). Note the videos with the highest rates; roughly half is excellent for a video that opens on a problem.
6. **Log everything.** Paste preview links into a creative audit section split into four lists: recent top spenders, older top spenders, strongest hook rates and strongest CTRs.
7. **Identify the angle of each candidate.** Several ads that share one angle can share one page.
8. **Research that angle, build the offer around it, then build the page.** The page is the last step, not the first.
9. **With dynamic creative,** note the post ID or split the winning asset into its own ad; otherwise you cannot tell which creative actually won.

## Example
Hypothetical: a cookware brand's audit shows one video ("non-stick without coatings") as the top spender at target CPA, an older "us vs. cheap pans" static that carried the account last spring, and a recipe-style video with a 3.5% CTR but conversion at half the account average. The team builds one page around the coating-free angle first because it already spends; the recipe video's ads move to that page too, since they share the angle. The old comparison static gets a cheap duplicate page to test whether a matching destination revives it.

## Common mistakes
- Picking ads by CTR alone. A 5% CTR on a tiny spend means nothing; spend gates every metric.
- Including retargeting ads. You want prospecting ads that can scale.
- Including catalog or dynamic product ads. They carry naturally high CTR and leave little for a page to do.
- Taking hook rates from engagement or awareness campaigns, where they run artificially high. Treat an 80% hook rate with suspicion.
- Comparing against an outside benchmark instead of the account's own baseline.
- Forgetting that dynamic creative masks the winning asset.
