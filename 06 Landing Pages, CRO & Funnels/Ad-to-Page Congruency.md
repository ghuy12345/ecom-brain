# Ad-to-Page Congruency

## What it is
Congruency (also called message match) means the landing page continues the ad's thread without a break. The ad sets an expectation: a promise, a problem, a phrase, a face, a product name, sometimes a before/after. The first screen of the page has to confirm that the visitor is in the right place and that the promise is about to be kept.

When it breaks, people do not consciously think "this is incongruent"; they just feel slightly lost and leave. That is why incongruent funnels can show healthy click-through with poor conversion: the ad earned the click and the page threw it away.

## When to use it
- Every time you build or edit a landing page for an ad.
- When an ad has strong CTR but weak conversion (diagnosis of curiosity-driven clicks is covered in [[Diagnosing Clicks That Do Not Convert]]).
- When the same page is serving several different ads.

## How (steps)
1. **Read ad hook and page headline aloud, one after the other.** If the pair sounds like two unrelated pages, rewrite the headline to pick up the same thread.
2. **Match the promise.** If the ad promises an outcome, the headline repeats that outcome. If the ad shows a transformation, the page opens with a story or headline that mirrors it.
3. **Match the language.** Reuse the ad's key phrases and the customer's words, not a new marketing vocabulary.
4. **Match names exactly.** The brand name, product name, ingredient or mechanism name on the page should be the one in the ad. A different name breaks recognition.
5. **Match visuals.** The product, packaging, colours and people should look like the ad. If a creator appears in the ad, consider their review or quote near the top of the page.
6. **Match the hook type to the page headline.** A curiosity hook pairs with a "what nobody mentions about..." list headline; a problem hook with "why X keeps coming back, and the fix"; a discovery hook with a "new findings about..." headline; a before/after hook with a "what changed after I solved..." story headline.
7. **Match the offer.** If the ad mentions a deal, the page shows that exact deal above the fold and carries it to checkout.
8. **Match the awareness level.** A problem-led ad needs a problem-led page, not an offer page that assumes the visitor already wants the product.
9. **Write the copy first, then adapt it.** Research, decide the awareness stage and format, write the page copy from problem to solution, and adapt video scripts from that copy. Ads and page then share one source of language.
10. **Give each angle its own page** once it spends meaningfully (see [[Landing Pages as a Scaling Lever]]).

## Example
Hypothetical: a static ad for a magnesium spray reads "Calf cramps at 2 a.m.? Try the 10-second spray" and shows a blue bottle. It links to a generic page headed "Premium Mineral Wellness" with a white bottle and the product called by its old name. CTR is high, conversion poor. The fix: headline "Stop 2 a.m. calf cramps in about 10 seconds", the blue bottle in the hero, the ad's exact product name, and a review from someone who mentions night cramps. Nothing about the offer changed, but the page now feels like the next sentence of the ad.

## Common mistakes
- Sending many different angles to one generic page.
- Clickbait hooks that the page cannot or does not pay off.
- Renaming the product or mechanism between ad and page.
- Generic ad images that would fit any product, leaving the page nothing visual to echo.
- Mentioning an offer in the ad that is missing or different on the page.
- Treating congruency as only the headline; it covers names, visuals, offer and tone.
