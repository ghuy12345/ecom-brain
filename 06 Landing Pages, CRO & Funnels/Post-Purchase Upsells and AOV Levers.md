# Post-Purchase Upsells and AOV Levers

## What it is
Average order value (AOV) is half of revenue per session, and it is often easier to move than conversion rate. The main levers, roughly in the order a shopper meets them:

1. **Tiered bundles** on the product or offer page ("buy more, save more"), with the larger tier preselected or highlighted.
2. **Gifts with purchase,** where the gift's perceived value far exceeds its cost.
3. **Free-shipping thresholds** set to pull orders upward.
4. **Cart upgrades:** a slide-out cart with a progress bar toward free shipping or a gift, and a single tap to switch to a bundle or routine.
5. **Post-purchase upsells:** offers shown after payment, accepted with one click, no re-entry of card details.

## When to use it
- When conversion rate is healthy but revenue per session is not.
- When shipping costs are flat across quantities, so multi-unit orders raise margin.
- When a page test has won on conversion but cut AOV, and you want AOV back.

## How (steps)
1. **Build tiers that make sense.** Three is typical (for example 1, 2 and 3 units, or build-your-own 3, 6 and 12 packs), with the middle or largest highlighted. Check each tier's margin in your offer spreadsheet; keep margins healthy as discounts deepen.
2. **Test multi-buy formats** such as buy-two-get-one alongside straight percentage bundles.
3. **Use gifts instead of deeper discounts.** A low-cost, highly relevant gift with a high perceived value often beats a bigger price cut, and you can raise the price slightly to offset it. A single inexpensive complementary item inside an offer can carry a lot of spend.
4. **Set the free-shipping threshold above current AOV,** close enough to reach. Avoid setting it too low. Collected shipping is almost pure margin, and modest shipping charges often have little effect on conversion; test it rather than assume.
5. **Add a cart upgrade.** Show progress to the threshold or gift and a one-click switch to the bundle.
6. **Build a post-purchase stack, not a single offer.** Several offers (around five to seven) can follow checkout. Take rates fall sharply after the first offer, so make each later one cheaper and simpler to say yes to. Give every offer its own full page rather than a small pop-up.
7. **Check processor compatibility.** Certain post-purchase apps only work with the store's built-in payment processor, so customers who pay another way skip the whole sequence.
8. **Weigh subscriptions carefully.** Establish the core product's value first and present subscription as an option or an after-checkout offer rather than the headline, unless you have repeat-purchase data and LTV that justify a front-end subscription.
9. **Measure profit per session, not just AOV,** so a bigger basket does not hide a thinner margin.

## Example
Hypothetical: a coffee brand at a $38 AOV adds three tiers (one, two and four bags) with the two-bag tier marked "most popular", a free scoop worth $12 that costs $1 to make at the two-bag tier, and free shipping from $55. After checkout, buyers see a one-click offer for a grinder accessory at a discount, then a cheaper second offer for a sample pack. AOV moves toward $55 while margin per order holds, because the gift is cheap and shipping costs barely rise with a second bag.

## Common mistakes
- A single upsell when a short stack would earn more.
- Upsell tools that silently do not fire for part of your orders because of the payment processor.
- Free-shipping thresholds set below current AOV, which gives away margin for nothing.
- Deep percentage discounts on every tier, eroding margin.
- Leading with a subscription before customers value the product.
- Judging AOV changes without checking profit per session.
