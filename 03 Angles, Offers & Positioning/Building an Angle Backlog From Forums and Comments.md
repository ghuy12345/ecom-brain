# Building an Angle Backlog From Forums and Comments

## What it is
A repeatable research habit that keeps you from running out of angles. Research works like filling a sponge: once your head is soaked in real customer detail, angles appear while you write. Skip the research, or hand all of it to AI, and angle generation feels impossible.

There are three sources:
1. **Your own data:** patterns in what won and lost across your ads, store and sales material.
2. **Competitor intel:** the angles, benefits and objections in competitors' best-running ads. Useful, but on its own it only gets you level with them, because copying results teaches you nothing about the cause.
3. **Customer language:** reviews, forums and comments. Most winning ads trace back here, so give it roughly four-fifths of your research time.

## When to use it
- Weekly, when ideas go stale, and before briefing anyone on a new persona.

## How (steps)
**1. Review your own data weekly.** Note which benefits, pains and objections keep showing up in ads that won (lean in) and ads that lost (pull back).

**2. Build a customer-language doc per product and persona.**
- Paste the harshest and the happiest reviews of rival and similar products from big marketplaces.
- Search forums with *problem + demographic*, *category + demographic* and *objection + demographic* rather than generic product terms.
- Paste in the most detailed threads and replies you find.
- You can ask an LLM to pull out pain points, desired outcomes, objections and exact wording, but go through a handful of original threads with your own eyes too; the small details that turn an average ad into a breakout are the first thing a summary drops.

**3. Define the target before searching a forum.** Write down the funnel stage the batch serves, the awareness stage, a richly described persona rather than an age-and-gender label, and the sophistication you think applies.
- For problem-aware people, search the problem in their words, adding persona traits if useful ("breakage after bleaching", "breakage after pregnancy").
- For solution-aware people, search the product category with a qualifier ("[product type] for acne").
- Prefer threads with many comments and recent dates; old threads can reflect outdated products and beliefs.
- Resist starting with fully unaware audiences; they convert hardest. Problem-aware buyers are usually easier wins.

**4. Mine one thread line by line.** For every distinct detail, force two or three angle variants from different types in [[Ad Angle Types by Appeal Category]]: story, direct claim, confession, effort economy, expert, contrarian, self-diagnosis, fear, suppressed knowledge, paradox, myth-bust, social proof, identity loss. One detailed post can produce dozens of raw angles. Keep the prospect's phrasing close to verbatim; it stays literal and believable.

**5. Mine comments on your top-spending ads.** In Ads Manager, widen the date range as far as it goes, open the ad with the most spend or results, view it as a post with every comment visible, and sort what you find:
- rival brands people bring up (your real competition);
- questions (raw material for objections and FAQs);
- complaints about the landing page (CRO signals);
- unexpected ways people use the product (often the best place to find new angles);
- phrasing that already works as a headline;
- how replies are handling objections;
- requests for new colours, sizes or products, for whoever runs product.
Negative comments are data, not a reason to skip this.

**6. Filter before anything gets produced.** Keep an angle only if it:
1. offers a reason to buy rather than only a concept;
2. is specific to this avatar;
3. is believable and the product delivers it;
4. fits the awareness and sophistication stage;
5. is not already saturated in competitor ads;
6. has any stat or claim backed by a source;
7. stays within ad policy: no medicines named, no made-up figures.

**7. Refine for the brief.** For each survivor, note which level of desire it touches (a practical result, a feeling or who they want to be), which pain or benefit it turns up, and what motivator links it to the product (speed, effort saved, status).

## Example
A hair-care brand finds a thread asking whether hair can be too damaged to repair, with 200 replies. From it come: a self-diagnosis listicle ("three signs your hair is past saving"); a confession angle from someone who used a cheap product and regretted it; a demonstration angle from a commenter describing a ten-second strand test; a contrarian angle from "do I really have to stop bleaching?"; and a comparison angle from replies about imitation ingredients (only usable once verified). Meanwhile, comments on the brand's top ad reveal parents buying it for teenage daughters before prom, which becomes a new seasonal angle.

## Common mistakes
- Taking the first angle each paragraph suggests and moving on.
- Assuming mined angles are finished copy; most are workable, few deserve production.
- Relying only on competitor ads.
- Letting AI summaries replace reading.
- Naming medicines or quoting unverified statistics in ads.
