# The Post-Black-Friday Offer Ladder

## What it is
A sale collapses your whole funnel (every warm prospect who hadn't purchased yet) into a single spike in revenue, so a slump always follows. The mistake is snapping straight back to the evergreen offer the Monday after. An **offer ladder** steps the deal down in stages through December and January, so you keep capturing gift demand, early buyers still feel they got the best deal, and you exit the season without training people to expect the peak discount forever.

## When to use it
- Any brand running a Black Friday or Cyber Monday promotion.
- Gift-friendly products with strong December demand.
- Health, wellness and self-improvement brands planning for January.
- When a holiday offer performed so well you are tempted to keep it.

## How (steps)
1. **Cyber week: extend.** Keep the sale offer running for roughly one more week after Cyber Monday. Cut budgets back from peak-day levels the day after the peak instead of holding Friday-level spend.
2. **Holiday period: drop to an "A-minus" offer.** Make it slightly weaker than Black Friday (for example 25% down to 20%, or drop one free item from the bundle). It captures gift demand while protecting the credibility of the peak offer.
3. **Use delivery cutoffs as urgency.** Run a site banner and ad copy with a guaranteed holiday delivery date. Confirm the date with your carrier and add one or two days of padding; do not promise specific delivery dates you cannot control. Order-cutoff countdowns and "get it before Christmas" gifting messages create real, self-expiring urgency. Once standard shipping cutoffs pass, free or faster shipping can replace a discount and protect margin, but only sell rush handling if your warehouse can genuinely honour it.
4. **Out of stock? Pre-order.** A pre-order widget with an honest ship window keeps selling. Waits of a few weeks hurt conversion little; waits measured in months do real damage.
5. **After Christmas: "treat yourself".** A short day-after-Christmas promotion aimed at people spending gift money or buying what they did not receive.
6. **January: fresh start.** Health, fitness and wellness brands frequently do more business in January than in November, thanks to new-year resolutions. Reposition around new-year themes and the seasonal desire for a reset (see [[Seasonal Shifts in Dominant Desires]]).
7. **Use the cheap auction.** From mid-December onward, ad auctions are often the cheapest of the quarter, which makes a post-peak offer good value.
8. **Carry a proven holiday offer into evergreen, carefully.** If a holiday offer worked and the margin supports it, extend it by editing the existing page while keeping the same URL, so the ads keep their learning. Run any new holiday promotion with gifting angles in a separate campaign. A dip after the holidays is normal (buyers are tired of promotions); do not read it as the offer failing.
9. **Pre-build backup offers.** Before the season, prepare a ladder of fallback offers (a free gift, free delivery, a bundle, a deeper store-wide sale) with creative and pages already built, so if you fall short you switch one on instead of starting from scratch.

## Example
A hypothetical candle brand runs 30% off sitewide for Black Friday. Its ladder:
- Cyber week: 30% continues for seven days; daily budget drops back to about 60% of the peak day.
- 6-17 December: 20% off plus a delivery-cutoff banner ("order by the 17th for Christmas delivery"), with gifting ads.
- 18-24 December: no discount; free express shipping and a digital gift card for last-minute buyers.
- 26-28 December: a "treat yourself" offer of 15% off for those with gift money.
- January: a calm-home, fresh-start angle with a two-candle bundle; the bundle performs well enough that the brand keeps it on the same evergreen page into February.

## Common mistakes
- Snapping back to evergreen immediately and losing December gift demand.
- Running a holiday offer stronger than Black Friday, which annoys early buyers.
- Guaranteeing delivery dates without carrier confirmation.
- Changing the landing page URL when extending an offer, which resets ad learning.
- Reading the post-holiday dip as proof the offer stopped working.
- Keeping peak-level discounts into the new year and training customers to wait.
