# Ad Angle Types by Appeal Category

## What it is
A reference palette of the recurring ways ads give people a reason to buy: 28 types arranged as a 7x4 grid (seven appeal categories, four types each). Tag each line of research with the types it could become. Treat the types like paint: the strongest angles usually blend two or three.

## When to use it
- While mining research, to see more than one angle in each detail.
- When briefing, to check the type suits the persona.
- When sorting saved ads by type.

## How (steps)
1. **Place the persona.** Decide their awareness stage and the sophistication of the market they live in (see [[Plotting Personas on Awareness and Sophistication]]).
2. **Shortlist types that fit those stages** using the palette below.
3. **Blend two or three** into one angle (say, a hidden enemy told as a confession).
4. **Match the format to the type.** Longer scripted video or creator content suits transformation and root-cause stories; statics and listicles suit self-checks and teaching; unpolished selfie-style video suits confessions and customer proof.
5. **File real examples by type** for future briefs.

### The palette
The grid below shows all 28 types at a glance; the lists after it explain each one.

| Desire-led | Fear and pain | Curiosity | Proof and credibility | Authority | Story | Offer and structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct claim | Fear/agitation | Open loop | New discovery | Expert/educational | Confession | Problem/solution |
| Effort economy | Hidden enemy / root cause | Suppressed knowledge | Logical reasons | Insider access | Unlikely hero | Direct deal |
| Transformation | Mistake to avoid | Contrarian | Demonstration | Borrowed authority | Topical | Variety/optionality |
| Identity upgrade | Self-diagnosis | Paradox | Social proof | Bold proclamation | Controversial/taboo | Scarcity/urgency |

**Desire-led**
- *Direct claim:* say the benefit plainly. Strongest in young markets where optimism is high.
- *Effort economy:* promise the result without the usual price in hours, discomfort or willpower. Fits almost any product.
- *Transformation:* a before-and-after arc that needs a genuine low point and a dramatically better ending.
- *Identity upgrade:* sell the person the buyer gets to become (or avoids becoming) rather than a result. Good for people who have tried everything.

**Fear and pain**
- *Fear/agitation:* raises a buried fear through loss aversion. It must be specific and relatable; vague fear reads as noise.
- *Hidden enemy / root cause:* names one secret cause as a villain. Pairs with a unique mechanism; suits sceptical markets but needs time to explain.
- *Mistake to avoid:* "you've been doing this wrong". Suggests damage has already started.
- *Self-diagnosis:* the viewer recognises the problem in their own life through a checklist or quick test. A soft sell for unaware and problem-aware audiences.

**Curiosity**
- *Open loop:* the gap must promise something the viewer personally gains.
- *Suppressed knowledge:* an insider truth someone bigger kept from you.
- *Contrarian:* the common advice is wrong, and here is why.
- *Paradox:* flip what the viewer expects. Useful for jaded buyers.

**Proof and credibility**
- *New discovery:* frame the finding as new and as an expert's. Fits sceptics.
- *Logical reasons:* facts and data that let the buyer justify a choice they already want to make. Analytical niches, lower funnel.
- *Demonstration:* show it working on camera so doubt has nowhere to go; the strongest reply to heavy scepticism.
- *Social proof:* real user stories or sheer volume ("everyone's already switched"). Strong mid-funnel.

**Authority**
- *Expert/educational:* the brand or an ambassador teaches, but every line still builds the sale.
- *Insider access:* the viewer feels invited into an exclusive group.
- *Borrowed authority:* credibility lent by a recognisable person or a creator the audience already trusts.
- *Bold proclamation:* a sweeping, confident statement that cuts through conflicting advice; needs credibility behind it.

**Story**
- *Confession:* a raw first-person account.
- *Unlikely hero:* "I was just like you". Dissolves self-blaming objections.
- *Topical:* links the product to news, holidays or the season; a route to people not yet looking.
- *Controversial/taboo:* a deliberately uncomfortable pattern interrupt that heightens fear, curiosity or anger; the sharpest version of a contrarian take.

**Offer and structure**
- *Problem/solution:* the baseline skeleton of most ads; on its own it only works for optimistic, early-stage markets.
- *Direct deal:* states the literal offer plainly; bottom of the funnel.
- *Variety/optionality:* shows flexible uses, colours, recipes or functions; mid funnel.
- *Scarcity/urgency:* the deadline or limit has to be genuine, or buyers will smell it.

## Example
A hair-repair brand targets problem-aware women with bleach damage. Direct claims are worn out, so the team blends self-diagnosis ("three signs your hair is past conditioner") with a strand stretch test on camera, as a static listicle plus a short talking-head video.

## Common mistakes
- Copying one type in isolation instead of blending.
- Picking a type without checking awareness and sophistication first.
- Vague fear instead of specific, believable fear.
- Fake urgency or unsupported "secret" claims.
- Confusing the type (a shape) with the angle (the specific reason to buy for this avatar).
