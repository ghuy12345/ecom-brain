# Product Integrity and Claims That Outgrow Reality

## What it is
A simple rule with big consequences: **the product you advertise must be the product you ship, and the claims you make must be ones it can keep.** Claims that outgrow reality often do scale for a while. Shoppers figure the price is low enough to gamble on. Then reviews, comments, refunds, chargebacks and creator backlash catch up, and the brand collapses, sometimes with its ad accounts and store along with it. This happens at every size of brand.

Integrity is also a positioning advantage. A product that genuinely delivers earns word of mouth, which is the cheapest and most durable form of authority (see [[Authority as Proof in the Claim Stack]]).

## When to use it
- Before launching any new angle, mechanism or "new information" claim.
- When you are tempted to show a premium version in ads and ship a cheaper one to protect margin.
- When an ad is scaling unusually fast on a bold promise.

## How (steps)
1. **Match the ad to the box.** If what you ship differs from what the ad shows, stop the ad immediately. Then test two honest paths side by side: price the premium version high enough to be profitable, and give the cheaper version its own ads built on what it genuinely does well.
2. **Run every claim through two questions.** Can the product actually deliver this for most buyers? Would the customer feel misled after using it?
3. **Lean on what is true instead of inflating.** Your real mechanism, the specific avatars you can genuinely help, identity, and relatability all scale without lying. You can even test honest contrast lines that admit what the product won't do and then state what it will.
4. **Verify statistics and studies** before they appear anywhere. Educated buyers will check, and false claims spread faster in comments than true ones.
5. **Check whether the economics need the lie.** If you can only hit your target by overstating the product, the issue is the offer, price or product itself. A breakeven ROAS that is unreachable after offers and pricing are exhausted means you should ask whether the product can survive long term, not stretch the claims.
6. **Watch post-purchase signals.** Rising refund requests, negative comments about "not what was shown", or a spike in chargebacks after a scaling push are early warnings that a claim has outrun the product.

## Example
A hypothetical cookware brand ran ads showing a heavy, multi-ply pan but shipped a lighter single-layer model to protect margin. The ad scaled for six weeks; then reviews and a creator's comparison video went viral and refund requests rose sharply. Applying the rules: it pulled the ads, relaunched the heavy pan at a higher price that still met its margin, and built separate ads for the light pan around a true angle: "the pan for people who hate heavy cookware". Both now sell without complaints.

## Common mistakes
- Showing a premium product and shipping a cheaper one.
- Treating early scale on an inflated claim as proof it works.
- Copying a competitor's bold claim because "they're getting away with it".
- Using unverified statistics or invented study results.
- Assuming policy changes only affect other advertisers.
