# Authority as Proof in the Claim Stack

## What it is
A claim on its own asks the prospect to trust you. A believable claim rests on three layers, the **claim stack**:
1. **Angle:** the claim, the reason to buy.
2. **Mechanism:** why it is plausible (see [[Unique Mechanism - Problem Cause and Solution]]).
3. **Authority:** why the prospect should believe it, coming from someone or something other than the brand's own assertion.

A mechanism alone often does not build enough belief. Authority does most of the heavy lifting when beliefs need to move, and it matters most in three situations: highly sophisticated markets where every claim has been heard; products with no obvious unique mechanism; and products whose results take weeks or cannot be seen (supplements, skincare, longevity). It does not need to be in every ad, but it needs to appear somewhere in the funnel.

## When to use it
- When an ad gets clicks from the right people but they do not buy: that is usually a belief problem, not a hook problem.
- When your mechanism is not visible or is hard to explain.
- When moving into a sceptical avatar that already "knows" the category.
- When writing landing pages for higher-priced or health-adjacent products.

## How (steps)
1. **Write the stack for every key claim.** Three lines: claim, mechanism, authority. If the authority line is empty, the claim is resting on the brand's word alone.
2. **Choose a source of authority.** Options, roughly from most to least effort:
   - *A credible expert:* a doctor, engineer, chef or other specialist explaining why the mechanism works.
   - *The mechanism itself:* when it is clear and visible, the demonstration is the authority.
   - *Demonstrated knowledge:* content that shows deep understanding of the category, for example explaining exactly what is wrong with the usual solutions.
   - *Comparison:* one creator showing every alternative they tried and why each failed.
   - *Volume:* enough customers, micro-creators and repeat buyers that the sheer number is the proof. When enough people talk about a brand, it turns into an authority through sheer weight: it shows up first when people search the category, creators keep discussing it, friends recommend it. Real word of mouth keeps working year-round and props up many claims at once.
   - *A known face:* occasionally the proof is the creative asset itself, for example a recognisable person holding the product.
3. **Place authority before the pitch.** Earn credibility first, then make the "here's why ours wins" argument, never the other way round.
4. **Verify every authority statistic** before it runs. An unchecked number does more damage than none.
5. **Match the authority to the avatar.** A clinician persuades a health-anxious buyer; a busy parent of five persuades other parents more than a lab coat would.

## Example
Hypothetical stacks:
- *Kids' greens gummies.* Claim: your child will actually enjoy their greens. Mechanism: it is a gummy. Authority: a parent of several children showing all of them asking for it.
- *Premium chicken.* Claim: more flavour. Mechanism: a richer fat profile. Authority: used by well-regarded restaurants.
- *Canned water.* Claim: no microplastics in your drink. Mechanism: a recyclable aluminium can. Authority: the reported figure that a single litre of bottled water can hold more than 240,000 plastic particles.
- *Weighted hoodie.* Claim: feels calming. Mechanism: weighted fabric. Authority: a clinician explaining the same principle behind weighted blankets, or a large volume of creators describing how it feels.

A comfort-apparel brand is a useful public pattern: it built authority less through one celebrity and more through a very large number of micro-creators wearing and recommending it, so the volume itself became the proof.

## Common mistakes
- Pitching first and building credibility afterwards.
- Treating a mechanism as enough proof in a market that has heard many mechanisms.
- Using an authority statistic you have not checked.
- Choosing an authority the avatar does not respect.
- Fixing the hook or the hold rate when the real problem is that the right people do not believe the claim. Write down each belief the avatar holds and work through them one at a time.
