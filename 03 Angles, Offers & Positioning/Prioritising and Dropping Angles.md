# Prioritising and Dropping Angles

## What it is
Research produces more angles than you can ever produce. This note covers three decisions: which angles to test first, how to divide effort between iterating winners and testing new angles, and when to give up on an angle. None of them have a universal number attached; they are judgement calls made with a simple scoring habit and a clear read of where your account is.

## When to use it
- After a research sprint, when your angle backlog is long.
- When planning the week's creative batch.
- When an angle has had a few tests and nobody is sure whether to keep going.

## How (steps)
**1. Score the backlog.** Give each angle a 1-10 score on three axes and multiply or average them:
- *Impact:* if it wins, how much could it scale? Broad desire, big avatar, strong emotion.
- *Confidence:* how much evidence supports it? Repeated in reviews and forums, already working for a competitor, a proven sibling angle.
- *Ease:* how cheaply can you produce it? Existing footage, a static you can build today, versus a shoot with a specialist creator.
Add two tie-breakers: is it an uncontested angle that rivals are overlooking, and is it easy to keep producing content for if it wins?

**2. Split effort between iterating and new angles, every week.** These are not two modes to choose between; you run both at once.
- *Iterations* on whatever is winning: new hooks, executions and formats of the proven angle. This is usually the larger share, say roughly 80% of creative effort as a rough starting point.
- *New-angle swings* from the top of your scored backlog: fewer, more distinct bets, each executed well. Keep this share running (say roughly 20%) even while a winner is fresh, because new angles are how accounts find the next unlock, including ones already spending heavily.
You can lean the split as results change (more toward new angles when a winner flattens, more toward iterations when one takes off), but never let either track stop. Before you have any winner, the iteration share goes to refining the most promising early signal. In both tracks, polish the execution rather than hopping between angles: a strong angle badly produced is indistinguishable from a dead one.

**3. Test the strongest version first.** Within an angle, launch the hook with the heaviest emotional charge. A win earns iterations; a loss sends you to the next angle in the queue.

**4. Drop on judgement, not on a spend number.** No set dollar amount or number of concepts exists after which an angle is dead. Before dropping one, ask:
- Was it tested with at least one strong execution, or only weak ones?
- Did any version show leading signals (hook rate, click-through, add-to-carts) even without purchases?
- Is it scalable and easy to make more content for?
- Is it a blue-ocean angle worth more patience because no one else owns it?
If the answers are no, drop it and log why.

**5. Niche down before abandoning a big market.** If a broad angle fails inside a large market, try the same desire for a more specific avatar before giving up on the market.

## Example
A supplement brand has fifteen angles after research. Scored on impact, confidence and ease, "afternoon energy crash for shift workers" ranks first (strong forum evidence, existing footage), while "longevity for over-60s" scores high on impact but low on ease. The team runs three distinct swings rather than ten variations of one idea. The shift-worker angle wins; for the next month most production goes into iterating it, while a steady fifth of the batch keeps testing the next angles on the list, starting with longevity on a proper shoot. When the shift-worker results flatten, the longevity angle has already been tested once, so the team knows where to lean. An earlier angle that failed twice with weak UGC is re-tested once with a stronger execution before it is retired.

## Common mistakes
- Testing angles in the order they were thought of rather than by score.
- Making ten near-identical variations and calling it testing.
- Killing an angle after one poor execution.
- Keeping a losing angle alive because a lot of work went into the research.
- Treating iteration and new angles as either/or, so one track stops: all new swings while a winner still has headroom, or all iterations until the winner dies with nothing ready to replace it.
