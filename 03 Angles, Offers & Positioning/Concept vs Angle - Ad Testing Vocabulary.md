# Concept vs Angle - Ad Testing Vocabulary

## What it is
A small, fixed vocabulary for planning and reviewing ad tests. Without it, a team says "let's test UGC" or "let's try an us-vs-them ad" and believes it has an angle, when it only has a container. The terms below separate what you are testing (internal) from why the customer should buy (external).

- **Test structure:** the repeatable unit you launch. A common one is a few creatives, each with a couple of primary-text and headline options, inside one ad set. The whole unit counts as one concept.
- **Test number (batch number):** a running ID for every test ("test 27"). It lets you see how many tests each winner cost you and find old learnings fast.
- **Concept (the swing):** your test strategy for this batch. It faces inward: iterate on last month's winner, try superlative claims, try a new format, try a different avatar, try a different awareness level.
- **Angle:** the selling strategy: the single biggest reason you hand someone to purchase. It faces outward: "buy it once and never replace it", "stops the tossing and turning", "no more microplastics in your water".
- **Execution:** the creative style that carries the angle (educational, entertaining, a "three reasons" list, a demo).
- **Iteration:** a small, fast change to an ad that already exists, such as new hook text or a new first frame. A fresh concept usually needs new research or new footage.
- **Variation:** one individual version inside the test. Either different wordings of a single angle, or different angles sitting inside one concept (steel construction / designed by a doctor / simplest to clean).
- **Format:** the delivery vehicle: video, image, slideshow, carousel, GIF, plus narrower versions like creator-style UGC or AI-narrated video. The full format list is in [[Ad Format Taxonomy - Format vs Style]].

## When to use it
- Before you write any brief, so everyone records tests the same way.
- When reviewing results: separating angle from execution tells you whether the idea failed or only the delivery did.
- When a template "that works for everyone" fails for you. A format never closes a sale by itself; the words inside the format do the selling.

## How (steps)
1. **Name the concept.** Write one line on what this batch is testing and give it the next test number.
2. **Name the angle, every time.** Even when the concept is a format or an iteration, each ad inside it still has to state why someone should buy. When the concept and the angle are the same thing ("test the durability angle"), write it in both fields anyway; it keeps you asking what the customer gets.
3. **Run the "of what?" check.** Say the concept out loud and ask "of what?". "Comparison ad" - comparing what? "Transformation" - of what? "Three reasons why" - reasons for what? If no customer-facing answer comes in customer terms, you do not have an angle yet.
4. **Choose execution and format last.** Pick them to suit the angle, not the other way round.
5. **Log it.** Record test number, concept, angle, execution, format and result in your ad log (see [[Creative Roadmap and Ad Log]]).
6. **Read results by layer.** A strong angle in a weak execution deserves a new execution. A weak angle in five formats is still a weak angle.

## Example
A pet-accessories brand plans test 14 as an us-vs-them comparison. Run the check: us-vs-them on what? The team settles on "easier to put on than a buckle harness" as the angle. Three variations: a split-screen demo, a static with two columns, and a creator video. Test 15 keeps the identical us-vs-them concept but swaps the angle to durability. Because both were logged separately, the team can later see that the concept worked with one angle and not the other, instead of concluding "comparison ads don't work for us".

Another case: a sticky-note static is simply a native-looking format. What is handwritten on it ("My dog finally stopped pulling") are the angle. Copying the sticky note without a strong line on it copies nothing that matters.

## Common mistakes
- Treating formats or test types ("UGC", "before/after", "problem-aware ad") as angles.
- Throwing together vague benefit words (durable, easy, cool) without thinking about who needs them or why.
- Blaming the format when the angle inside it was weak.
- Changing angle, format and hook in the same test, then not knowing which caused the result.
- Inventing new terms per person. Use whichever definitions make sense to you, but keep them shared so time goes into execution.
