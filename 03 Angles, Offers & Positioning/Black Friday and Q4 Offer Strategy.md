# Black Friday and Q4 Offer Strategy

## What it is
The offer decisions for the Black Friday and Cyber Monday period (BFCM) and the wider fourth quarter. Buyers expect deals, competitors all discount, and ad costs climb, so a normal evergreen offer gets lost. This note covers offer depth, framing, gifting angles and how to judge the customers you acquire. Campaign mechanics are in [[Sale-Period Campaign Structure]] and the margin math is in [[Checking Whether a Discount Is Profitable]].

## When to use it
- Planning from late summer onward for a Q4-heavy brand.
- Any brand that plans to run a promotion in November.
- When you have discounted all year and are unsure what is left to offer.

## How (steps)
1. **Review last year.** Sales and spend by month, breakeven, best sellers, new vs returning split, and which offers and creatives won.
2. **Recompute the economics before choosing.** With the discounted price, recalculate margin, breakeven ROAS and target CPA, then scale against those new KPIs. A gross margin after discount of around 55% is a bare floor; above 60% is healthier. Higher AOV tolerates a lower percentage margin; products under roughly $60 AOV need more.
3. **Set depth against the market.** Around 25% off is typically the floor for being noticed; 30-40% is competitive in many categories. Weigh how deep you go against what a customer costs to acquire and what they are worth over time. There is no reliable public data on whether, for example, 50% beats 60%; test if it matters.
4. **Make it stronger than evergreen.** The sale offer must clearly beat anything you run the rest of the year. Rebranding your normal offer as "Black Friday" leaves sales on the table. If you run buy-one-get-one all year, step up to buy one get two. If your store has been on promotion all year, either dial the everyday deal back well before Q4, lift the base price early (testing it first) so the sale can bring it back down, or add store credit on top.
5. **Choose the mechanic.** Percentage off for lower-ticket items, dollar off for high-ticket (on a $5,000 item, "$500 off" sounds bigger than "10% off"). Buy-X-get-X-free converts better than buy-X-get-percent-off. Gifts and free or faster shipping are add-ons. Bundles tested before Q4 stack well with sale discounts.
6. **Frame with "up to".** "Up to X% off" grabs attention. Bundle tiers that already save 11-18%, plus an extra sale discount, can honestly support "save up to 40%". Remember stacked discounts multiply rather than add: 10% then 20% is 28%, not 30%.
7. **Show the saving at checkout.** Use automatic discount codes rather than manual markdowns so the cart and checkout display the saving. That builds trust and lifts conversion.
8. **Keep ad and site congruent.** If the ad says 25% and the site gives 40%, nobody minds. If the ad says 40% and the site gives 25%, people get angry. Never inflate compare-at prices; anyone who has watched your ads for months will spot it.
9. **Use gifting angles.** Start testing gifting creative in September or October. As the season nears, buyers become more product-aware and simple product-plus-offer ads work. A dedicated gift collection makes giftable products easy to find.
10. **Use the seasonal desire arc** for angles even when the product itself is not seasonal: October leans to identity, status and belonging; November to family and togetherness; January to health and fresh starts (see [[Seasonal Shifts in Dominant Desires]]).
11. **Validate offers on new customers.** Do not judge an offer only by email results; existing customers shop differently.
12. **Judge promo customers by cohort.** Plenty of Q4 orders are gifts, and gift buyers come back less often, so a customer acquired at a thin margin may be worth less than one acquired in an ordinary month. Compare the 3-, 6- and 12-month value of promo cohorts with normal cohorts before deciding how hard to discount next year. Prefer a smaller day at healthy profit over a record day at thin profit.

## Example
A hypothetical skincare brand runs a 15% bundle saving all year. For BFCM it keeps the bundle tiers (saving 12% and 18%) and adds 25% sitewide via an automatic code, so ads can say "up to 38% off" and checkout shows every saving line. Breakeven ROAS rises from 1.5 to about 2.2, so it resets its scaling target. Gifting ads ("the gift she'll actually use") launch in October. In February it compares the November cohort's repeat rate with a normal month's before planning next year's depth.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping last month's ROAS target after cutting price: revenue rises while profit falls.
- Rebranding the evergreen offer as the sale.
- Inflated compare-at prices and ad-site mismatch.
- Judging offers only from email or only on first-order profit.
- Testing offers or changing the site during the peak weekend itself.
- Over-producing creative: a simple product shot carrying a sale banner often outscales polished content.
