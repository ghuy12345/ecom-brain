# The Offer as the Biggest Early Lever

## What it is
The offer is everything the customer gets for their money and how it is packaged: the bundle, the bonus, the guarantee, the perceived value and the risk removed. It is not just the price or a discount code.

Few levers are as overlooked. A stronger offer can push conversion rate and order value up together, and because revenue per session is simply AOV times conversion rate, moving both at once lifts ROAS steeply. Across the board, creative remains the strongest lever, since a great ad can also cut CPMs and attract buyers with more intent, but the offer is usually the easiest next thing to improve.

A weak offer also sabotages creative testing without anyone noticing. Strong ads send visitors to a page that fails to close them, so the ads get labelled losers. Fix the offer and every ad you test has a better shot.

## When to use it
- Smaller accounts, roughly under six figures a month, where most of what people call page testing is really testing the offer.
- When ads get clicks and add-to-carts but purchases lag.
- When CPMs and creative are reasonable but ROAS sits just below target.
- Before investing in elaborate advertorials or page redesigns.

## How (steps)
1. **Work in this order: offer, creative, then page.** The offer gives the most return for the least effort; creative is next; pages eat the most time and pay off least at small scale. Whether a page is an advertorial or a listicle, and how its sections are arranged, counts for much less than the deal on it.
2. **Recompute breakeven first.** Before any new offer goes live, check that the margin still works at your expected conversion rate (see [[Checking Whether a Discount Is Profitable]]).
3. **Think in three dimensions:**
   - *Packaging:* bundles, multi-packs, buy-two-get-one, a free add-on.
   - *Perceived value:* a gift worth more to the buyer than it costs you; an extra item meant for someone else in the household.
   - *Risk removal:* guarantees, easy returns, try-before-you-buy, especially in sceptical categories.
4. **Test the offer cleanly.**
   - Under about $10k a month: change the offer directly on the store. With little already working, there is little to break.
   - At roughly $30-40k a month, with ads pointing at the main store: use a page-building tool so each offer lives on its own URL and the live store stays untouched.
   - In the ad account: put a few of your best ads in a dedicated offer-test ad set pointing at the new page. A radical change (such as try-before-you-buy) belongs in a separate campaign, since it can knock the main campaign's sales off course; a small tweak (buy two get one vs buy two get two) can sit in the main campaign.
   - Statics that state the offer explicitly, sent straight to the offer page, are a fast read.
5. **Read revenue per session, not just conversion rate.** A cheaper offer may convert more while earning less per visitor.
6. **Then go back to creative.** Once the offer converts, your creative tests become more reliable, and winners are easier to spot.

For the full menu of offers and the rules for picking one, see [[Ecommerce Offer Types and How to Choose]].

## Example
A hypothetical kids' supplement brand sells a single bottle at $35 with a 1.5% conversion rate. It tests two offers for two weeks each: buy two get one free, and a bundle that adds a parent version of the supplement to the same order at a reduced price, so one checkout serves two people. The bundle lifts AOV from $35 to $58 while conversion holds steady, so revenue per session rises by roughly two thirds. Only after that does the brand start testing new video concepts, and they now clear its target far more often.

Pages that win in practice often win on the offer alone, and the change can be small: an extra slice of discount, a three-for-two structure, or a free extra item. A solid offer paired with reasonable static ads is often enough to take a small brand to a healthy five-figure month and beyond.

## Common mistakes
- Treating the offer as only a discount percentage.
- Redesigning pages before testing a stronger offer.
- Launching an offer without recomputing breakeven.
- Judging an offer on conversion rate alone instead of revenue or profit per session.
- Blaming creative when the offer is what is not converting.
- Running a big offer change inside the main campaign and disrupting proven sales.
