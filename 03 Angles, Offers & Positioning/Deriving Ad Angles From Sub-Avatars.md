# Deriving Ad Angles From Sub-Avatars

## What it is
The point of avatar research is to produce angles you can test. If you have built sub-avatars properly, the angles are already sitting inside them; this note is the method for pulling them out and turning them into hooks.

Most brands work the weak chain: product, features, benefits, a demographic persona, then an angle. That produces broad, unrelatable lines such as "fix your acne", which speaks to everyone who has acne and therefore to no one in particular.

The stronger chain runs: product, what it does, the benefit, **the reason a person craves that benefit (the desire)**, the core avatar, sub-avatars (defined by behaviours, feelings and experiences rather than age and gender), angle, then hook or headline.

## When to use it
- After you have core avatars and sub-avatars (see [[Building Sub-Avatars From a Core Desire]]).
- When your angles feel generic, or every ad sounds like the product page.
- When you need a fresh batch of angles after a winner plateaus.

## How (steps)
1. **Pick one sub-avatar.** Even a thin profile with only a desire and a behaviour is enough to start.
2. **Name the desire.** What does this person actually want?
3. **Name the behaviour.** What are they doing right now to get it?
4. **Find the gap.** Despite the behaviour, the desire stays unmet. That shortfall is your angle.
5. **Look for more than one angle.** A single sub-avatar often supports several: one that displaces their current solution and one that stacks on top of it. Sub-avatar and angle are not one-to-one, but every angle test is, in practice, a test of the sub-avatar behind it.
6. **Write direct hooks.** Until you are advanced, state the angle plainly rather than hinting at it. Specific beats clever: a precise outcome, a precise person, a precise situation.
7. **Rank and test.** Order angles by how much emotional weight they carry, test the strongest hook of the strongest angle first, and fold the supporting benefits into body copy. A win means you iterate there; a loss means you try the next one down. You do not need to test every angle you generate.
8. **Use AI as a drafting aid, not a source of truth.** Feeding an LLM a sub-avatar, product facts and a few real winning ads can produce ranked angles with hooks for each. Review critically: seed ads bias the output toward their structure, and without real avatar context the model cannot write a specific hook. The workflow is in [[Building Buyer Avatars With an LLM]].

### Scaling a winning sub-avatar
Once one angle wins, put most creative effort (roughly four parts in five) into iterating it, and the rest into net-new angles. You can then:
- **Vary execution** (educate, entertain, "three reasons" structures).
- **Go deeper** (narrow further so the ad feels personal to a smaller group).
- **Go broader** (remove one specific experience from the avatar to widen it).
Once it flattens out, go back to hunting for fresh angles.

## Example
A sleep-aid brand has a sub-avatar: wants deeply restful sleep, already wears nasal strips, still wakes up tired.
- Desire: restorative sleep. Behaviour: nasal strips. Gap: still waking up unrested.
- Angle A (displace): "works where nasal strips stopped working."
- Angle B (stack): "pair it with your strips for the deepest sleep you've had."

A blackout-curtain brand has a sub-avatar who already avoids screens before bed. Angle: "all that bedtime discipline is being undone by the light in your room." Direct hooks: "No screens after nine. Now block the streetlight." / "It's not a curtain, it's the last step of your sleep routine."

Other direct angle-to-hook pairs: "stops swelling" becomes "fourteen hours on your feet, no swelling"; "fills you like a meal" becomes "this is lunch, not a protein shake"; a parent of a moody teenager becomes "teen always grumpy?". Notice the ad seldom states who the avatar is; the specificity does that work.

## Common mistakes
- Beginning with the spec sheet rather than with a human being.
- Calling formats ("us vs them", "before/after", "sticky note") angles. See [[Concept vs Angle - Ad Testing Vocabulary]].
- Broad avatars, which force broad angles and weak ads.
- Getting stuck on whether an angle is "broad" or "narrow". Angles sit on a spectrum, nested angles are normal; keep them actionable.
- Writing indirect, clever hooks before you have proven the direct version.
- Treating demographics as the sub-avatar.
