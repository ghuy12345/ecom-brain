# Competing With Low-Price Marketplace Sellers

## What it is
Very large marketplaces run broad automated campaigns that can appear on almost any keyword in their catalogue. Many of their listings are cheap relisted products. When they push hard, direct-to-consumer brands lose Shopping impression share and see cheaper look-alike listings beside their own.

A related problem: your own ads can send demand to marketplace sellers of the same product. Someone sees your ad, then searches the product and buys it from a marketplace listing that ships faster or cheaper.

You cannot win a budget war with these sellers. You can make your listing the obvious choice for the right buyer, protect your listings, and outlast them. Low-price marketplaces often wear down their own reputation over months through slow delivery and weak product quality, and some shoppers come back.

## When to use it
- Your Shopping impression share falls and a marketplace appears in auction insights.
- Copies of your products or listings appear at a fraction of your price.
- Your paid traffic grows but direct sales do not, while marketplace sellers of your product grow.

## How (steps)
1. **Confirm the threat.** Check auction insights for your top campaigns and search your main terms in a private window. Note which sellers show and at what price.
2. **Police copied listings weekly.** Search your brand and product titles on the marketplaces. Report listings that copy your images or branding; they are usually removed quickly but can reappear. A copied listing at half price with your photos diverts traffic you paid for.
3. **Differentiate on shipping and convenience.** Put fast, cheap shipping rates and delivery times into Merchant Center so they show under your listing. Speed and convenience are where a direct brand can beat a cheap overseas seller, and they are your main defence against your ads feeding marketplace sales.
4. **Differentiate in the title.** Marketplace titles are stuffed with keywords. Put your premium or distinguishing descriptor at the front of yours so it reads differently at a glance.
5. **Differentiate in the main image.** This is the biggest lever. Study the top-ranking images for your key queries, which earn the highest expected CTR, and design against them. If rivals show huge quantities for the same price, show the full contents and quality of your set.
6. **Compete on value, not just price.** Decide whether to match, undercut or stay premium ([[Pricing Strategy - Premium, Match or Undercut]]), and make the reason for your price visible in the listing.
7. **Hold your Shopping budget.** Do not cut spend in a panic. Track impression share and new-customer revenue through the period, and expect some lost buyers to return.

## Example
Say a lash brand sells a 5-pair pack at the same price as a marketplace listing showing 50 pairs. Side by side, the cheaper-looking offer wins the click. The brand tests a hero image showing the whole set and its packaging, moves a premium descriptor to the start of the title, and adds a free two-day shipping rate to the feed. It reports two copied listings in the same week and keeps its Shopping budget steady.

## Common mistakes
- Trying to outbid a marketplace on every query.
- Matching rock-bottom prices and destroying your margin.
- Leaving shipping details out of the feed, so your speed advantage is invisible.
- Ignoring copied listings because "they will get taken down eventually".
- Cutting Shopping budget the week a big marketplace advertiser appears.
