# Search Terms and Negative Keywords

## What it is
The **search terms report** lists the actual queries that triggered your ads, as opposed to the keywords you chose. **Negative keywords** tell a campaign which queries it must not serve on. Together they give you your strongest form of control, and that matters most in Shopping, because you cannot pick keywords there and the feed determines what you match.

You can view the report for Search campaigns and for standard Shopping. Performance Max has historically offered little or no query visibility and few negative options (brand exclusions and newer negative controls now exist), one more reason to avoid launching a fresh account on PMax ([[Performance Max vs Standard Shopping]]).

## When to use it
- In the first weeks of any new account or campaign, to confirm you are showing for relevant queries.
- Weekly afterwards, to catch new wasted spend.
- When a cold campaign looks too good and you suspect brand traffic is inside it.

## How (steps)
**Reading the search terms report**
1. Go into a Search or standard Shopping campaign, choose keywords, then search terms.
2. Customise the columns (cost, conversions, conversion value, ROAS) and sort by cost so the biggest spenders are on top.
3. Tick irrelevant queries (competitor brands you do not want, job or repair searches, wrong product types) and add them as negatives straight from the report.
4. Look for your own brand in cold campaigns; if it is there, see [[Brand Search and Branded Shopping Campaigns]].
5. Note converting queries too: they become title keywords for the feed and candidates for dedicated search campaigns.

**Choosing a negative match type.** Use phrase and exact; avoid broad.
- *Phrase negative* ("fresh"): blocks any query containing that phrase, even inside a longer search, so "fresh fruit" is blocked.
- *Exact negative* [fresh]: blocks only that exact query, so "fresh fruit" still serves.
- *Broad negative*: blocks queries containing all of its words in any order, without expanding to synonyms or close variants, which makes it less predictable than people expect.
Phrase-negative a root word to block every variant (a fruit-box store might phrase-negative "flowers"). Exact-negative a word that is junk on its own but valuable in combination.

**Using shared lists**
1. Add negatives at campaign level, or save them to a new shared list.
2. Attach shared lists (often 100-200 terms) to every relevant campaign instead of re-entering them.
3. Keep one universal list of low-intent terms (jobs, careers, hiring, salary, intern, free, repair, DIY as relevant) on every campaign.
4. Keep a separate brand list to apply as negatives on all cold campaigns.

**Cadence**
- First 30 days of a campaign: review weekly, or daily at high budgets. This is when negatives have the most leverage.
- After that: weekly or fortnightly. Each edit can nudge learning, so batch changes rather than tinkering constantly.

## Example
Say a fruit-box store's Shopping campaign spends noticeably on "flower delivery", "fruit tree" and "fresh fruit jobs". It phrase-negatives "flowers", "tree" and "jobs" via the universal list, leaves "fresh fruit box" running, and exact-negatives "fruit" alone because the bare word converts poorly while longer fruit queries convert well. The next week it sees "[brand] discount" spending in the cold campaign and adds the brand to the cold campaign's negative list.

## Common mistakes
- Never opening the search terms report.
- Broad-match negatives that block less, or differently, than you expect.
- Typing the same negatives into ten campaigns instead of one shared list.
- Over-negating in week one and choking a new campaign of volume.
- Making daily negative edits forever on a campaign that has stabilised.
- Leaving your brand unblocked in cold campaigns.
