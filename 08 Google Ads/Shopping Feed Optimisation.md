# Shopping Feed Optimisation

## What it is
Shopping and PMax ads are built from your product feed in Merchant Center. There are no keywords to pick and little ad copy to write: the feed's titles, images, prices, attributes and shipping details decide which searches you match and whether people click. That makes the feed arguably the most important lever in ecommerce Google Ads, ahead of bidding and structure.

## When to use it
- Before scaling any Shopping or PMax campaign.
- When CTR is weak or Shopping impressions are low on your main queries.
- When Merchant Center shows disapprovals, limitations or warnings.
- When you start selling in more than one country.

## How (steps)
**1. Get to zero problems.** Review disapprovals, limitations and warnings. Fix what you can; remove products that will never be approved. Aim for a disapproval rate under 5%. Fill every relevant attribute. Feed clean-ups alone can produce meaningful performance gains.

**2. Rewrite titles.** Titles are the most under-used part of the feed.
- Lead with the words shoppers actually search, taken from your search terms data.
- Push the brand to the end, or drop it if nobody knows it yet.
- Include attributes like colour, size, material or format within the length limit.
- Give size, weight or pack variants their own titles, images and descriptions so they match different keyword pools while landing on the same page.
- Put a premium or distinguishing word near the front if you compete with keyword-stuffed marketplace titles.

**3. Treat the image as a hook.** The image takes most of the listing's space. Its job is to stop the right person and stand out from the listings beside it, for example a coloured background when every rival uses white, or showing everything included in a bundle. Fashion's tall default images crop badly in Shopping, so supply a supplementary image in a 4:5 ratio. Study the top six listings for your main query and design against them.

**4. Add differentiating feeds.** Promotion and review feeds add sale callouts and star ratings. Shipping rates and delivery times entered in Merchant Center appear under the listing; fast, cheap shipping is a real reason to click.

**5. Run one feed per currency.** If you sell in several countries, give each its own currency feed. A single-currency feed shown abroad with "converted from" pricing and tax notes hurts both CTR and conversion rate.

**6. Roll out slowly.** Mass title changes can reset learning. Change titles in batches (say 50 products at a time, starting with top sellers) and watch CTR, CPC and returns per listing before the next batch.

**7. Keep watching stock and product churn.** Out-of-stock top sellers, sudden catalogue growth and products flipping in and out of stock all disrupt Shopping and PMax learning. Check top spenders for availability and disapprovals whenever performance dips ([[Google Ads Self-Audit and Bottleneck Diagnosis]]).

## Example
Say a bookshop's Shopping campaign for paperbacks shows low ad relevance. Its titles read "[Brand] - Great Read". Rewriting them as "Paperback - [Book Title] - [Author]", adding review and promotion feeds and testing a cleaner cover image fixes relevance without touching bids. A hypothetical apparel brand re-titles its top 200 SKUs in four batches of 50, checking CTR between batches, and adds 4:5 images for its dresses.

## Common mistakes
- Leaving the brand name first on an unknown brand.
- One title for every variant, so a single listing competes for every size and pack.
- Default white-background images that look identical to every competitor.
- Ignoring warnings because the products "still serve".
- Rewriting the whole catalogue in one day and resetting learning.
- Showing converted foreign-currency prices instead of running a proper per-country feed.
