# Performance Max vs Standard Shopping

## What it is
Both campaign types serve your product feed in Shopping results. Standard Shopping stays on Shopping surfaces and gives you a search terms report, negative keywords and product-group control. Performance Max (PMax) spreads across Shopping, Search, YouTube, Display, Discover and Gmail, decides most things itself, and is excellent at following site visitors across those surfaces.

There is no universal winner. You can lead with PMax and treat standard Shopping as support, or lead with standard Shopping for cold acquisition and keeps PMax small as a retargeting layer. The better question is not "which one?" but "what share of spend should PMax get, given this account?"

A platform change makes this choice real. PMax used to take automatic priority over standard Shopping when both covered the same products, so a Shopping campaign beside it barely served. Ad rank now decides which serves, so standard Shopping can win auctions and spend profitably on the same catalogue.

## When to use it
- Setting up product ads on a new account.
- When PMax reports a high ROAS but store revenue does not move as you scale it.
- When a low-budget account runs several PMax campaigns and none has enough data.

## How (steps)
Work through five questions and record the answers.
1. **Is the account new?** Start with standard Shopping. PMax has too little data for smart bidding, and on a small budget a slice of it (often 20-40%) leaks into Search and other surfaces instead of Shopping.
2. **What other traffic do you run?** If you spend heavily elsewhere (say a large Meta budget), PMax will spend much of its budget retargeting that traffic through brand terms, display and video, and claim the sale. For growth, lean on standard Shopping or hold PMax back. If Google is your only paid channel, there is little to retarget and PMax has to find new buyers.
3. **How long is the purchase cycle?** Measure average days from first click to purchase. For quick, low-ticket buys (hours to a few days), PMax's long follow-up is wasted and extra budget mostly buys retargeting. For considered purchases (one to two weeks, such as furniture), that follow-up can be what closes the sale.
4. **Control or a possible small edge?** Standard Shopping gives you brand vs cold separation, search terms, negatives and a predictable scaling ceiling. PMax may perform slightly better at current spend, but you cannot steer extra budget and it is hard to measure.
5. **If you run both, how will you judge PMax?** On incremental returns, not its own reported ROAS. See [[Is Performance Max Incremental]].

**Which approach fits when**
- *PMax-led* fits small accounts (roughly under $15-20K/month) getting moving, Google as the main channel, longer purchase cycles, and owners who prefer simplicity. Run it with proper asset groups and brand exclusions ([[Performance Max Setup - Asset Groups and Brand Exclusions]]).
- *Shopping-led* fits mid-size and larger accounts, brands with heavy spend on other channels, short purchase cycles, and anyone who needs clean new-customer data. Here PMax often settles at 20-40% of product-ad spend at most, frequently with a higher ROAS target so it acts as the converter for warm users.

**Testing standard Shopping beside PMax**
1. Make sure the two campaigns include identical products.
2. Launch a low-budget catch-all standard Shopping campaign (or one category).
3. Once a few weeks have passed, look at impression share, attributed ROAS and the search terms side by side.
4. If cold performance holds, shift budget toward Shopping and keep PMax throttled.
5. Judge on new-customer revenue and total store revenue.

**Low-budget accounts**
One PMax per category spreads a small budget too thinly. Check the channel split: if roughly 85-90% of PMax spend already goes to Shopping, PMax adds little. Consolidate weak category PMax campaigns into one catch-all standard Shopping campaign, give proven categories their own Shopping campaign, attach a universal negative list, and use the search terms report to seed search campaigns. You can pull a winner into its own campaign without the learning reset PMax suffers.

## Example
Say a store spends $25K/month on Meta and $6K on Google. PMax shows ROAS 8 and a standard Shopping test shows 4. Since most of what PMax does is retarget people who came from Meta, base your scaling plan on an incremental ROAS of around 3-4 for PMax, and direct fresh budget to standard Shopping on cold terms. A hypothetical rebalance: PMax from $200/day to $120/day, Shopping from $30/day to $80/day, watching total revenue rather than either campaign's ROAS.

## Common mistakes
- Running default-settings PMax with no brand exclusions and calling the result "Google performance".
- Discounting standard Shopping's lower reported ROAS without noticing that PMax over-credits and Shopping beside it under-credits.
- Running a separate PMax per category on a small budget.
- Assuming results from a small account carry over to a large one, or vice versa.
- Adding a standard Shopping campaign to "feed" PMax and letting it run unprofitably without proving any lift.
