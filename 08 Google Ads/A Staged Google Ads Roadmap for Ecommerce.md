# A Staged Google Ads Roadmap for Ecommerce

## What it is
A sequence for building Google Ads for a store, rather than launching everything at once. It has two versions: a ramp for a new account and a 90-day repair plan for an existing one. Both rest on the same idea: get clean data and conversion volume first, then add automation and complexity once the models have something to learn from.

Before you start, decide which kind of problem you have. Some brands overspend on Google, letting it harvest demand created by Meta, TikTok, email or PR while it reports a high ROAS. Others underspend, treating Google as a small bottom-of-funnel line while plenty of cold search demand goes unbought. The roadmap differs for each.

## When to use it
- Launching Google Ads on a new account.
- Inheriting an account that runs on default PMax with little visibility.
- Hitting a plateau and wondering whether the next step is inside or outside the ad account.

## How (steps)
**New account ramp**
1. **Start with standard Shopping and brand search**, plus a little remarketing. PMax scales more slowly, needs a bigger starting budget and works better once the account already holds conversion data. If you want PMax early, keep it small and give it a higher ROAS target than Shopping, so it behaves like a retargeting layer.
2. **Keep the first structure consolidated.** Hero products or categories get their own ad groups and everything else sits in a catch-all, often inside one campaign. Two SKUs are not enough volume for a "top sellers" group; work at category or brand level. If several hero products already perform well individually, they can have separate campaigns; if you do not know yet, start them together and split once data arrives.
3. **Bid simply at first.** Use conservative manual CPC, or maximise clicks if you accept that it can eat budget.
4. **Aim for conversion volume, not profit, for about three months.** Find which products and queries convert, then move into an optimisation cycle.
5. **Switch to smart bidding** (maximise conversion value, later a ROAS target) once volume allows. See [[Google Bidding - Manual, Smart and Target ROAS]].
6. **Add feed-only PMax last**, on proven brands or categories, rarely single SKUs. Some accounts do best with just search, standard Shopping and display remarketing; that is a valid end state.
7. **Localise the whole journey** when entering a new country: ads in a language your site does not speak convert poorly.

**90-day repair plan for an existing account**
1. **Days 1-30, tracking and hygiene.** Install a direct conversion tag with enhanced conversions and auto-tagging ([[Google Ads Data - Enhanced Conversions and Customer Lists]]). Map every campaign and justify each split. Add brand exclusions and negatives to cold campaigns. Fix PMax asset groups.
2. **Days 31-60, structure.** Launch or rebalance standard Shopping alongside PMax. Audit waste: tiny orphan campaigns, unprofitable search terms, Display network switched on in Search campaigns. Refresh assets (sitelinks, callouts, promotions that have expired).
3. **Days 61-90, feed.** Rewrite titles and images in batches, watching CTR, CPC and returns per listing ([[Shopping Feed Optimisation]]).

**Removing products safely**
Once something works, strip out the parts that are failing instead of lifting the winner into its own big campaign, which rarely holds up. Never remove most of a catalogue at once: excluding the bulk of SKUs, or all low performers in one go, can collapse a campaign. If it happens, switch products back on, let the campaign settle, then remove them in chunks.

**Baseline test before blaming structure**
Choose the brands or categories that show healthy volume plus a better-than-average conversion rate, and put them together in one PMax on maximise conversion value for 30-60 days. If even that cannot convert, the problem is product-market fit, the site or positioning, not campaign setup.

## Example
Say a furniture store spends about $8K/month. Months one and two: one Shopping campaign with a "hero sofas" ad group and an "everything else" group on manual CPC, plus brand search. Month three: maximise conversion value, and products with zero sales removed a few hundred at a time. Month four: a feed-only PMax on the sofa category at a higher target than Shopping.

## Common mistakes
- Launching a new account on default PMax with no data and no search terms report.
- Chasing profitability in month one and starving the account of the volume it needs to learn.
- Excluding thousands of SKUs at once and resetting learning.
- Rebuilding every time performance dips instead of applying one fix and waiting.
- Looking for the next gain inside the ad account when the ceiling is actually LTV, margin, MER or site conversion rate.
