# Analysing Video Ads With Multimodal AI

## What it is
Some models accept video files directly and can assess the opening visual, captions, overlay text and narration in one pass. Used well, they help you extract the principle behind a strong ad, whether you made it or a competitor did and apply it to a different product. Used badly, they produce surface descriptions ("strong hook, good energy") that transfer to nothing. Capabilities change quickly, so check which current models accept video.

## When to use it
- When you have a top-spending ad and want to know what to test next.
- When studying a competitor's long-running ad to learn why it works.
- When you want a structured second opinion on the emotional tone of a batch of headlines.

## How (steps)
1. **Get the file.** Screen-record the ad, or download it through an ad-spy tool after narrowing to the advertiser and ordering by recency and engagement. Only analyse; do not reuse the footage.
2. **Load context.** Attach your avatar and angle files. Without them, its reads on audience and demographics are unreliable.
3. **Frame the question.** Explain that this ad spends the most in your account, state today's daily budget and the one you want to reach, and ask which angles to try next and where the execution could be stronger. Insist that it looks at the opening visual, the captions and overlay text, and the narration.
4. **Ask the core questions:** What makes a viewer care? Which viewer exactly? What do they care about, and which desire drives that?
5. **Ask for the principle.** A principle is a reason the ad works that would still be true for another product. "It lifts the blame off the viewer" is a principle; "great opening shot" is a description.
6. **Transfer it.** Request fresh angles and hooks that carry the principle over to your product, optionally matched against your hook library.
7. **Watch for contradictions.** Models sometimes recommend the opposite of the principle they found (proposing a guilt-heavy hook right after deciding the ad won by lifting guilt). When that happens, reconcile it yourself.
8. **Filter by your research.** Keep suggestions that fit your avatar research and discard what conflicts with it. Then test; some outputs will not make sense.
9. **Run a valence and intensity check on headlines.** Ask the model to rate how positive or negative each headline's emotion is and how strong, so you can spot fear-heavy lines and gaps in your emotional range.

## Example
Say you analyse a winning pet dental-care ad. It opens on an unpleasant close-up, leans on the owner's guilt, then offers a low-effort fix. The model first describes the hook as "attention-grabbing". Pushed for a principle, it identifies the real move: the ad names a chore owners put off because it feels difficult, then tells them it is not their fault. Transferred to a postpartum supplement, that becomes: find what new mothers put off because it feels impossible, then validate them ("you're not failing; it's hormones"), rather than adding guilt. A valence check on the resulting headlines flags one as low-valence and high-intensity, so it is softened before testing.

## Common mistakes
- Accepting description-level feedback.
- Running the analysis with no brand context.
- Missing it when the model confuses one ad or brand with another.
- Treating every suggestion as worth testing instead of filtering by research.
- Analysing only mechanics (cuts, captions) and ignoring the psychology underneath.
