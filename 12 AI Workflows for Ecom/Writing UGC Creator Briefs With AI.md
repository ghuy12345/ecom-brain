# Writing UGC Creator Briefs With AI

## What it is
Unlike a voiceover script, a UGC brief has to be filmed by a real person. The brief has to fit who that person is, where they film and what they can show. An LLM loaded with your research and avatar can draft these briefs quickly and can compile raw sources (such as forum threads) into briefs, which lets a small team raise volume without lowering quality.

## When to use it
- When you send briefs to creators regularly and writing each from scratch is a bottleneck.
- When you have fresh research that has not yet been turned into creative.
- When you are scaling and want more briefs before you can hire another strategist.

For what a brief should and should not dictate, see [[Creator Briefs - Frameworks, Not Scripts]].

## How (steps)
1. **Make sure the brand project holds** your research, avatar and product facts.
2. **Supply creator details.** Make the model ask for them if they are missing: gender, age range, ethnicity, filming location, and whether one creator or several will film. A script for a 25-year-old in a gym is different from one for a 55-year-old at a kitchen table.
3. **Generate concepts.** Ask for a list where each concept has a hook, a short explanation, the mass desire it targets and the research it came from. Read them, pick about three, and invent at least one of your own. The best use of AI here is to spark your thinking, not replace it.
4. **Generate the brief** with a fixed structure:
   - overview, concept and target duration (45 to 60 seconds is common; confirm what the model suggests)
   - filming requirements
   - the opening hook sequence, with the first few seconds described visually
   - a direct-to-camera script with an action direction for each beat
   - a B-roll shot list, noting where each shot will be used in the edit
   - key reminders and what success looks like
5. **Critique and revise.** Push for a more arresting opening shot and a sharper case for why the product wins over the alternative.
6. **Fix platform details** such as where the link sits and how the CTA is phrased.
7. **Format and share** in a shareable document rather than a static PDF, and polish before it goes out.
8. **Set expectations with the creator.** Make clear that sample ads are there to inspire, not to be copied. When results take a while to show, give creators around two weeks of use before asking for their reaction.

## Example
Say a drinkware brand wants a side-by-side taste test. The brief opens with slow-motion pours into two cups labelled with very different prices, then cuts to the creator's knowing look. Tasters prefer the "premium" one; then the twist shows the drinks were identical and only the cups changed. Because the whole ad depends on the comparison being real, the brief requires a genuine blind test and tells the creator to film real reactions, not staged ones.

A pet brand compiling forum research might paste twenty threads about dogs that pull on walks into the project and ask for three briefs, each built around a specific scene owners described in their own words.

## Common mistakes
- Skipping the creator-information step, so scripts do not fit the person filming.
- Briefs that ask creators to rig a test while presenting it as genuine. Require a real test, or disclose that it is a dramatisation.
- Over-scripting every word, which kills the natural delivery UGC depends on.
- Forgetting platform-specific call-to-action wording.
- Sending the AI draft without your own polish.
