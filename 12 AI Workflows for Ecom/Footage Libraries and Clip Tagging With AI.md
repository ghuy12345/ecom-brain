# Footage Libraries and Clip Tagging With AI

## What it is
As creator and product footage piles up, nobody can find anything, and good material gets shot once and forgotten. A clip library tool transcribes every upload automatically and cuts it into short segments rather than keeping whole videos. With consistent tags on top, editors and strategists can find the right shot in seconds, and AI passes over the transcripts can suggest hooks tied to clips you already have.

## When to use it
Only once you produce a lot of creative across several editors. A young brand can skip it; a tidy, well-named shared drive does the job. Use the library once retrieval time and lost footage are slowing your briefs.

## How (steps)
**Set up**
1. **Put all raw footage in a single shared folder** and link that folder to the library tool.
2. **Think in clips.** Labels apply to clips, not full videos, so design your taxonomy at clip level.
3. **Build three retrieval layers:**
   - transcript search (what was said)
   - a product tag per SKU or bundle
   - visual filters: whether a creator is in frame, B-roll, talking versus silent, CTA moments, and product close-ups
4. **Add custom categories** that matter for briefs, such as setting, creator age range, or pets and family on screen. Many tools pre-label common types (product shot, testimonial, unboxing).

**Bulk labelling**
1. Search or filter for the theme.
2. Set up the new label on one clip first; some tools only offer bulk tagging for labels that already exist.
3. Multi-select every qualifying clip and apply the label at once.
4. Reuse existing categories when new footage arrives.
5. Assign one editor to own the taxonomy so labels do not drift.

**Mining**
1. **Search transcripts for angle words:** ingredients, conditions, product nicknames, objections.
2. **Prospect seasonally.** Ahead of the holidays or a cold season, look for gifting and weather words in transcripts to surface content you shot and forgot, then tag it.
3. **Generate hooks from your own footage.** Running AI across the transcripts each week can return hook suggestions, each pointing to its source clip, so the editor can build straight from existing material.
4. **Brief with clip links.** Strategists pick clips in the library and drop the links into the brief.

**Build or buy**
- Do tagging in-house with editors at moderate volume; pay for a managed tagging service once the library has grown big.
- Software-only tiers usually cost about what a modest subscription does; managed plans run to a multiple of that. Pricing changes often.
- Treat AI hook lists as inspiration; they still need testing.

## Example
Say a skincare brand has two years of creator footage across six editors. Searching transcripts for "winter" and "dry" surfaces forty clips nobody remembered, including several creators describing cracked hands. They are tagged for a winter campaign, and the weekly AI hook list suggests "the reason your hands crack every December" with three matching clips. An editor builds the first cut the same afternoon without new filming.

## Common mistakes
- Labelling by eye with no standard, so later searches are unreliable.
- Guessing demographics such as age range with no agreed definitions.
- Dropping edge cases into a category without flagging them.
- Letting several people invent labels independently.
- Buying the tool before you have the volume to justify it.
