# AI Product Images and B-Roll

## What it is
AI image and video tools can create product scenes, lifestyle shots and B-roll without a photoshoot. They are useful for filling gaps, making visuals congruent with a headline, and avoiding the legal risk of borrowing footage from social media. They are also a time sink: the message outweighs visual polish, so the tools should serve a tested concept rather than replace one.

## When to use it
- When a static or video needs a specific scene you do not have (the product next to an object that makes the headline concrete).
- When you need B-roll and would otherwise be tempted to use footage you do not own.
- When a designer or editor has time to match each visual to its headline. If you are a beginner without one, focus on copy first.

## How (steps)
**Two routes to product imagery**
1. **Compositing route (fastest).** Drop a clean cutout of the product onto a background (generated or picked) in an AI image tool, then finish the blend in a photo editor. The product stays accurate because it is your real photo.
2. **Prompt route (more control).** Describe a complete, realistic scene, guided by reference photos you like, then tidy it in a photo editor. More work, but you control the environment.
3. **Products on people** (jewellery on a hand, clothing on a body) can go either way; treat it as experimental and check carefully for realism and accurate product details.

**Images for headline congruence**
1. Show the generator your layout with the picture zone highlighted, add the real product photo, and describe a scene that makes the headline concrete.
2. Ask for the image only, not the whole ad. Iterate (bigger product, different angle), remove the background and drop it into the layout.

**AI B-roll**
1. Generate generic scenes (routines, settings, problem moments) to cover gaps in a script.
2. Use it instead of clips lifted from social platforms.

**AI avatars and presenters**
1. AI-generated presenters can lower production spend and turnaround.
2. Any promise that it matches real creators stays unproven until your own account confirms it.
3. Never use AI to fabricate visual proof of results (before-and-after images, fake testimonials, or people styled as doctors or experts).

## Example
Say a skincare brand tests a static with the headline "five bottles on the shelf and still no change". A generated image of the product beside a crowded bathroom shelf makes the line instantly visual. The team composites the real product photo so the label is accurate, rather than letting the model redraw it. For a matching video ad, they generate B-roll of a morning bathroom-routine scene instead of borrowing a clip from social media. A vendor's AI presenter is trialled in one ad set, and its results are compared with creator ads before anyone decides it can replace them.

## Common mistakes
- Spending hours playing with image tools before the concept has been tested.
- Letting a model redraw the product so packaging, colours or text are wrong.
- Generating images that imply results the product cannot deliver.
- Lifting footage from social platforms instead of generating it.
- Believing vendor claims about AI avatar performance without your own test.
- Testing several visuals when nobody can match each one to its headline.
