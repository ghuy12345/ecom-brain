# Glossary - Metrics & Media Buying

Short definitions of the numbers and structures you meet when buying ads. Follow the arrow to the note that covers the term in depth.

**ABO (ad set budget optimisation)** — Each ad set gets its own fixed daily budget, so you decide how money is split rather than the platform. Useful for guaranteeing spend to a proven ad, at the cost of some efficiency. → [[Isolating Winners in a Dedicated ABO]]

**Acquisition efficiency (aMER)** — Revenue from first-time customers divided by total ad spend. It strips out returning-customer revenue, so it shows whether ads are actually buying new customers at a profit.

**Advantage+ / automated shopping campaign** — Meta's automated campaign type that decides audience, placements and much of the delivery for you. You trade control for simplicity. → [[Catalog Ads and Automated Shopping Campaigns]]

**AOV (average order value)** — Total purchase value divided by the number of orders. A higher AOV lets you afford a higher cost per purchase at the same margin. → [[Post-Purchase Upsells and AOV Levers]]

**Attribution setting / window** — The rule that decides which purchases an ad gets credit for, such as purchases within seven days of a click. It shapes both what you see in reporting and who the platform optimises toward. → [[Click vs View-Through Attribution]]

**Blended ROAS** — Total store revenue divided by total ad spend across every channel. Easy to read, but it hides whether growth came from new or returning customers. → [[Reading an Ecommerce P&L]]

**Breakeven ROAS** — The ROAS at which an order covers its variable costs and leaves zero contribution. Roughly one divided by your gross margin percentage. → [[Breakeven ROAS and KPI Tiers]]

**Breakthrough ad** — A rare new ad that lifts the whole account's spend ceiling, not just one that meets target. Only a small share of launches reach this tier. → [[Defining Winning, Losing and Breakthrough Ads]]

**CBO (campaign budget optimisation)** — The budget sits at campaign level and the platform moves it between ad sets and ads by expected results. The default for consolidated scaling. → [[Consolidate vs Segment Campaigns]]

**Click-based ROAS** — ROAS counting only purchases from people who clicked the ad. Usually lower than reported ROAS, and a better guide to what the ad truly caused. → [[Click vs View-Through Attribution]]

**Concept** — The thing a test batch is trying out: an angle, a format, an avatar or an iteration of a winner. → [[Concept vs Angle - Ad Testing Vocabulary]]

**CPA (cost per acquisition)** — Ad spend divided by platform-reported purchases. It is attributed, so it often looks better than your true customer acquisition cost. → [[Ads Manager Setup - Columns, Benchmarks, Naming and UTMs]]

**CPC (cost per click)** — Spend divided by link clicks. A soft metric: two ads with very different CPCs can still return similar profit.

**CPM (cost per mille)** — What you pay for a thousand impressions. It reflects auction competition and season more than ad quality. → [[Reading Performance Swings and Stabilising Volatility]]

**CTR (link click-through rate)** — Link clicks divided by impressions. A useful read on whether the ad earns interest, but never the goal in itself. → [[Diagnosing Clicks That Do Not Convert]]

**CVR (conversion rate)** — Purchases divided by landing-page views (or sessions). Compare against your own baseline, since niches vary widely. → [[Diagnosing a Store With Conversion Benchmarks]]

**Dynamic creative / flexible ads** — A single ad that holds several images, videos and text options and lets the platform mix them. Flexible ads replaced the older dynamic format and do not report results per asset. → [[Creative Testing Campaign Setup]]

**Frequency** — Average number of times each reached person saw your ad in a period. Rising frequency on prospecting usually means the audience or creative is wearing out. → [[Diagnosing Plateaus and Stalls]]

**Hard vs soft metrics** — Hard metrics are money outcomes (spend, purchases, CPA, ROAS). Soft metrics (CTR, CPC, hook, hold, add-to-cart) help diagnose why, but should not decide what you keep. → [[Ads Manager Setup - Columns, Benchmarks, Naming and UTMs]]

**Hit rate** — The share of launched ads that become winners. Expect it to be low; volume and quality of ideas both move it. → [[Hit Rate vs Creative Volume]]

**Hold rate** — ThruPlays divided by 3-second plays: of the viewers who stay past the hook, the share who keep watching to about the fifteen-second mark, or to the end of a shorter clip. Some teams divide by impressions instead, which is delivery efficiency (hook rate times hold rate). Compare only between videos of similar length. → [[Winning Video Ad Teardown - Line by Line]]

**Hook rate** — The share of impressions that turn into at least three seconds of viewing: a read on how well the opening stops the scroll. Irrelevant for static images. → [[Video Hook Fundamentals]]

**Incrementality** — Whether a sale happened because of the ad or would have happened anyway. Reported numbers rarely show it directly. → [[Using a Third-Party Attribution Dashboard]]

**KPI tiers** — Target bands (for example breakeven, target and stretch) used to judge ads and decide when to scale. → [[Setting a Target ROAS - Breakeven Plus One]]

**MER (marketing efficiency ratio)** — Total revenue divided by total marketing spend. A whole-business view that ignores which platform claims credit.

**Multi-creative test** — Launching one concept as a few creatives plus a couple of primary texts and headlines, so the platform can find the best combination. → [[Multi-Angle vs Single-Angle vs Volume Testing]]

**Partnership ads (whitelisting)** — Ads that run through a creator's or partner's handle instead of the brand's page. → [[Whitelisting and Partnership Ads]]

**ROAS (return on ad spend)** — Revenue attributed to ads divided by ad spend. Good for comparing ads within one platform, weak for big business decisions. → [[Breakeven ROAS and KPI Tiers]]

**RPS (revenue per session)** — Revenue divided by store sessions; it combines conversion rate and AOV into one number for judging page tests. → [[Revenue and Profit Per Session]]

**Spend as the signal** — In one broad campaign, an ad that wins meaningful budget at target efficiency is treated as a winner; one that gets no spend is treated as a loser. → [[One Broad Campaign - Spend as the Signal]]

**ThruPlay** — Meta's count of views that hit fifteen seconds, or the end of any clip shorter than that. It feeds the hold-rate calculation.

**UTM parameters** — Tags added to ad links so analytics tools can tell which campaign and ad brought each visit. → [[Ads Manager Setup - Columns, Benchmarks, Naming and UTMs]]

**View-through attribution** — Credit given to an ad that was seen but not clicked before a purchase. Helpful for tiny accounts, but often counts buyers who were coming anyway. → [[Click vs View-Through Attribution]]

**Winning ad** — An ad that takes meaningful spend and holds your KPI over time; a losing ad fails to get spend or misses target. → [[Defining Winning, Losing and Breakthrough Ads]]
