# Glossary - Ops, Finance & Sourcing

Short definitions of the money and supply-chain words used in the finance and sourcing notes. Follow the arrow to the note that covers the term properly.

**AQL (acceptable quality limit)** — The maximum share of defective units allowed in a random inspection sample before a batch fails. It turns "good enough" into an agreed number. → [[Building a Quality Control System]]

**Breakeven CAC** — The most you can pay to win a customer before the first order loses money; equal to the gross profit on that order. → [[Breakeven ROAS and KPI Tiers]]

**CAC (customer acquisition cost)** — Total ad spend divided by new customers counted in your store's own data. Stricter than platform CPA because it is not attribution-based. → [[LTGP to CAC Ratio and the CAC Death Spiral]]

**Cash conversion cycle** — Days your cash sits in stock and receivables, minus the days you take to pay suppliers. The shorter it is, the less cash growth demands. → [[Cash Conversion Cycle]]

**Certificate of analysis** — A lab document confirming a batch of a formula meets its specification. Ask for one before each skincare or supplement run. → [[Developing a Custom Skincare or Supplement Formula]]

**Chinese New Year shutdown** — The weeks around the lunar new year when most Chinese factories and logistics stop, delaying production on both sides. → [[Preparing for the Chinese New Year Shutdown]]

**COGS (cost of goods sold)** — What the product costs you. Use the fully loaded version (product, packaging, freight, duties, fulfilment) rather than the factory price alone. → [[Contribution Margin and Variable Costs]]

**Cohort** — A batch of customers who first bought in the same month or week, followed as one group to see how much they spend over time. → [[Cohort LTV Modelling]]

**Contribution margin** — Revenue minus every cost that moves with sales, including ad spend, before fixed overheads. The number scaling decisions should rest on. → [[Contribution Margin and Variable Costs]]

**DDP (delivered duty paid)** — An Incoterm where one price includes freight, duties and the final drop at your warehouse; the shipping provider acts as importer. → [[Supplier Payment Terms and Incoterms]]

**Defect rate** — The share of units that arrive faulty. Each point lost goes straight to refunds, reviews and margin. → [[Defect Rate as a Profit Lever]]

**Deposit and balance** — The common factory payment split: a share up front to start production and the rest on completion or shipment. → [[Supplier Payment Terms and Incoterms]]

**DFMEA / PFMEA** — Failure-mode analyses that list how a design (DFMEA) or a production process (PFMEA) could fail and how each risk is controlled. A sign of a mature factory. → [[Building a Quality Control System]]

**EBITDA** — Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation: contribution margin minus operating expenses. → [[Reading an Ecommerce P&L]]

**EXW (Ex Works)** — An Incoterm where you take over at the factory door and arrange all pickup, export and freight yourself. → [[Supplier Payment Terms and Incoterms]]

**Float** — The gap between spending money and having to pay the bill, created by invoice terms, charge cards or supplier terms. Useful leverage, and a liability if payback slips. → [[Ad Account Billing and Credit Float]]

**FOB (free on board)** — An Incoterm where the supplier gets goods onto the vessel at the origin port and handles export clearance; cost and risk pass to you from that point. → [[Supplier Payment Terms and Incoterms]]

**Fulfilment agent** — A warehouse plus purchasing office that sources existing stock and ships orders. Very low minimums and fast, but little customisation or price transparency. → [[Supplier Types - Factories, Trading Companies and Agents]]

**Golden sample** — The approved reference unit that both you and the factory keep, against which every batch is judged. → [[Building a Quality Control System]]

**Gross margin** — Net revenue minus all variable costs to deliver the order, as a share of revenue. Much lower than "product margin", which only subtracts the factory price. → [[Reading an Ecommerce P&L]]

**Incoterms** — Standard trade terms that fix where responsibility, cost and risk pass from supplier to buyer, and so when net-terms clocks start. → [[Supplier Payment Terms and Incoterms]]

**Inspection checkpoints (OQC, IQC, in-line, final)** — Quality checks on outgoing components, incoming components at assembly, during production and before packing. → [[Building a Quality Control System]]

**Landed cost** — The full cost of a unit once it is in your warehouse: product, freight, duties and handling. → [[Contribution Margin and Variable Costs]]

**Lead time** — Time from placing a purchase order to having stock ready to sell. Longer lead times tie up more cash. → [[Inventory Planning for Q4-Heavy Brands]]

**LTGP (lifetime gross profit)** — Gross profit a customer generates over a fixed window such as 90 or 180 days. More honest than revenue-based LTV. → [[LTGP to CAC Ratio and the CAC Death Spiral]]

**LTV (lifetime value)** — What a customer is worth over time. Measure it in profit over a fixed window, not revenue forever. → [[Cohort LTV Modelling]]

**MOQ (minimum order quantity)** — The smallest order a supplier will accept. Factories need higher MOQs to keep lines running; intermediaries can go lower. → [[Finding and Vetting Manufacturers]]

**Net revenue** — Gross sales minus refunds and discounts, adjusted for shipping charged. → [[Reading an Ecommerce P&L]]

**Net terms (net 30, net 60)** — Paying a supplier a set number of days after shipment or delivery rather than up front. → [[Supplier Payment Terms and Incoterms]]

**OEM / ODM** — OEM: the factory builds to your own design and specification. ODM: the factory sells its existing design, which you brand.

**OPEX (operating expenses)** — Fixed running costs such as people, software and space, which do not rise with each order. → [[Reading an Ecommerce P&L]]

**Payback window** — The time a customer cohort has to earn back what it cost to acquire, often around 60 to 90 days. → [[Cash Conversion Cycle]]

**Rolling deposit** — A standing deposit held by the factory for materials while you pay each shipment in full or on short terms. → [[Working With a Factory Over Time]]

**SKU grading** — Ranking products by how fast they sell (fast, medium, slow, stuck) so you protect stock on the best and turn slow stock back into cash. → [[Cash Flow Forecasting]]

**Sourcing company** — A team that represents you with a network of factories, handling development, quality checks and negotiation for a fee. → [[Supplier Types - Factories, Trading Companies and Agents]]

**Stress test** — Rerunning your model with worse inputs (higher CAC, lower repeat rate, more refunds, slower payouts) before you scale. → [[Stress-Testing Unit Economics]]

**Trading company** — An office that resells factory output, often presenting itself as a factory. Lower minimums and easier communication, but a hidden margin. → [[Supplier Types - Factories, Trading Companies and Agents]]

**White label** — Selling a supplier's stock product under your brand with only logo, colour or packaging changes.
