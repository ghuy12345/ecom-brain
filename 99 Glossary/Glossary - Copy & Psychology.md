# Glossary - Copy & Psychology

Short definitions of the words used when planning and writing ads and pages. Several come from Eugene Schwartz's Breakthrough Advertising. Follow the arrow for the full treatment.

**Angle** — The core selling reason an ad puts forward, such as "lasts a lifetime" or "no plastic touches your water". Every ad needs one, whatever its format. → [[Concept vs Angle - Ad Testing Vocabulary]]

**Avatar** — A specific group of people most likely to buy, defined by what they want, have been through, feel and do, not only by age and gender. → [[Avatar Fundamentals - Beyond Demographics]]

**Awareness levels** — Five stages describing how much a prospect knows about their problem and your product: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware and most aware. The stage decides how an ad should open. → [[Market Awareness Levels]]

**Belief** — Something the customer holds as true about the problem, the category or themselves. Copy either works with existing beliefs or builds new ones step by step. → [[Customer Beliefs - Where They Come From and How to Build Them]]

**Bridge** — The lines right after the hook that connect the attention you grabbed to the problem, mechanism or product. → [[Writing the Bridge After the Hook]]

**Callback** — Restating an earlier tease just before you pay it off, which re-raises tension right before the release. → [[Open Loops - Layering, Chaining and Closing]]

**Camouflage** — Presenting an ad in a familiar, editorial-looking form so it gets read rather than skipped. → [[Breakthrough Advertising Ch 13 - Camouflage]]

**Concept** — What a batch of ads is testing: an angle, a format, an avatar or an iteration. → [[Concept vs Angle - Ad Testing Vocabulary]]

**Core avatar** — An avatar defined by one category only, usually a single desire, experience or behaviour. → [[Building Core Avatars From Desires]]

**Core vs surface desire** — Core desires are broad human drives (health, status, safety, love). Surface desires are the specific "I want..." statements real customers say. Build ads on the surface desire while knowing the drive beneath it. → [[Core Human Desires and Layers of Desire]]

**Emotional zones** — Four regions created by crossing valence with intensity: calm reassurance, excited pride, low-key irritation and alarm. Each suits different audiences and angles. → [[Emotional Valence and Intensity]]

**Format** — How an ad is delivered: video, static image, carousel, slideshow, plus styles such as creator-style or voiceover. → [[Ad Format Taxonomy - Format vs Style]]

**Future pacing** — Helping the reader picture life after the purchase, so the result feels near and easy. → [[Show, Don't Tell - In Words and Visuals]]

**Gradualization** — Leading the reader through a chain of small, easy agreements so a big claim feels believable by the time it arrives. → [[Breakthrough Advertising Ch 9 - Gradualization - Part 1]]

**Hook** — The first one to three seconds of a video, or the headline of a static, whose only job is to stop the right person and earn the next line. → [[Video Hook Fundamentals]]

**Identification** — Selling the role or identity the buyer wants to step into, not just the object. Dominant in the most crowded markets. → [[Identification - Selling the Role, Not the Object]]

**Intensification** — Making the desire more vivid and urgent through detail, imagery and proof, so the reader feels it more strongly. → [[Breakthrough Advertising Ch 7 - Intensification - Part 1]]

**Intensity** — How strong an emotion is, from mild to overwhelming. Paired with valence. → [[Emotional Valence and Intensity]]

**Juxtaposition** — Placing two ideas side by side that seem to clash, so the gap between them creates curiosity. → [[Juxtaposition in Copy]]

**Market sophistication** — How many similar claims and products a market has already seen. Five stages run from first to market, through bigger claims and new mechanisms, to identity-led marketing. → [[Market Sophistication Stages]]

**Mass desire** — A wish already shared by many people. Copy cannot create it; it channels an existing desire onto your product. → [[Mass Desire - Channel It, Don't Create It]]

**Mechanization** — Explaining how a product works so the claim becomes believable. → [[Breakthrough Advertising Ch 11 - Mechanization]]

**Most aware** — Knows your product and wants it but has not bought yet. Usually needs only the offer and an easy path. → [[Market Awareness Levels]]

**New, unspoken and renamed mechanisms** — A new mechanism is genuinely unique; an unspoken one is shared by competitors but never mentioned; a renamed one gives a familiar component a fresh name. → [[Types of Unique Mechanism]]

**Open loop** — An unanswered question or teased payoff that keeps the viewer watching until it is resolved. Every loop you open must close. → [[Open Loops - Layering, Chaining and Closing]]

**Payoff parity** — The rule that a payoff has to match the size of the tease before it. Over-promising erodes trust further down the funnel. → [[Open Loops - Layering, Chaining and Closing]]

**Positioning** — The place your brand holds in the buyer's mind relative to alternatives. Aim to be different rather than slightly better. → [[Brand Positioning - Different, Not Better]]

**Problem aware** — Feels the problem clearly but has no idea what fixes exist. Open by naming the problem so precisely they recognise themselves. → [[Market Awareness Levels]]

**Product aware** — Has heard of your product but still doubts it. Build trust, stack benefits and handle doubts. → [[Market Awareness Levels]]

**Proof** — Evidence that makes a claim believable: demonstrations, reviews, numbers, authority and guarantees. → [[Escalating Levels of Proof]]

**Redefinition** — Reframing a product's drawback or an unfamiliar idea so it reads as an advantage or becomes easy to accept. → [[Breakthrough Advertising Ch 10 - Redefinition]]

**Solution aware** — Knows solutions exist but has not chosen yours. Show what yours does that the others do not. → [[Market Awareness Levels]]

**Sub-avatar** — A core avatar narrowed by adding one more category, such as an emotion or behaviour. Most angle testing happens at this level. → [[Building Sub-Avatars From a Core Desire]]

**Superlative** — A "best" or "first" claim. Strong in young markets, weak where buyers have heard it all. → [[Responding to High Market Sophistication]]

**Unaware** — Not yet conscious of the problem or desire. The hardest stage; ads usually start from an emotion or story anyone can relate to. → [[Unaware Ads - Identity Hooks and Openers]]

**Unique mechanism** — The specific reason your product works where others failed, often split into the cause of the problem and the way you solve it. → [[Unique Mechanism - Problem Cause and Solution]]

**Valence** — Whether an emotion is pleasant or unpleasant. Paired with intensity to place an ad's tone. → [[Emotional Valence and Intensity]]

**Variation** — One execution of an angle or concept: a different wording, visual or opening of the same idea. → [[Iterations, Imitations and Ideations]]

**Voice of customer** — The exact words customers use about their problem and results, collected from reviews, forums and surveys and reused in copy. → [[Writing in Customer Language]]
