# Glossary - Google Ads & Shopping

Quick definitions for the Google side of paid media. Follow the arrow to the note that explains the term in practice.

**Ad relevance** — Google's rating of how closely your ad, keywords and page match the search. Shown as low, average or high. → [[Quality Score and CTR as Diagnostics]]

**Asset group** — The building block inside Performance Max: a set of products, images, text and signals aimed at one product type. → [[Performance Max Setup - Asset Groups and Brand Exclusions]]

**Audience signals / search themes** — Hints you give Performance Max about who might convert. They steer early learning but are not hard targeting, and matter less once an account has data. → [[Performance Max Setup - Asset Groups and Brand Exclusions]]

**Brand exclusions** — Lists that stop a campaign from serving on searches for your own brand name, so it cannot claim easy branded sales as new demand. → [[Performance Max Setup - Asset Groups and Brand Exclusions]]

**Brand vs non-brand** — Splitting searches that include your brand name from everything else. Branded traffic is cheap and already warm, so it is always tracked separately. → [[Brand Search and Branded Shopping Campaigns]]

**Consolidation** — Merging small campaigns so each model learns from more conversions. Only split when margin, geography or product audiences genuinely differ. → [[Google Ads Account Structure for Ecommerce]]

**Custom labels** — Your own tags in the product feed (for example margin band or best-seller) used to group products into campaigns or listing groups. → [[Product-Level Reporting and Zombie Products]]

**Customer lists (Customer Match)** — Uploaded customer emails or phone numbers used to exclude buyers, target them, or give bidding a signal. → [[Google Ads Data - Enhanced Conversions and Customer Lists]]

**Demand Gen** — A Google campaign type that shows visual ads across YouTube, Discover and Gmail to people who are not actively searching. → [[A Staged Google Ads Roadmap for Ecommerce]]

**Display network** — Google's network of partner sites and apps showing banner-style ads. Usually switched off for search campaigns and used deliberately for retargeting. → [[Google Display Retargeting]]

**Enhanced conversions** — Sending hashed first-party customer data with each conversion so Google can match more sales to clicks. → [[Google Ads Data - Enhanced Conversions and Customer Lists]]

**Expected CTR** — Google's projection of how likely your ad is to be clicked for a given search. One of the three Quality Score parts. → [[Quality Score and CTR as Diagnostics]]

**Feed-only Performance Max** — A Performance Max campaign with its creative assets removed so spend goes almost entirely to Shopping placements. → [[Performance Max vs Standard Shopping]]

**Feeder campaign** — A low-target Shopping campaign that buys cheap first clicks from a wide pool, paired with a higher-target campaign that converts them later. → [[Performance Max vs Standard Shopping]]

**Landing page experience** — Google's rating of how relevant, fast and usable your page is for the search. Weak on-site conversion pulls it down. → [[Quality Score and CTR as Diagnostics]]

**Limited by budget** — A status saying the campaign could spend more. Treat it as information, not an order to raise budget.

**Manual CPC / Maximise clicks** — Bidding without conversion data. Only sensible for a brand-new account. → [[Google Bidding - Manual, Smart and Target ROAS]]

**Match types** — Rules for how closely a search must match a keyword: broad (related meanings), phrase (contains the idea) and exact (same meaning, including close variants). → [[Search Terms and Negative Keywords]]

**Maximise conversion value** — Smart bidding that chases the most revenue for your budget with no efficiency target. Good for gathering data before switching to a target. → [[Google Bidding - Manual, Smart and Target ROAS]]

**Maximise conversions** — Smart bidding that chases the most purchases regardless of value. In ecommerce it tends to push spend toward the cheapest products.

**Merchant Center** — The Google account that holds your product feed and must approve products before they can show in Shopping. → [[Shopping Feed Optimisation]]

**Negative keyword** — A term that tells Google not to show your ad for searches containing it. Phrase and exact negatives are the safe defaults. → [[Search Terms and Negative Keywords]]

**Performance Max (PMax)** — Google's automated campaign that serves across Shopping, Search, YouTube, Display and more from one budget. Strong early, but can drift into retargeting at higher spend. → [[Performance Max vs Standard Shopping]]

**Portfolio bid strategy** — One bid strategy shared across several campaigns so they pool learning, with optional bid caps. → [[Google Bidding - Manual, Smart and Target ROAS]]

**Product feed** — The data file of titles, descriptions, images, prices and attributes that Shopping ads are built from. Arguably the biggest Shopping lever. → [[Shopping Feed Optimisation]]

**Quality Score** — A 1 to 10 keyword rating built from three parts: ad relevance, expected CTR and landing page experience. Use it to find what to fix, not as a goal. → [[Quality Score and CTR as Diagnostics]]

**Responsive search ad** — A search ad where you supply many headlines and descriptions and Google tests combinations. → [[Google Ads Interface Basics]]

**Search terms report** — The list of real searches that triggered your ads. The main source for new negative keywords. → [[Search Terms and Negative Keywords]]

**Shared negative list** — A saved list of negatives applied to many campaigns at once, such as job and salary searches. → [[Search Terms and Negative Keywords]]

**Smart bidding** — Any automated bid strategy that sets each auction bid from conversion data (target ROAS, target CPA, maximise value or conversions). → [[Google Bidding - Manual, Smart and Target ROAS]]

**Standard Shopping** — The classic product-listing campaign where you control products, priority and bids more directly than in Performance Max. → [[Performance Max vs Standard Shopping]]

**Target CPA (tCPA)** — Smart bidding aimed at an average cost per conversion. Restrictive, so it trades reach for efficiency.

**Target ROAS (tROAS)** — Smart bidding aimed at an average return on spend. It steadies results rather than raising them; set it too high and spend shrinks toward warm buyers. → [[Google Bidding - Manual, Smart and Target ROAS]]

**tROAS spiral** — When results dip below a target, spend drops, the model gets less data, targeting worsens and spend drops again. Break it by lowering the target decisively.

**URL expansion / auto-created assets** — Performance Max settings that let Google pick landing pages and write extra ad text. Often turned off for tighter control.

**Zombie products** — Products in the feed that get almost no impressions or clicks. Grouping them separately can give them a chance to sell. → [[Product-Level Reporting and Zombie Products]]
