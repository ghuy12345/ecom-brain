# Short-Video and Social Research - TikTok, YouTube, Instagram

## What it is
Social video platforms show what your market watches, how it talks and which formats hold attention. Each platform plays a slightly different research role:
- **TikTok** - search by desire to find native formats, struggles in the comments and creators already speaking to your avatar.
- **YouTube** - titles that the algorithm has effectively tested, long-form reviewers who say their deep desires out loud, and comment sections full of objections.
- **Instagram** - with one trick, a feed made almost entirely of live ads.

## When to use it
- During desire hunting, after you have a first list of wants and do-not-wants.
- When you need hook ideas, format ideas or creators for seeding and paid content.
- As a daily habit (Instagram), to keep your sense of current ad creative fresh.

## How (steps)
**TikTok - search by desire**
1. Search using the wants, don't-wants and desires already in your research document, alongside broad niche terms. Searching the desire ("get rid of frizz") surfaces more than searching the product type.
2. Favour high-engagement videos and look at three things: the clip itself (format, hook, style), the comment section (where the real gold is - struggles, use cases, viewers saying they'll report back) and the person who made it.
3. Watch for words that recur across the top videos; they reveal the market's favourite topics.
4. Qualify creators: confirm they post consistently in your niche rather than once. Record their audience size and links to their other channels - particularly the platform you run ads on.

**YouTube - titles, reviewers and comments**
1. Search the category loosely; off-target results sometimes uncover neighbouring audiences.
2. Collect titles that earn clicks and adapt the structures - head-to-head comparisons, best-value roundups and shortcut or hack formats.
3. Mine descriptions for buzzwords, read the comments for objections and corrections, and actually watch the videos.
4. Turn what reviewers say into a chain running from feature to benefit to everyday want to underlying desire.
5. Educational shorts can expose the fears of an unaware market.

**Instagram - an ads-only feed**
1. In the main feed (not the reels tab), keep going until a sponsored post appears, then tap on that ad.
2. Keep scrolling: from there the feed serves only sponsored posts.
3. Save what matters - send to yourself, copy links into a database from desktop, or download to a local swipe file.
4. Spend ten to fifteen minutes a day here instead of ordinary scrolling. Your behaviour trains the feed: engage with your niche, and flicking past quickly tends to serve shorter ads, while watching ads through tends to serve longer ones and video sales letters.
5. Log hooks that imply a surprising claim, and treat high engagement on an ad as a hint that it performs.

## Example
Imagine a hair-styling tool. On TikTok, searching "frizz" and "healthy hair routine" surfaces tutorials, routines, point-of-view clips and ASMR-style demos. Titles like a "healthiest hair" routine or getting rid of frizz "overnight" become ad ideas - note the time promise ("overnight") strengthening the hook. Split-screen transformations with a deliberately messy "before" make results more believable. One creator posting daily about curly hair turns out to already work with a competitor, which confirms the avatar fit.

On YouTube, a reviewer explains that the vacuum's cyclonic suction gives a deeper clean, which means fresher air and floors, which really means feeling proud to have guests over - a complete chain in one video. A loose search also turns up vacuum-hum sound videos for sleep with huge view counts: an adjacent format idea, but check that audience actually buys before chasing it.

## Common mistakes
- Searching product names instead of desires.
- Skipping the comments, the richest layer on every platform.
- Logging a creator after one relevant video without checking consistency.
- Assuming views or engagement equal buying intent.
- Expecting the Instagram ad feed to match your niche without steering it.
- Not refining search terms when results drift to a different intent.
