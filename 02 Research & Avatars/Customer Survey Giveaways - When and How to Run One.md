# Customer Survey Giveaways - When and How to Run One

## What it is
A survey giveaway is a prize draw open to past buyers where every survey answer earns extra entries. It reveals who your marketing is pulling in and, more usefully, the reason they chose you over everyone else - often the clue that unlocks new angles. The surrounding emails tend to drive some sales too.

This note covers whether to run one and how to build it. The emails are in [[Giveaway Email Sequence]], the draw in [[Giveaway Draw, Winners and ROI]], and analysis in [[Analysing Customer Survey Responses]].

## When to use it
Only when the numbers work and the product is good.
- **Product quality first.** If buyers are disappointed, fix the product before surveying - unhappy customers give complaints, not insight.
- **Aim for at least ~100 entries.** Multiply the past buyers you can actually email by a likely entry rate. Rough rates:
  - one-off, high-ticket products bought once: around 3.5-4%
  - products people rebuy or collect: around 6-9%
  - brands with a devoted fan base: up to ~10%
- A rough floor is about 3,000 emailable purchasers. Say your list is 1,000 and your rate is 3.5%: that is ~35 entries - not enough.
- **Too small?** Call customers instead and ask the same questions verbally (see [[Talking to Customers - Calls, Immersion and Seeding Feedback]]).

## How (steps)
1. **Design the prize ladder.** Number of winners is the variable most clearly linked to participation: three minimum, five better, up to ten. One prize feels unwinnable. Match prizes to what your ideal customer actually wants. Common patterns: tiered kits; a store-credit ladder; an experience for first place plus credit; the flagship product plus accessories. Store credit is cheap at high margins but only motivates repeat buyers. Avoid token prizes, and never offer something you could not actually fulfil.
2. **Segment the audience.** In your email platform, segment buyers from roughly the past two years who live where you ship free (take the countries from your shipping settings). Profiles missing a country drop out silently. Use single opt-in, since they already consented. Check the segment size against the thresholds above.
3. **Write the questions using "commit, then explain".** Follow every tick-box question with an open "why?" box. The tick becomes a filter; the explanation becomes the quote. Around 14 questions is right; 20 drags. A strong core set:
   - Where did you first come across us?
   - What one thing would most improve the website? (then explain)
   - What inspired you to buy? (then explain)
   - Who are you shopping for?
   - How do you feel about our pricing? (then explain)
   - Any worries before buying? (then explain)
   - Why did you choose us over other options?
   - What is the main benefit you get from the product?
   - How has it changed things for you?
   - Describe how you found and learned about us.
   - What could be better about the product itself?
   Keep website and product improvements as separate questions, and use single-priority wording ("the *most*") to force a ranking.
4. **Reward effort with entries:** one for a tick-box, two for an explanation, three or four for the hardest question. Tell people their answers are read and that bonus entries go only to real responses.
5. **Build the draw** in a giveaway app: plain prize title, seven-day run, correct time zone, number of winners, single-entry mode. Keep the pre-entry form minimal (name, email, bot check). Use the tool's official-rules template and fill in eligibility, prize values, dates and privacy details.
6. **Host the entry page on your own site**, styled to match the brand. Add the tool's pre-fill parameters to the email button so contact details arrive already completed, and use a shared button block so one edit updates every email. Preview as a real profile to confirm pre-filling, check mobile, register the page URL in the tool, and decide whether to hide the page afterwards.
7. **Capture entrants in a dedicated list** (not a rule-based segment), single opt-in, connected through a narrowly scoped key you revoke afterwards. If you reuse an old promotion, check where its integration sends entries before going live. Only contact details sync; answers are exported separately.

## Example
Imagine a home-goods brand with about 9,000 emailable buyers. At a 6% rate, it expects roughly 500 entries. First prize is a bundle of the brand's own products, followed by four credit prizes. Analysis later shows most buyers chose it for durability, not for design - a fresh angle the ads had missed.

## Common mistakes
- Drawing conclusions from a tiny list and a few dozen replies.
- Surveying buyers of a poorly reviewed product.
- One prize, or a token prize.
- Overlong surveys, and radio questions respondents cannot go back and fix.
- Skipping the integration disconnect on a duplicated promotion.
