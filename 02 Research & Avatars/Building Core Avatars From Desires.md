# Building Core Avatars From Desires

## What it is
A **core avatar** is a single, isolated trait that defines a buying group - nothing else attached. Sub-avatars are built by stacking more traits on top (see [[Building Sub-Avatars From a Core Desire]]). Keeping the core to one trait lets you recombine it freely later.

Avatar traits fall into five categories, roughly in order of buying power:
1. **Desires** - can it be phrased "I want X" or "I need Y"? Use specific surface desires; broad instincts (health, status, belonging) are too wide except for large brands. Keep it to one desire per avatar. Complaints convert easily: "brushing takes ages" becomes a wish for quicker brushing.
2. **Experiences** - situations a person is in or has lived through, either life events (a too-bright bedroom, a new baby) or product history (bought curtains to darken the room, still waking exhausted).
3. **Emotions** - how they feel about it. Break vague secondary feelings ("anxious", "overwhelmed") into primary ones (fear, anger, sadness) to write something recognisable.
4. **Behaviours and habits** - what they regularly do. Very relatable because behaviour ties to self-image, and rarely used by competitors.
5. **Demographics** - static facts. Use them only when the product genuinely differs for a group; otherwise they mostly cut people out.

Beliefs are not a separate category - they are experiences, emotions and behaviours bundled together. Save them for hooks that name a belief directly ("Convinced your metabolism is broken?").

## When to use it
- After the product document and first round of desire hunting.
- When an existing core avatar is exhausted and you need to move to a broader one.

## How (steps)
1. **Start** from the desires you listed (as assumptions) when mapping the product.
2. **Prune hard.** Cut vague items, duplicates, anything too broad and anything this product does not clearly deliver.
3. **Fix mislabels.** "Makes smart investments" describes a behaviour, so split it into a desire (better health) and a behaviour (spends money on health), which suggests a hook such as "For people who treat their health as an investment...". Mark every item with its category.
4. **Score the survivors** on scope (how many people), urgency (how pressing) and staying power (does the desire recur, or is it solved once?).
5. **Match breadth to revenue stage.** Narrow audiences exhaust; broad ones are more competitive and demand a stronger product, offer and brand. As a rough guide:
   - Under ~$100K/month: the narrowest credible desire and a single core, with all effort on the sub-avatars below it.
   - ~$100K-$1M/month: a middle-breadth desire; consider extra cores only when execution is strong, usually nearer the top of this band.
   - $1M+/month: broad desires and several core avatars, backed by strong mechanisms, information and brand.
   Step up a level once the narrow market stops yielding.
6. **Search the free ad library** for your product and category: which desires and avatars competitors scale on, and how sophisticated their messaging is. Include offline rivals, such as installers offering free home visits.
7. **Search forums** for the chosen desire and save phrases, behaviours, product experiences and the **labels** people use for themselves ("light sleeper").
8. **Watch your vocabulary.** Talk about optimising sleep and you pull in data-tracking enthusiasts; talk about recovering from exhaustion and you pull in tired parents. One desire, two crowds.
9. **Check claims and "new information".** Verify anything from a search snippet with a second source. Estimate how many likely buyers have already met the idea; if most have, drop the big-reveal framing. People who have heard the term but cannot explain it form a usable sub-avatar of their own.
10. **Commit to one core avatar** - desire-based for nearly everyone; experience- or behaviour-based once you are more experienced. Out-avatar the biggest competitors by going deeper beneath the same desire rather than chasing the broadest one.

## Example
Imagine a blackout-blinds brand scoring its pruned desires:
- *Sleep in a quiet, dark room* - smallest scope, highest urgency, weakest staying power (once solved, solved). Fits a brand under ~$100K/month.
- *Restorative sleep* - middle scope, moderate urgency, good staying power (there is always deeper sleep). Fits ~$100K-$1M/month.
- *Better health* - huge scope, strongest staying power, most competitive. Fits $1M+/month.

Nursery blinds that darken a room without spoiling the decor would be a narrow, reachable first-stage avatar. A forum user describing a room so dark that evening feels like the dead of night suggests the headline "Turn 9PM into 2AM." A thread on deep sleep reveals people who already tried magnesium - "Magnesium bought you more hours. Now make them deep ones."

## Common mistakes
- Using an AI-generated desire list as-is - it is always generic.
- Picking the biggest desire because it is the biggest.
- Combining several desires into one avatar.
- Switching core avatars after a week instead of exhausting sub-avatars first (give it two to four weeks).
