# Building Sub-Avatars From a Core Desire

## What it is
A sub-avatar is your core avatar plus one or two extra traits from the other categories - an emotion, an experience, a behaviour or a demographic. Sub-avatars exist for one reason: to create angles. A broad desire like "wants restorative sleep" gives you one message; a dozen specific people who share that desire in different situations give you a dozen relatable messages. The more a viewer thinks "that's me", the more they trust you.

The five categories are defined in [[Building Core Avatars From Desires]]. Turning sub-avatars into a formal angle list is covered in [[Deriving Ad Angles From Sub-Avatars]].

## When to use it
- Once you have committed to one desire-based core avatar.
- When your core avatar's ads are working but you need more angles to keep scaling.
- Before giving up on a core avatar - exhaust its sub-avatars first.

## How (steps)
1. **Confirm the core.** It should be a surface desire, broad but narrower than a basic human instinct ("wants restorative sleep", not "wants to be healthy"). The product has to work for most people who want this, and the mechanism has to make sense for it - a sleep device is credible for sleep; daytime sunglasses are not.
2. **Run one plain forum search per category,** adding the forum name to a simple query:
   - *Desire:* "want more restorative sleep"
   - *Emotion:* "how does no sleep make you feel" (plain, but often the richest)
   - *Product experience:* "[product type] for sleep" to reach people who own a similar product or a workaround
   - *Demographic:* "sleep worse as you age", "shift work and sleep", then particular groups
3. **Read the headline, the opening post and the most upvoted answers.** Capture any lone sentence that reveals a behaviour, emotion, experience, demographic fact or label. One sentence can count as several categories - do not agonise over classification; you are collecting words.
4. **Stack a sub-avatar:** the core desire with one or two extra traits (wants restorative sleep + on night shifts + winds down for an hour before bed). Keep at least one desire in every avatar - a habit on its own (skipping food late at night) leaves you with no message until you learn the reason behind it.
5. **Give it a label** people would recognise and you could say in an ad ("night-shifters", "early-to-bed retirees"). Note every label you come across, synonyms included ("graveyard shift").
6. **Find the angle:** ask which part of this person's situation the product fixes. Shift workers have to sleep while the sun is up, so complete darkness is the hook.
7. **Draft hooks.** Give an AI tool the thread, your product context and the sub-avatar, and ask for hooks. Prefer the specific outputs over the generic ones; richer context gives better results.
8. **Flag assumptions** ("older people turn in before it gets dark") and validate before building a campaign on them.
9. **Cap research** at about an hour a session and move to making ads. Treat each sub-avatar as something to test, not something proven.

## Example
Say you sell blackout blinds, core avatar "wants restorative sleep". Your searches produce:
- *Emotion:* someone overwhelmed and irritable after a bad night. Hook: "After one rough night, you're not even yourself" or "Why you overthink everything after a night of tossing and turning."
- *Experience:* keeps waking several times every night without knowing why. Frequency matters - a nightly problem creates stronger desire than an occasional one.
- *Demographic plus behaviour:* people over 60 who only sleep four to six hours and go to bed early. Assumed angle: the bedroom lets in too much light. Call-out: "Over 60 and only getting six hours? Here's a likely reason."
- *Behaviour:* shift workers who sleep through the daytime - an obvious fit for full blackout.
- *Product experience:* people who tried magnesium and still sleep lightly.

Reject non-buyers: someone happily sleeping well with a nightcap or another aid has no reason to buy. Flip it instead - the person who leans on that aid but wishes they didn't is a real prospect.

## Common mistakes
- Using a basic human instinct as the core, so nothing is specific.
- Choosing a desire the product or mechanism cannot credibly deliver.
- Over-stacking traits until the avatar is tiny (a specific wearable *and* a cold-plunge habit *and* red-light therapy).
- Creating desireless avatars from behaviours or demographics alone.
- Clunky labels nobody would say aloud.
- Treating one comment as proof of a big market.
- Gathering more and more avatars without ever testing one.
