# Creative Over Media-Buying Structure

## What it is
Media-buying changes (campaign structure, audience settings, bid strategies, reacting to each algorithm update) typically shift results by perhaps 5-20%. A better ad, offer or landing page can shift them by several times. Meta's delivery now decides who sees your ad mainly from the ad itself, the landing page and the product, so the creative effectively is the targeting. Until you spend very large amounts, your hours are worth far more in making ads than in rearranging campaigns.

## When to use it
- When you are tempted to rebuild the account after a bad week.
- When choosing what to learn next.
- When a new "Meta strategy" headline makes you doubt a structure that works.

## How (steps)
1. **Do the arithmetic on your real budget.** A 15% media-buying gain on $300/day is about $45 more spend. On $2,000/day it is about $300. Only at tens of thousands per day does a structural win become a big number. A new winning ad, by contrast, can double spend and improve ROAS at the same time.
2. **Set your focus by spend.**
   - Under roughly $10,000/day: put nearly all effort into creative, offers and pages.
   - Media-buying strategy deserves deep study at roughly $50k-$100k/day. Many accounts run on a single campaign even at tens of thousands a day.
3. **Understand how Meta targets.** The person you speak to and the images you use decide who gets shown the ad. Take two broad ads, aiming one at women and the other at men, and the audience breakdowns you get will not match. Primary text counts too. No setting guarantees reaching a sub-avatar; the message does.
4. **Commit to a structure that works.** Pick a sensible method, such as one broad campaign with concept tests, and stop changing it. Structural changes rarely double a budget; creative does.
5. **Judge any structure over about 30 days,** not a hot 24 hours on a small budget.
6. **Test in the right order.** For a young brand the efficient path is offers first (highest leverage, cheapest to test), then creative, then landing pages (slowest to build).
7. **Make the default answer "make better ads".** When results fall for no obvious reason, do not spend an hour deciding whether to pause one ad; spend it making a new one.
8. **Spend more time on planning than on pushing buttons.** Most of an ad's quality comes from research and planning before anything is written or edited.

## Why people drift back to hacks
A failed creative test and a failed media-buying trick feel the same: tried it, didn't work. So people return to tricks. The difference is the ceiling. Structure tweaks almost never multiply an account; a strong ad can. Breaking through takes repeated testing until your sense of a good ad matches what actually wins in your market. The goal is to be so good at ads that even an average media buyer could scale the account.

## Example
Say a home-fitness brand spends $400/day in one campaign. Rebuilding into five campaigns with interest audiences would at best add a modest percentage. Instead, it ships one new concept a day for three weeks. One ad takes off; spend climbs to $2,000/day and ROAS improves too, roughly a tenfold gain in profit from a single ad and no structural change.

## Common mistakes
- Moving winners into a different campaign type and expecting a multiple; typical gains are 10-20%.
- Worrying about trust badges or the latest algorithm rumour while ads are weak.
- Spending more hours inside the account than making ads.
- Overlooking strong organic content that could go straight into ads.
