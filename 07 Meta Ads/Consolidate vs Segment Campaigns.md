# Consolidate vs Segment Campaigns

## What it is
Every campaign and ad set you add divides the data Meta learns from. Consolidation keeps purchases flowing into a few places so delivery learns fast; segmentation gives you control and cleaner reporting when you have genuinely different things to sell or markets to serve. The right answer depends mainly on budget and on whether the segments truly differ.

## When to use it
- When you add a second hero product or a new country.
- When an account has grown messy and you are deciding what to merge.
- When a platform rep suggests restructuring.

## How (steps)
1. **Start from budget.**
   - Under about $1,000/day: consolidate. One campaign, few ad sets. Fragmenting starves each piece of learning data.
   - Between about $1,000 and $20,000/day: no firm rule. Split only when there is a clear reason (below).
   - Above about $20-30k/day: segmenting by country, product line or avatar becomes worthwhile, because each segment has enough volume to learn on its own.
2. **Cap the number of ad sets.** A rough ceiling is daily budget divided by target CPA. At $1,000/day and a $50 CPA, about 20 ad sets; at a few hundred a day, about five including the champions set.
3. **Decide on multiple hero products.** If each product already performs on its own, give each its own campaign. If you do not yet know, start them together and split once data shows they behave differently. Factor in AOV gaps: a $40 product and a $200 product in one campaign will be judged on one ROAS target that suits neither.
4. **Split for different objectives, not different ideas.** A different product, a very different price point, or a distinct audience backed by dozens of ad sets' worth of creative can justify a separate campaign. A new angle or format does not; it belongs as a new ad set in the main campaign.
5. **Use a champions ad set inside the consolidated campaign.** Hold proven ads there, keep two or three slots for new tests, move new winners in by duplicating them, and switch off underperforming test ad sets. That makes testing creative the core activity while delivery handles allocation.
6. **Gender splits rarely need separate accounts.** A product line for men and women can usually share one pixel and account, with separate campaigns only for distinctly different audiences; check the gender breakdown to confirm delivery is behaving.
7. **Simplify again at very high spend.** Large accounts often drift into too many campaigns. Around $35k/day and above, stop testing in underperforming campaigns and fold activity back into the ones that work.

## How to treat platform rep advice
Reps often warn about "audience fragmentation" or push consolidation into their newest campaign type. Treat it as a hypothesis: test the change against your current setup over a few weeks before restructuring. Their advice is not tailored to your margin or creative pipeline.

## Example
Say a fitness-accessories brand spends $4,000 a day on one campaign selling resistance bands and a $180 adjustable bench. The bench's ROAS target needs to be lower than the bands' because of its higher margin per order, yet both share one target. The brand moves the bench into its own campaign with its own KPI, keeps the bands consolidated with a champions ad set, and leaves country targeting combined because no single market yet spends more than a few hundred dollars a day.

## Common mistakes
- Building a campaign per angle, per format or per creator at a few hundred dollars a day.
- Copying a $50k/day account's segmentation while spending $2k.
- Grouping products with very different AOVs under one ROAS target.
- Restructuring because of a rep call rather than evidence.
- Stacking new campaigns on old ones, then blaming the auction when results wobble.
