# Simplifying Meta Ads Structure Framework

Framework by Josh Omosebi, Seba Agency.

## The idea in one line

Most Meta advertisers are not short on tactics. What they are missing is a lean structure, one clear message for one buyer, and enough variations of that message. This framework gets the right person to click; the product page then has to close the sale (see [[Product Page Essentials Framework]]).

## The problem: noise

New formats, new features, playbooks lifted from big brands and structure hacks arrive constantly. Following all of it leaves you wondering what to test next, while the handful of actions that drive results go undone.

**The filter:** before you take on any tactic, put one question to it: does it make you better at speaking to your buyer? When the answer is no, skip it.

## Pillar 1: Structure (consolidate)

A small account should be consolidated. A single campaign is enough.

```
1 CAMPAIGN  (CBO)
├── Ad set A: concept A, ads 3–5
├── Ad set B: concept B, ads 3–5
└── Ad set C: concept C, ads 3–5
```

| Level | Rule |
|---|---|
| Campaign | Just one, using campaign budget optimisation (CBO) |
| Ad sets | A few, and none left empty |
| Ads | Each ad set holds a single concept with 3 to 5 versions |

**Why it works:** spreading a modest budget over many campaigns also spreads out the learning; CBO moves spend toward whichever ad set is performing; and with one concept per ad set, each one poses a single clear test question.

**Applying it:** what counts as "small" depends on your spend. A concept is one angle, such as a pain point, a desire or a use case, while the ads under it change the hook, format or creator. When a concept wins, produce more variants of that concept. When it flops, bring in a fresh concept rather than layering on extra campaigns or ad sets.

## Pillar 2: Message (talk to the customer)

Above everything else in the early days, your priority is figuring out how to address your buyer: the right image, pointed directly at their problem. That stays the job at every stage of revenue, and ads that achieve it also push costs down (see [[Meta Ad Metrics Framework - Part 1]]).

**The customer-message test,** run before launch:
1. Name the one customer this ad is for.
2. Name the problem of theirs it calls out.
3. Check whether the visual shows that problem or the result at a glance.
4. Ask whether that person would sense the ad was built with them in mind.

## Pillar 3: Scale the message (breadth and depth)

After a single message works, extend it sideways and downward.

| Axis | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Breadth | How many separate routes lead to the same buyer? | Fresh concepts, angles, formats |
| Depth | What does the buyer need to hear as they move toward purchase? | Messages covering first contact through to ready-to-buy |

**Map it to the funnel:** an ad's frequency hints at its position (about 1 to 1.3 means top of funnel, 2 to 4+ means bottom). Lay out concepts on one axis and journey stages on the other; each blank square marks an ad still to be made.

## The advanced layer (and when you've earned it)

Awareness levels and market sophistication are useful frameworks, but they belong later. The order is: simple structure, one clear message, many messages, and only then the advanced frameworks. Avoid adding complexity before that point.

## The mantra

| | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Run more ads | Higher volume of concepts and versions, per Pillar 3 |
| Run better ads | Addressing the customer directly, per Pillar 2 |
| Keep it simple | A consolidated setup free of noise, per Pillar 1 |
| Use your brain | Judgement beats borrowed tactics |

## Where this appears in the numbers

| Idea | Signal |
|---|---|
| Speaking to the customer | Stronger quality and engagement rankings plus cheaper CPMs |
| Reaching the right customer | A hook rate that screens viewers as well as attracting them |
| Message fits the problem | Hold rate once the hook is over |
| Ad and page aligned | No mismatch of high-quality ranking with low-conversion ranking |
| Fewer campaigns, concepts grouped | Tidier data, quicker learning |

## Ad account audit checklist

- [ ] One CBO campaign when the account is small
- [ ] A few ad sets, each with ads in it
- [ ] A single concept per ad set, with 3 to 5 ads
- [ ] Each ad clears the customer-message test
- [ ] Concepts cover multiple angles
- [ ] Messages cover multiple journey stages
- [ ] Nothing changed purely to copy big brands
- [ ] Awareness and sophistication work waits until the basics perform
