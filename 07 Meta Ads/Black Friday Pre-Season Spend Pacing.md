# Black Friday Pre-Season Spend Pacing

## What it is
A big sale collapses months of demand into a few days: everyone who knew you but had not bought converts at once, and then sales slump. The people who buy on the peak were mostly reached earlier. In November conversion rates are high but reach is expensive, so you largely harvest audiences you built in October and early November. The trap is that platform reporting undercounts that earlier spend, because its sales arrive one to two weeks later, and makes the sale weekend look better than it is, so teams pull money out of the pre-season and pile it into the peak, where the next dollar is often least useful.

## When to use it
- When setting September-December budgets for a brand with meaningful Q4 demand.
- When leadership wants to "save budget for Black Friday".
- During the peak, to decide whether more spend is still worth it.

## How (steps)
1. **Last year's peak is not a reliable baseline.** The auction shifts every year; when ad inventory tightens, expect higher CPMs and fewer impressions.
2. **Measure with lag in mind.** Throughout the period, report on a 7-day click window (optionally 7-day click combined with 1-day engaged view). Compare last year's pre-peak ROAS under 1-day and 7-day click and record the gap as a premium you apply mentally to today's early numbers. In November the gap between short and long windows typically widens a lot, so real-time ROAS looks worse than it will once delayed sales arrive.
3. **Read the attribution ladder in reverse pre-peak.** Ordered from most generous to strictest: the platform default, then 7-day click, then incremental attribution, then new-customer revenue over ad spend in your P&L. Before the peak, true returns are higher than shown.
4. **Write a pacing document.** Lay out a base case and a stretch case, each with agreed efficiency thresholds that unlock more spend, so the team can act on a Saturday morning without chasing sign-off. Treat the budget as a ceiling released only while returns stay incremental, never as a target to spend.
5. **Fund the pre-season.** For a typical portfolio, November spend runs well above October, and a solid share of the September-December budget should sit in September and October.
6. **Keep evergreen on.** Switching evergreen off is the single biggest mistake; those campaigns carry conversion history. They should still hold roughly half or more of spend in the period.
7. **Write scaling rules in advance,** read on three-day rolling marginal averages: marginal contribution improving, scale; flat, hold; two poor reads in a row, cut.
8. **Approve increases on marginal returns, not blended ones.** Adding 25% more spend can leave blended CAC acceptable while the added dollars cost several times more per new customer. Contribution peaks where marginal ROAS falls to break-even (1 / gross margin). Once the ratio between 1-day and 7-day click revenue stops moving, extra spend is harvesting existing demand rather than creating new demand.
9. **Plan creative volume.** Look up the most a single ad spent in one day during last year's peak. Divide this year's target peak daily spend by a realistic per-ad figure; the answer is how many ads must each carry serious spend. Count distinct concepts, not visual variants. Peak spend is spread wide; winners hit ceilings and the long tail absorbs much of the budget.
10. **Phase creative:** teasers or early access first, then launch, a refresh midway through, and deadline messaging for the last day or two. Set up additional pages if a large ramp could hit per-page ad limits.
11. **Prepare fallback offers in advance** (a free gift, free shipping, a bundle, a deeper sitewide discount) with ads and pages already built, so missing a target only requires switching them on.
12. **Give November its own CAC limit.** Many brands' November cohorts repeat noticeably less than other months; check your own cohorts.

## Example
Say a fashion brand's real-time ROAS in mid-November reads 1.1x and the team wants to cut. Last year the same week read 1.0x live and settled near 3x once delayed sales were credited. The pacing document calls for holding, so the brand keeps funding prospecting, and its peak weekend beats plan.

## Common mistakes
- Treating pre-sale budget restraint as "saving dry powder".
- Approving peak scaling on blended ROAS.
- Ranking creative by ROAS mid-sale, when discounted bestsellers win no matter the ad; use CTR and CPC instead, read at ad-set level.
- Catalog ads that never mention the sale.
