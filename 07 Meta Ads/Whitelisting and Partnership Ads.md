# Whitelisting and Partnership Ads

## What it is
Normally your ads run from your brand page. Two formats let them run from a creator instead:
- **Whitelisting:** the creator's profile is chosen as the identity, so the ad appears as the creator's own post.
- **Partnership ad:** the partnership toggle is switched on with both your page and the creator selected, so both names appear.
Both make an ad read like a recommendation rather than a brand pitch. Neither fixes a broken account or finds new winners; they are a performance nudge on creative that already works.

## When to use it
- A creator already has winning ads with you.
- You want a little more performance from a proven creator ad.
- You are setting up new creator deals and can ask for access at the start.

## How (steps)
1. **Pick the format.** Partnership ads have recently tended to perform at least as well as plain whitelisting in many accounts, but results vary with platform changes, so test both in your own account. If offered, leave the identity setting on dynamic so the platform chooses which identity leads.
2. **Ask for access the easy way.** For new creators, request partnership access for a set period (30 days is common) at briefing, explaining it raises the chance their ad performs and leads to a longer deal. Show them a screenshot of what the ad will look like. For existing creators, raise it after a strong first month, when they have been paid and are happy.
3. **Use a request-link tool if you work with many creators.** Asking creators to share access to your business ID manually fails because most do not know how. Access-request tools turn it into a few clicks for the creator. Many Instagram-first creators need a Facebook page first; send a short how-to.
4. **Select their identity in the ad.** After access comes through, the creator's Facebook page and Instagram account appear in the identity section when you build an ad.
5. **Let creators write their own copy** in their own voice, using your best-performing copy as a reference.
6. **Place it by spend.**
   - Below roughly $2k/day: place creator-identity ads directly in your main campaign; segmenting costs too much data.
   - Above roughly $2k/day: a separate creator-identity campaign becomes an option. The reason is risk, not performance: it prevents one creator who can revoke access from becoming your whole spend engine. Merge back once creators are proven.
   - Prefer campaign-level budgets here too.
7. **Plan for expiry.** Re-request access before a time-limited grant ends if the ad is still spending.

## Operating rules
- Use a creator's identity only for ads featuring that creator. Running your other creative from their handle without explicit written agreement is unacceptable.
- Tell creators up front that comments, follows and profile visits will land on their page.
- Creators may pull access if the ads look questionable.
- Budget a modest monthly fee for access in addition to what you pay per video, often in the low hundreds of dollars per creator. Walk away from sudden demands of several times the going rate unless their content is truly outstanding.
- Use a written agreement with a non-compete clause covering directly competing products; take legal advice rather than relying on a template.
- Never let your single biggest spender be an ad you cannot control.

## Example
Say a pet-grooming brand has a creator whose demo video has been a top-three spender for a month. It asks for 30-day partnership access, duplicates the same video as a partnership ad into the main campaign, and over two weeks the partnership version takes more spend at a slightly better return. The brand extends access in a paid agreement and asks the creator to film two more videos.

## Common mistakes
- Whitelisting creators with no proven ads.
- Pushing lots of ads through a creator who guards their profile closely.
- Depending on one creator's access for most of your spend.
- Building a separate creator campaign while spending under $100/day.
