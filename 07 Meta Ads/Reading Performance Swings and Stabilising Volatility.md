# Reading Performance Swings and Stabilising Volatility

## What it is
Day-to-day ROAS on Meta moves a lot even when nothing is wrong. Four things move it: how buyers feel (they are humans, not data points), the market (news, competitors, a better offer elsewhere), platform randomness (CPMs can jump overnight), and your own strategy. Only the last is yours to control. The aim is to stop overreacting to the first three while building a strategy that is less exposed to them.

## When to use it
- After a bad day or two, before you change anything.
- When the account feels like a rollercoaster week to week.
- Around holidays, elections and late summer, when demand shifts.

## How (steps)
**Reading a swing**
1. **Use three-day averages,** not single days. Conversion-rate wobble is normal; reacting to it with constant setting changes is the actual mistake.
2. **Remember early allocation is exploration.** In a new test, spend jumps between ads as delivery samples them. A shift on day two is not a verdict.
3. **Check market-wide causes first.** If CPMs rose or everything dipped at once, look for seasonal or market events before touching creative. Dips in late summer are mostly seasonal: families spend on school supplies and people are tired after the holidays. Buying intent drops in the days around Christmas and usually recovers from the day after. Elections can depress results; an ad that wins in a weak auction can keep performing once it passes.
4. **Decide whether to spend through a dip.** It is a per-brand data decision. Sometimes protecting profit means a sharp temporary cut instead of holding a revenue figure.
5. **Check view-through share.** Volatility is often inflated by view-through attribution.
6. **Look upstream if instability persists:** funnel, offer and customer journey, not ad settings.

**Stabilising a volatile account**
1. **Move off the most competitive messaging.** Living only on product-aware and most-aware ads in a saturated market leaves you fighting every competitor for the same small pool.
2. **Anchor on permanent desires.** Trending desires (news cycles, holidays) vanish. Broad, lasting ones such as health, wealth and relationships do not. Ask how your product improves one of them and build a bridge from the person to the product.
3. **Move up-market in awareness.** Problem-aware and unaware ads reach far more people and keep working off-season. Use education (be an authority on the problem, written very simply) or entertainment (avoid boredom; length is not the issue), carried by story.
4. **Send them to a congruent page.** Problem-aware clickers have not seen the product yet; route them to an advertorial or listicle, not a bare product page. Test pages as new ads in the campaign.
5. **Strengthen the offer.** Strong risk reversal (backed by real proof), honest scarcity, bundles, subscription perks or free gifts can lift results quickly, provided they are believable.
6. **Increase learning time.** Review every winner and one loser per batch each week.

## Example
Say a hypothetical air-purifier brand swings between 1.2x and 3.5x daily. Its ads are all "best air purifier" product-aware ads. It adds a problem-aware angle ("waking up congested? It may be your air, not your mattress") sent to an advertorial, and an unaware angle about what floats in indoor air. Over a month, three-day averages tighten, and the unaware ad keeps selling in spring when the product-aware ads fade.

## Common mistakes
- Reacting to one day.
- Changing creative when the cause was a market-wide CPM spike.
- Blaming the platform rather than strategy.
- Assuming social proof on old ads beats fresh creative; newer ads regularly outperform old ones with many likes.
