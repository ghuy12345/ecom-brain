# Learnings From Losing Ads

## What it is
Most ads lose. The difference between accounts that improve and accounts that stall is whether losses turn into better next guesses. A learning is an educated guess, not a fact; it becomes closer to a fact only when a new ad built on it wins. Skill here shows up as reviews that regularly turn into winning ads.

## When to use it
- After each test window (about seven days).
- In a weekly review session, alongside winners.
- Not on ads with almost no spend: a variant with $10 of spend tells you nothing, only a question of why customers did not care.

## How (steps)
1. **Choose the dates the ad was live,** sort by spend, and break the concept's ad set down by day to watch how spend built up.
2. **Note the metrics, most important first:**
   - Spend, ROAS or CPA, and the ad's share of all account spend.
   - Hook rate, hold rate, delivery efficiency and average play time (formulas in [[Ads Manager Setup - Columns, Benchmarks, Naming and UTMs]]).
   - Conversion rate and AOV for finer detail.
   - Frequency: if it is high while spend is low, delivery could only find warm people.
3. **Use percent-of-spend benchmarks over seven days:** about 2% means no real data; about 10% is the floor for something worth studying; 30-50% at KPI is probably a winner. For an account spending $1,000 a week, those are roughly $20, $100 and $300-500.
4. **Localise the failure:**
   - No spend: the verdict is in. Do not force it.
   - Weak hook rate: the opening seconds failed. Write the replacement hook in the exact on-screen words and re-cut the same day.
   - Strong hook, weak hold: the bridge or payoff failed.
   - Clicks but no sales: look at whether the message matches, then at the page.
5. **Ask whether the customer even cares.** The most common disqualifier is a premise the team found interesting but the customer finds boring. Make hooks about the person, and add a bridge line on why they should care.
6. **Look at the variant that got the spend.** Preview all variants side by side and ask what sets apart the variant that got the spend: contrast, an oddly specific number, a power word like "easy", naming the mechanism. Treat it as direction. The top variant inside a losing concept remains a loser.
7. **Compare against known winning patterns:** a clear call-out of who it is for, borrowed credibility, wordplay, a specific pain phrase. Does your ad do any of these?
8. **Check congruence:** the image should back the claim, and the product should carry the same name on the ad and the page.
9. **Finish with an action plan:** what changes next time? "Stop doing X" or "do nothing, keep direction" are valid outcomes.
10. **Tag re-cuts as iterations** so you can compare them with the original.
11. **Time-box it:** roughly a quarter of an hour per loser and half an hour per winner. Record everything in your roadmap.

## Example
Say a weight-loss supplement tests four statics and all of them lose, but one took most of the little spend there was. It paired two ideas that normally clash, an exact calorie count with the word "easy", used an oddly specific number and was the only one naming the mechanism. Yet it took only around 2% of spend, which makes it a direction, not a winner. The action plan: rebuild it as a video that explains the mechanism, since the page did not.

## Common mistakes
- Broad first-draft learnings ("the image is bad") when the real cause is messaging or failing to explain the product.
- Treating a guess as a rule for every future ad.
- Autopsying every low-signal loser instead of parking it.
- Discarding paid-for footage instead of trying new hooks first.
- Confusing liking an ad with it working.
