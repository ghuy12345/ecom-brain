# Feedback Loops - Learning Why an Ad Worked

## What it is
A feedback loop takes what a system produced and uses it to adjust what goes in next time. Repeating the same work for years without such loops brings little progress; each completed, well-run loop speeds you up. Winners are frequently unexpected, so treat each one as something to explain afterwards, then test whether the explanation holds.

## When to use it
- Weekly, on winners and on a sample of losers.
- Whenever an ad unexpectedly breaks out.
- When you seem to be working hard without improving.

## How (steps)
1. **Write it down.** With a bad meal you learn automatically because the pain is sharp; with ads the pain is mild, so you must record the loop. Keep one document with a tab per loop.
2. **List every reason it might have worked** (or failed). Order them by how confident you are.
3. **Break the ad down** into its working parts: avatar; positioning (the frame it puts around the product for that person); mechanism; credibility and authority; curiosity (what holds attention); story and emotional zone. Keep positioning separate from avatar; ask "why would someone care?" every time.
4. **Separate format, style and message.** Formats (static, headline image, long video, voiceover) carry no bias. Styles (us versus them, native, podcast) do. The message, what is actually said and communicated, is usually why it worked.
5. **Reject surface learnings.** "The presenter is older", "a dog appears", "it's UGC" are not learnings; if those details were the cause, every ad containing them would win. Look for the emotional and positional reason.
6. **Isolate elements.** Form hypotheses (the creator, the setting, the message, the format), then pull out single elements and test them to see which one carries the result.
7. **Re-execute the constant.** Once you know the desire, avatar and message that won, express that same constant in other formats: image, voiceover, creator video.
8. **List action items and execute them.** A loop is only complete when you test what you learned.
9. **Confirm by winning again.** If you build an ad from a learning and it wins, the learning is confirmed and your focus shifts to execution. If it fails, you probably executed poorly, or the learning was wrong.
10. **Stay in the system.** The critical error is finishing a loop and then switching systems: a failed creative test is not a reason to jump to media-buying tweaks.
11. **Loop on more than ads:** offers, pages and campaigns too.
12. **Keep the entries tidy** so an AI assistant can later scan them for patterns.

## What tends not to work alone
Adjusting media-buying structure, studying competitors and nothing else, producing ads without knowing the customer, or cloning ad templates have seldom driven consistent results on their own. Beginners can copy a proven structure first, but the learning comes from asking why it works.

## Example
Say a pet-grooming brand's top ad opens on an odd close-up of matted fur. The team's first note, "UGC product-aware ad", is a surface label. The loop lists: curiosity from an unfamiliar visual; a mechanism so visible it needs no explanation; the owner's embarrassment at the groomer. Testing two new ads that keep the embarrassment angle but change the visual, both earn spend; one that keeps the visual but drops the embarrassment does not. The learning, owner embarrassment, is confirmed.

## Common mistakes
- Analysing only winners, so you never learn what to stop.
- Looping through research, creation and testing without real review.
- Writing learnings as formats rather than reasons.
- Treating one successful change as proof it was the only factor.
- Skipping the execution step.
