# Creative Roadmap and Ad Log

## What it is
Three linked records keep a creative operation honest:
- **Ad roadmap:** the backlog and status of every ad idea, from first thought to written learnings.
- **Ad log:** a dated list of each modification you make inside the ad account.
- **Concept map:** a visual board of what you have already tested, so gaps and repeats are obvious.
Together they let you compute a real hit rate, trace performance drops back to their cause and stop re-testing the same idea under a new name.

## When to use it
- From the first week of running ads; it costs almost nothing to start.
- Essential once more than one person touches creative or the account.
- Whenever you feel short of ideas.

## How (steps)
1. **Set up the roadmap columns:** status; date added; batch number; author; desire or core avatar; sub-avatar (optional); angle(s) (one for a depth test, several for a breadth test); concept; a short memo on why it should work; awareness level; file type (static or video); idea source; brief link; live ad link; result tier; learnings.
2. **Write the memo before briefing.** Three lines: what you are leading with (a benefit or idea, not a feature list); why you are making it (a research finding, an earlier winner, or a combination of two winners); how it will be made quickly. Start with what and why.
3. **Tag the idea source.** Iteration (from your own ads), imitation (from others' ads) or ideation (from your own research). Using outside inspiration for execution does not make an ideation an imitation.
4. **Use simple statuses:** working (building), filming (with a creator), learning (launched), done. An ad is only "done" once its learnings are written. This keeps finished-looking ads on your radar until the insight is extracted.
5. **Classify every result** as breakthrough, spend winner, KPI winner or loser so the sheet calculates hit rate.
6. **Keep the active list short.** Keep just the top four or five ideas in the roadmap; dump everything else in a separate idea document.
7. **Add the next test as a new row** straight after the learnings, for example a single-angle follow-up when one angle took the spend.
8. **Keep a page roadmap too.** Pages ship far less often than ads (perhaps one or two a week against 15-20 ads). Plan each page against the specific ad that will send it traffic.
9. **Keep the ad log daily.** Every budget change, ad switched off, new launch, page swap or offer change, with the date. When the account dips, you can trace it back. A common pattern: a high-spend ad is switched off after two bad days, the whole account slides for weeks, and turning it back on restores performance.
10. **Map concepts visually** on a whiteboard or design tool, grouped by desire and angle. You will see which desires have never been tested and which angle you have run five times.
11. **Use team upvotes** before strategy meetings once the team grows past two or three members, and filter by the highest-voted ideas.
12. **Put the batch number in ad set and ad names** so reports stay clean.

## Example
Say a sleep-supplement brand's roadmap filtered to "breakthrough" shows three winners, all on the angle of night-shift workers who cannot switch off. The concept map shows no tests yet on parents of newborns, a sub-avatar with the same desire. The next batch is a multi-angle test aimed at new parents, and its memo cites forum threads about sleeping in short blocks.

## Common mistakes
- Marking ads done without written learnings.
- A roadmap so long nobody reads it.
- No change log, so drops cannot be explained.
- Learnings that restate results ("video got spend") instead of why the customer cared.
- Planning pages in isolation from the ads that feed them.
