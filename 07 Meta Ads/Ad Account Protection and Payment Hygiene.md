# Ad Account Protection and Payment Hygiene

## What it is
An ad account that stops delivering for a day during a big week can cost more than a month of optimisation gains. Restrictions, failed payments, hacks and simple human error (a budget typed with one zero too many, a wrong link pasted into an ad) are ordinary risks once you spend seriously. Protection means a short list of boring safeguards set up in advance: split your assets so one ban cannot take everything, verify the business, keep payments healthy, buy access to support, add automated rules that catch disasters, and have a backup ready before you depend on the account most.

## When to use it
- As soon as the account spends consistently every day.
- Before any peak season, especially Q4.
- After a scare: a payment failure, a restricted page, an unexplained spend spike.

## How (steps)
1. **Split assets across three Business Managers.** Put every pixel in one Business Manager, every ad account in a second, and run campaigns from a third "holding" Business Manager that the pixels and ad accounts are shared into. Make the same real people (you plus one or two trusted admins) admins on all three, and keep the Facebook page at profile level rather than inside a Business Manager. If one Business Manager is banned, the pixels and other assets in the others survive.
2. **Verify your business with Meta.** Completing business verification in the business settings ties the assets to a real registered company and reduces the chance of pages and accounts being restricted without recourse. If the Security Center shows no "Start verification" button, a known workaround is to make it appear: in Business Settings, add a new app, choose the Instant Games type, fill in the basic fields (any name, tagline, description and category, a 1024 x 1024 icon), add your own store's privacy-policy page in the basic settings and save. The verification option then shows up in both the app and the Security Center. Only remove the placeholder app after the business shows as verified; removing it earlier breaks the process. This depends on how Meta's tools currently behave, so check for the native button first.
3. **Consider paid platform verification as support insurance.** Meta's paid verification subscription costs a modest monthly fee and mainly buys faster, human support and some account protection. Treat it like insurance, not a performance lever.
4. **Keep payments clean.** A declined or lapsed payment quietly throttles delivery, and repeated failures can get the account restricted mid-scale. Keep a primary and a backup payment method on file at business level, watch card limits, and raise them before a season where spend will multiply. If a card limit is capping spend, move to a high-limit business card or a post-pay arrangement.
5. **Know the agency ad account option.** Some advertisers run through ad accounts provided by an official Meta agency partner, which can offer more stable billing and support. These typically cost a small percentage of spend, often in the 3-7% range. Weigh that fee against your margin.
6. **Add two catastrophe rules.** In Ads Manager automated rules, create (a) a rule that pauses ads whose spend jumps several times above normal within 24 hours, and (b) a rule or regular check that turns off any ad whose destination is not your own domain. These catch hacks and fat-finger errors before they drain the account.
7. **Prepare a backup before peak season.** Going into Q4, have a second ad account in good standing with the pixel and payment connected. Spend a little through it in advance so it is not brand new on the day you need it. If an account is restricted, appeal through official channels while the backup keeps you running.
8. **Protect your brand assets.** File a trademark early and enrol in Meta's brand rights programme so false IP reports are harder to file against you and copycats can be removed.
9. **Chase refunds through support persistently.** Refunds for platform-caused overspend are possible but slow; delegate the follow-ups.

## Example
Say a pet-supplement brand spends $3,000 a day and plans to triple spend over Black Friday. In September it completes business verification, subscribes to paid verification for support, raises its card limit and adds a second card, and sets up a rule that pauses any ad set whose 24-hour spend is five times its usual level. It also opens a second ad account inside its verified business, connects the pixel and runs a small evergreen campaign there. When a team member later pastes a staging URL into a new ad, the destination check catches it within the hour.

## Common mistakes
- Treating a failed payment as an admin nuisance rather than a delivery problem.
- Discovering card limits on the biggest sale day.
- Having no human support route when something breaks.
- Relying on an account you do not own and cannot recover.
- Keeping pixels, ad accounts and campaigns in one Business Manager, so a single ban takes everything.
- Removing the placeholder verification app before the business shows as verified.
