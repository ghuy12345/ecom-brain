# Beyond Meta - TikTok and Native Networks

## What it is
Most ecommerce brands get the bulk of their paid revenue from Meta. Two common next channels are TikTok, where short creator-style video is native, and native ad networks, which place ads styled as editorial content across publisher sites. Both can add scale, but each has its own learning curve, and time spent learning a new channel is time not spent improving your main one. Google is covered separately in [[Google Ads Account Structure for Ecommerce]].

## When to use it
- Your main channel is stable and well run, and you have spare team capacity.
- You already have the skill for the new channel, or are prepared to fund a real learning period.
- Your product suits the channel: short-video-friendly products on TikTok, education-heavy or compliance-sensitive niches (health is the classic case) on native networks.
- Not as an escape from a struggling Meta account; if you lack skill on the new platform, the learning time usually goes further on Meta.

## How (steps)
**TikTok**
1. Use spark ads, which run organic-style posts from your account or a creator's, inside a single performance campaign.
2. Load roughly 30 ads into that one campaign rather than splitting into many small ones.
3. When a campaign works, duplicate it rather than rebuilding.
4. Prefer creative quality over complex structure, as on Meta. The same principles of hook, message and avatar apply.
5. Reuse your Meta winners, re-edited to the platform's native pacing, before writing new concepts.
6. Use vertical 9:16 video throughout.

**Native ad networks**
1. Budget for higher costs than Meta and for a substantial spend before the network finds profitable placements.
2. Expect a patient optimisation period: you prune and favour placements over weeks, not days.
3. Write native ads well. The format rewards copy that reads like a genuine article, and the landing experience usually needs a pre-sell page (advertorial or long-form story) rather than a product page.
4. Keep claims compliant and honest. Fabricated personas and copied templates may produce short bursts but are ethically shaky, risk the brand and fall apart over time; learn the format properly instead.
5. For other off-platform networks that use end cards, treat the end card as the most important element and reuse your best static ad as it.

## Choosing whether to expand
- **Expand** when Meta is consistent, your creative engine produces more than Meta can absorb, and the new channel suits the product.
- **Wait** when Meta is still volatile, when your team has no experience on the new platform, or when your budget would only allow a small test. A small native test budget is the classic way to conclude "native doesn't work" without ever reaching the learning threshold.

## Example
Say a joint-supplement brand spends $5,000/day on Meta profitably and its claims are hard to run on social platforms. It allocates a fixed learning budget to a native network for six weeks, sends traffic to a long-form advertorial adapted from its best Meta pre-sell page, and prunes placements weekly. Separately, its video team re-edits five Meta winners into spark ads for a single TikTok campaign. TikTok finds a profitable ad in three weeks; native takes the full six before placements settle.

## Common mistakes
- Testing native networks on a tiny budget and quitting early.
- Splitting a new TikTok account into many small campaigns.
- Expanding channels while the main channel is unstable.
- Copying another channel's native ad formula instead of learning to write the format.
