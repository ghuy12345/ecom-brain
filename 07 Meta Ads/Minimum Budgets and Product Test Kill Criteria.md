# Minimum Budgets and Product Test Kill Criteria

## What it is
Two early decisions shape every new brand's first months on Meta: how much to spend per day at a minimum, and when to give up on a product or ad. Spend too little and delivery never gets enough purchases to learn. Kill too early and you abandon products that one better ad would have rescued. Kill too late and you burn cash on something the market does not want.

## When to use it
- Launching a new store or brand.
- Testing a new product in an existing store.
- Setting the floor in your scaling decision tree.

## How (steps)
1. **Set a minimum daily spend.** This is a daily figure you can afford to spend whatever happens, based on cash and reserves. Common range: $50-100/day, with $100/day a sensible start because it gives delivery enough data. Above about $500/day the question mostly answers itself.
2. **Fund at least one to two months of testing.** At $100/day that is about $3,000-6,000 before you can expect to learn much. If you cannot fund that, you are not ready to test.
3. **Use the normal structure.** Product tests run in the same broad, single campaign as creative tests; they need no special campaign type.
4. **Check what your budget can support.** At a $25 target CPA, $50/day takes roughly three days to give you even one likely purchase. $100-150/day lets you test two or three concepts at once.
5. **Judge on spend and ROAS only.** Good CTR, CPC or add-to-cart costs cannot save a product or ad. A high-CTR ad that barely spends is still a weak ad.
6. **Use break-even as the pass mark.** If a product reaches roughly break-even during the test window, it is worth investing in better ads, pages and offers to scale it.
7. **Choose the test length by experience:**
   - Complete beginner: about three days per product; without traction, try the next product. Volume of medium-effort tests teaches you faster than a few "perfect" launches.
   - Experienced operator building a brand: one to two weeks per product, continually adding new ads, since a single winning ad can rescue a product. What matters is how fast you find that ad, not whether the first ad worked.
8. **Do not turn ads off before the test window ends,** except one case: in a brand-new account with zero data, an ad that has spent about eight times your target CPA with no purchase can be stopped.
9. **Know when to pivot a product.** Pivot if the product is a novelty item or search trends show its market shrinking. Otherwise, exhaust the options on the current product first; a struggling second product drains focus.

## The minimum-spend caveat
Forcing minimum spend onto individual test ad sets can speed up validation, but it often buys attributed rather than incremental sales: delivery finds people likely to buy regardless so it can spend the money. Many minimum-spend ad sets also make it hard to reduce budgets later. Use per-ad-set minimums sparingly, as a deliberate experiment, not the default.

## Example
Say a beginner launches a posture product with $100/day and a $30 target CPA. After three days it has spent $300 with one sale and a 2.1% CTR. Under the beginner rule they move on. An experienced operator with the same product instead keeps $100/day running for two weeks, launches a new concept every other day, and on day nine one ad reaches break-even, enough to justify building a landing page for it.

## Common mistakes
- Starting with less budget than one test cycle needs.
- Keeping a product alive on CTR or add-to-cart numbers.
- Killing ads on day one in an account with history.
- Perfecting one launch for weeks instead of testing.
- Setting minimum spend on every ad set.
