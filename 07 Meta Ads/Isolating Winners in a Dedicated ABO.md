# Isolating Winners in a Dedicated ABO

## What it is
The default starting structure is one broad campaign with campaign budget (see [[One Broad Campaign - Spend as the Signal]]). Within it, one strong newcomer can take most of the money and starve older ads that were still profitable. Isolation means duplicating proven ads into a separate campaign where each ad sits alone in its own ad set with its own fixed budget (ABO, ad-set budget optimisation). No new launch can take that budget away, and an isolated ad that collapses takes nothing else down with it. It does not fix weak ads; it protects proven ones and gives you flexibility to scale them. Whether you need it yet depends on your starting point and your KPIs.

## When to use it
- Only once the account spends roughly $1-2k/day or more and you find winners regularly.
- When a new winner keeps pushing earlier winners out of the champions ad set.
- When you want to test an attribution or exclusion setting on proven ads without touching the main campaign.
- Not for ads that never earned spend; leave those running where they are rather than forcing budget onto them.

## How (steps)
1. **Wait for a clear winner.** Typically about seven days live, taking a large share of its campaign's budget (often 30-50% or more) while hitting KPI.
2. **Duplicate, do not move.** Duplicate the ad into its own ad set in the ABO campaign, named after the ad. Keep the original live in the main campaign. As the isolated copy grows, the original may get less spend there; that is just overlap.
3. **Preserve social proof.** A plain ad-level duplicate with nothing edited carries over Facebook and Instagram likes and comments, reportedly about 90% of the time; when it fails, fall back to a post ID. A post ID keeps Facebook engagement but loses Instagram's, so if Instagram engagement matters, duplicate without changes. If you must use a post ID, first confirm via the text and headline breakdown which copy combination is winning, because a post ID locks in a single combination.
4. **Set the starting budget.** See the choice below. Turn off any setting that shares budget across ad sets.
5. **Treat three winner types differently.**
   - Breakthrough (meets KPI while taking most of the spend): start toward the top of the total-spend range.
   - Spend winner (takes spend but misses KPI): throttle it to a small fixed budget and work on its conversion.
   - KPI winner (meets the KPI yet receives little spend): start at the low end of the total-spend range, then check whether it can take on more.
6. **Scale on consistency.** After two good days, double; after another good day, double again. Smaller isolated ad sets can move faster than the main campaign.
7. **Optional attribution test ad sets.** Load all top ads into extra ad sets that each test one setting against the main campaign's 7-day click: hard exclusions of site visitors, engagers and past customers so only new people are reached; 1-day click; or incremental attribution. Judge by blended revenue in a third-party tool, not platform ROAS.
8. **Use it to quarantine problem ads too.** If a former top spender stays below KPI for a couple of weeks (not just a week-long dip), reduce its budget before cutting it: pause it in the main campaign and run it in ABO at a lower, controlled budget. Should it recover, copy it back into the main campaign.

## Choosing the starting budget
Base the starting budget on a percentage of **total** daily account spend, not on the ad's own spend: for example somewhere in the 5-20% range, at the lower end when one winner is much bigger than the rest. Pick a starting number, write it in your notes, then keep growing it as the ad holds KPI. Starting low and doubling on good days is safer than starting high.

## Where this fits
Start with one broad CBO; it is the most efficient structure, and at the ad set level you separate by angle or avatar. Add the ABO once you regularly find winners at $1-2k/day or more. Isolation tends to give more predictable, horizontal scaling; the CBO keeps data consolidated and avoids paying for overlap. Stay consolidated when you cannot check incrementality, since isolated sets can show more spend at lower ROAS. In either campaign you can keep adding new ads to an existing ad set.

## Example
Say a skincare brand's main campaign spends $5,000 a day and a new video takes 60% of spend at a 2.6x return against a 2.2x target for a week. The brand duplicates it unchanged into an ABO ad set at $500/day (10% of total daily spend), leaves the original live, doubles to $1,000 after two good days, and adds a second ad set with existing customers excluded to see how much of the return is net-new.

## Common mistakes
- Turning off the original after duplicating.
- Editing the duplicate and losing its engagement.
- Isolating ads that never earned spend.
- Judging isolated sets by platform ROAS alone.
- Setting this up before the account has real spend or proven ads.
