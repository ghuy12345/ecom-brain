# Diagnosing Plateaus and Stalls

## What it is
Most accounts hit ceilings where spend refuses to climb without ROAS collapsing. Common stalls cluster around a few thousand dollars a day and again around $10k/day (roughly $300k a month). The reflex is to raise budgets, restructure or blame the auction. The usual real cause is that the current ads, avatar and funnel have reached everyone they can convert. Each spend level needs a slightly better skill set: not new tactics, but better execution of ads, funnels and offers.

## When to use it
- Spend has been flat for several weeks despite scale attempts.
- Every budget increase is punished.
- Results from one market or one product are fading while others hold.

## How (steps)
1. **First understand why your working thing works.** Write down the avatar, desire, angle, mechanism and offer behind your top spenders, and why a particular market or format scaled. Many people push one thing to its limit and then jump to random new ideas without knowing what made the first one win.
2. **Then understand why the other attempts did not.** Compare your winners with ads and brands that are scaling in your category and look for the gap. This pattern recognition comes from reviewing and producing a large volume of ads.
3. **Pick a route through the stall:**
   - **Improve the current winner.** Iterate its hook, format, creator and script; a strong winner can sometimes carry roughly double the spend.
   - **Open new sub-avatars.** Go back to ideation for other sub-avatars who share the core desire, and return to iteration later. Expect this to take months, not one or two attempts. You can force spend on new sub-avatar tests in a separate campaign, but it is optional; an ad that is good enough will earn spend in the main campaign anyway.
   - **Chunk up.** Once one sub-avatar carries $10-20k/day, widen to broader avatars or earlier awareness stages, which tend to open new audiences. Execution matters more as targeting widens.
   - **Scale through the product line.** When the account is maxed, a higher-priced version (say around three times the price) lifts how much you can afford to acquire each customer. Extra products for the avatar you already serve often beat new avatars or new countries.
4. **Check conversion before blaming reach.** Many stalls around $10k/day are conversion-rate and message-precision problems. A page built for the winning ad, or a sharper offer, can unlock the next level.
5. **Check sophistication by market.** If one country is fading while another holds, the second may simply be less sophisticated. New identity, new information, a new mechanism or shifting a belief can restore growth in the first.
6. **Shore up the backend** at very high spend: compliance, payment-processor holds, supply chain and legal risk. Do not chase aggressive growth while these are fragile.
7. **Stay with the plan long enough** to see results; a stall is not a reason to abandon the brand.

## Example
Say a sleep-aid brand has spent about $9,000/day for two months, all on one sub-avatar: shift workers. Its top ad works because it shows the exact moment of lying awake after a night shift. The team iterates that ad with three new creators (one becomes the new top spender), opens a second sub-avatar (new parents) with a multi-angle test, and launches a premium three-month bundle that lifts AOV. Over the following quarter, spend moves past $13,000/day.

## Common mistakes
- Raising budgets as the answer to a plateau.
- Assuming the landing page is the problem by default.
- Abandoning a sub-avatar after one or two ads.
- Running too many avatars at once; one to three primary avatars is plenty for most brands.
- Testing new countries before exhausting product and avatar growth at home.
