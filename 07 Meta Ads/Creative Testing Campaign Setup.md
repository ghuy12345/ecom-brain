# Creative Testing Campaign Setup

## What it is
The concept test is the unit of work in a Meta account. Each new idea launches as one ad set containing three creatives that share a concept and differ in one variable (three hooks on the same demo, for instance), each carrying two primary texts and two headlines. Delivery chooses the combinations; you read the result at ad-set level. Tests sit inside the main campaign, next to the ads already making money.

## When to use it
- Every time you launch a new concept, angle or format.
- With a campaign budget of at least about $100/day.
- Alongside existing profitable campaigns, never as a replacement for them.

## How (steps)
1. **Campaign:** sales objective, campaign budget on, automated catalog options off.
2. **Ad set:** website purchases, maximise conversions, start at midnight in the account timezone, broad targeting (location, age, gender; all adults and genders if unsure), automatic placements. Exclude past purchasers only if your repeat rate is high. Do not set per-ad-set minimum or maximum spend for normal tests.
3. **Ads:** upload manually, single image or video, multi-advertiser and automatic AI enhancements off, vertical 9:16 edited inside safe zones, Shop Now button, description used for the offer. Build one ad, duplicate it twice and swap only the creative. Add the two copies and two headlines inside each ad. Add UTMs and link Instagram.
4. **One concept per ad set.** Do not mix concepts, and keep images and videos in separate ad sets.
5. **Treat each ad as a congruent unit.** Delivery groups similar ads and can recombine parts. If a headline only makes sense with one image, build that pairing as its own ad rather than as interchangeable assets, so the unit is judged as a whole.
6. **Dynamic or flexible formats are still valid** when you want the platform to mix assets, but flexible ads cannot be broken down per asset. Use manual ads when the creative is the variable you need to read. The text and headline breakdown still works on manual ads.
7. **Check capacity.** Roughly daily budget divided by target CPA gives the number of concepts you can test at once ($100/day at a $20 CPA allows about five). At a few hundred dollars a day, run no more than about five ad sets including champions.
8. **Run 3-7 days.** Judge on the three-day average and whether spend rises day by day, never on day one. Decide at seven days. Do not leave tests running beyond about 14 days.
9. **Turn off at ad-set level** if, a week in, it has earned only a sliver of spend at a poor return, or if it grabbed most of the spend in the first three days, performed badly and dragged campaign ROAS down. Note why it drew attention before discarding it, and give any rework two to three days.
10. **Test new creatives in both campaign-budget and ad-set-budget setups** if you are unsure which suits your account; many find campaign budgets win on efficiency.
11. **Bulk launch at volume.** At high output, a spreadsheet-style bulk uploader (one row per ad, asset pulled from a shared drive, copy pasted in) removes the upload bottleneck. Three ads means three rows; multiple asset slots in one row are aspect-ratio versions of the same ad. Filter drafts and errors before you assume a batch is live.

## Adding a page to the test
A fourth dimension, two landing pages, doubles the ad count to six per ad set because the URL forces duplication. Only do this above roughly $2-3k/day. Read results pairwise (creative 1 on page A against creative 1 on page B), ignoring pairs where one side got almost no spend.

## Example
Say a hypothetical kettle-descaler brand spends $300 a day at a $30 target CPA. It launches one concept a day: three videos with different hooks about limescale in cheap kettles, the best existing primary text plus one new one, and the best headline plus one new one. On day seven, one concept has 40% of spend at target and the campaign's ROAS has risen, so it moves into the champions ad set by duplication. Two others with under 5% of spend are turned off.

## Common mistakes
- Pausing working campaigns to make room for tests.
- Mixing images and videos or several concepts in one ad set.
- Diluting a small budget across too many ad sets.
- Uploading horizontal creative.
- Killing ads that worked for weeks after one bad day.
