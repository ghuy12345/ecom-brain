# Multi-Angle vs Single-Angle vs Volume Testing

## What it is
A standard test batch is about three creatives under one concept. What changes between those three decides what you learn:
- **Multi-angle (breadth):** three different angles, one execution each. Tells you where attention is.
- **Single-angle (depth):** one angle, three executions (different hooks, styles, formats or copy strategies). Tells you whether an angle truly works and which execution of it is best.
- **Volume blast:** many loosely guided variations launched to see what sticks.
Whichever you choose, you must know which angle and which person each ad is aimed at. When something wins, that is how you learn who you are actually reaching.

## When to use it
Use this note whenever you plan a test batch, and especially after a plateau or when entering a new product. The three methods are stages, not rival options: where you are in the sequence decides which one the next batch uses.

## How (steps)
1. **Label every ad** with its desire, sub-avatar and angle before launch.
2. **Work out which stage you are at** (see the sequence below).
3. **Keep batches small:** two to four creatives. Two is fine if you only have two good ideas.
4. **Make the differences meaningful.** Delivery treats near-identical ads (a reworded hook, a recoloured background) as the same ad. Ten versions of one headline teach nothing.
5. **Make each ad visibly match its angle.** If the angle is "doctor-designed", show the doctor; if it is "stainless steel", show the steel.
6. **After results, write the learning as person plus angle** ("this sub-avatar responds to the cleaning angle"), not a structural note ("video works", "good hook rate").
7. **Loop:** breadth to find a direction, depth to find winners in that direction, volume on top, and back to breadth for new angles when the winners plateau.

## The default sequence
How far along you are decides the stage; the order itself does not change.

**Stage 1: multi-angle.** Start by testing several angles at once, with a few well-researched ads rather than many thin ones. This is the default for a new product or market, after a plateau, and on a limited budget. Images are a cheap, fast way to run it.
Limit: an angle that loses here is not dead. One execution cannot kill an angle; it only shows where attention went.

**Stage 2: single-angle depth.** Once you have a couple of winning angles, test those angles deeply with many variations: different hooks, styles, formats and copy strategies. This is also where an image winner becomes a video (video versions of image winners often scale further), and where you separate an execution problem from an angle problem, which takes about three honest tries.
Limit: going narrow too early causes tunnel vision. It over-commits to an unproven idea, is slow, and hides breakthroughs on other angles, so do not skip stage 1.

**Stage 3: volume on top.** With iterations and variations of proven angles running, add volume as an extra layer, not a replacement. Volume works best with content generated outside your strategy, such as seeded creator videos, marketplace creator content or shop-style creator clips, after which you identify the winning angle and sub-avatar and feed it back into a stage 2 test. A dedicated volume engine rarely pays at small spend (as a rough estimate, under about $100k a month).
Never volume-launch your own near-identical iterations; that is scattershot, and it teaches nothing.

## Example
Say a hypothetical water-bottle brand tests a "best bottle" concept as a multi-angle batch: lightweight, insulated, easiest to clean. "Easiest to clean" takes most of the spend. The next batch is single-angle: three executions of the cleaning angle (a grime close-up, a side-by-side teardown, a creator talking through her weekly routine). The winning execution becomes a video and starts to scale. Only then does the brand add a creator-seeding programme on top, mining its clips for new sub-avatars to test in the next depth batch.

## Common mistakes
- Testing without knowing which person each ad targets.
- Declaring an angle dead after one multi-angle execution.
- Jumping straight to depth or volume before multi-angle testing has found a couple of winners.
- Counting micro-variations as creative volume. Volume means concepts; a sensible floor is about one new concept (three ads) a day for a small team.
- Choosing the method over the ad. The customer never sees your method; build the strongest possible ad for each angle.
