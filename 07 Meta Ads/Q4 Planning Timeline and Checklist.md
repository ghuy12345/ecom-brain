# Q4 Planning Timeline and Checklist

## What it is
Q4 results are mostly decided before November. The brands that do well arrive with momentum, a bank of proven ads, offers and discount codes already scheduled, payments and stock secured, and a written plan for each week. Ad costs rise sharply across the quarter, so creative strength and preparation, not clever settings, carry you through.

## When to use it
- Start in July or August for supply chain and projections.
- Work the plan from September through December.
- Revisit it in January to plan next year.

## How (steps)
**July-August: foundations**
1. Gather last year's Q4 numbers: monthly revenue and spend, break-even, best sellers, new versus returning customers, and the offers and ads that won.
2. Build projections by comparing this year's run rate with last year's. Size ad spend and inventory using those projections, and avoid over-ordering unless you are confident of sell-through.
3. Confirm supply chain capacity about 60 days out, with suppliers who can handle peak volume.
4. Work out cohort LTV at three, six and twelve months and use it to cap CPA for Q4 buyers.

**September-October: build the bank**
5. Shift effort from structure changes to understanding why your winners work, and iterate on that reason. More evergreen winners now means more ads to badge for the sale later.
6. Begin testing gifting angles in September or October. As gifting season nears, buyers are more product-aware and simple product-focused ads work.
7. Check competing retail events (for example a large marketplace's early-October sale) and choose whether to counter with your own promotion. Use scarcity honestly in early promos.
8. Test offers before the season, inside the main campaign; a two-to-three-week multi-offer test is fine.
9. Secure accounts and payments: raise card limits, add backup payment methods and a compliant backup ad account (see [[Ad Account Protection and Payment Hygiene]]).

**Late October-November: execute**
10. Pre-schedule discount codes and theme switches using your store's scheduling tools so nothing depends on someone being online.
11. Upload sale ads early to avoid review delays.
12. Leave evergreen running beside the dedicated sale campaigns.
13. Expect rising costs. CPCs and CPMs climb with competition, and Black Friday CPMs may far exceed last year's. Respond with stronger ads rather than chasing the metric; if clicks grow while conversions drop, find out why people click without buying.
14. Have someone available to fix the store during peak days, and run a full mobile QA of checkout before the week.

**December: close out**
15. Confirm carrier shipping cut-offs, publish them with a day or two of padding, and use them as genuine urgency.
16. Plan the next gifting holiday if it matters for your product.

## Newer versus established brands
- **Newer brands** lack historical data and accumulated awareness. Run straightforward back-to-back sales, make minimal operational changes before Q4, and keep structures simple.
- **Established brands** can use last year's data for projections, consider launching new SKUs, and run more elaborate offer ladders and pre-sale list building.
Avoid launching a new store or start a new brand during Q4, and avoid team holidays during the quarter.

## Example
Say a gift-friendly candle brand pulls last year's numbers in August and sees November did 2.5x October's revenue at a lower ROAS. It orders stock for a 2x November (with pre-orders as a buffer), runs gifting concepts from late September, builds four new evergreen winners in October, schedules its sale theme and codes, raises its card limit and keeps its evergreen campaign untouched throughout.

## Common mistakes
- Treating Q4 planning as a November task.
- Extrapolating the current run rate straight into November inventory.
- Restructuring the account weeks before the peak.
- Letting payments lapse mid-scale.
- Validating sale offers only by email, since email mainly reaches past buyers, who behave differently from new ones.
