# Raw Creator Content Campaigns

## What it is
Raw creator content is unpolished video from creators: product-seeding responses, marketplace creator clips, ambassador uploads. It is not engineered around a tested angle, yet it often sells, partly because it looks like a real person's post. It behaves differently from strategised ads: it tends to be delivered to warmer audiences, its wins are harder to explain, and it arrives in volumes that would swamp a normal concept test. That raises the structural question of where it should run. The default answer is simple: alongside everything else. How polished an ad looks does not decide whether it gets spend; whether it converts does.

## When to use it
- You have a steady supply of creator content you did not script.
- You run product seeding and want to know which creators deserve a paid retainer.
- You want to compare creators against each other, for example to pick ambassadors.

## How (steps)
1. **Test it in the main campaign by default.** While testing, UGC, creator content and edited ads can all sit in one campaign with campaign budget, broad targeting and the same UTMs, judged by who earns spend at KPI.
2. **Structure the content.** When content is plentiful, put three different creator videos per ad set, or organise by asset (several creators each making videos about the same product). When content is thin, edit one raw video into three versions that start at different moments, so each opens with a different hook, with no added text or music.
3. **Use one ad set and one landing page** if you rely on flexible ads, and scale at ad level. Remember flexible ads hide which asset won; open the post view to see the leading asset, or use manual ads when you need to know which creator is winning.
4. **Expect high frequency.** Raw content is often pushed toward people who already know you, so frequency can run high even though the ad is not worn out. Read it alongside spend and return rather than as a kill signal.
5. **Give it time.** At a typical $20-30 target CPA and a $100/day budget, each ad may need three or four days to show anything; on lower budgets, give the full seven.
6. **Combine winners for scaling.** When scaling, put creator winners and regular winners together in one ad set or campaign rather than keeping them in separate lanes; let them compete on results.
7. **Promote insight, not just clips.** Use what wins in raw content as a signal for produced work: the angle, the opening line, the demonstration. Feed the best clips into main-campaign creative.
8. **If you run a creator-vs-creator test, cap it.** Give it a budget share of total spend (for example a fixed couple of hundred dollars a day at $3-10k/day) and an exit rule.
9. **Use original content.** Pay creators, source footage or generate it; do not re-upload other people's ads or videos.

## Separate campaign or same campaign?
**Default: one campaign while testing.** Put UGC, creator content and edited ads into the same campaign and let delivery allocate across all of them. This keeps data consolidated, and there is no need to protect raw content from polished ads: an ad's production value is irrelevant if it does not convert, and a raw clip that converts will win spend on its own.

**Run a separate UGC campaign only when** you specifically want to test creator against creator, such as pushing some spend to seeded creators to see which ones merit an ambassador deal. That is a deliberate comparison, not a permanent second lane.

**For scaling, combine.** Creator winners and regular winners go into one ad set or campaign together.

## Example
Say a haircare brand seeds product to 40 creators a month and receives about 25 usable videos. It launches them into its main campaign beside its edited ads, three creator videos per ad set. To choose ambassadors, it also runs a small creator-vs-creator test at $300/day out of $6,000/day. Two creators' videos keep taking spend at target for three weeks; the brand offers both monthly retainers, moves their clips into the same scaling ad set as its best edited ads, and briefs its editor to rebuild their opening lines into new produced ads.

## Common mistakes
- Treating raw content as strategy; it is a signal source.
- Reading high frequency as fatigue.
- Using flexible ads and then being unable to tell which creator won.
- Using clips without the creator's permission.
- Splitting raw content into its own campaign by default, on the assumption it cannot compete with polished ads.
- Keeping creator winners and regular winners in separate lanes once you are scaling.
