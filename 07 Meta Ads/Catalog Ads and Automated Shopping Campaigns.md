# Catalog Ads and Automated Shopping Campaigns

## What it is
Two Meta formats lean heavily on automation:
- **Catalog ads** (dynamic product ads) pull product images, names and prices from your product feed and show each person the items the system predicts they want.
- **Automated shopping campaigns** (Meta's Advantage+ shopping type) take broad control of audience and placement, with few manual levers.
Both can be useful. Both also tend to find people already close to buying, which can make them look better on platform ROAS than they are for growth.

## When to use it
- Catalog ads: stores with many SKUs where people browse variety, such as fashion, furniture or home goods.
- Automated shopping campaigns: as a home for ads that make money without being your best, typically once an account is spending around $10k/day or more.
- Reach campaigns aimed at awareness: rarely worth it until budgets approach $100k/day.

## How (steps)
1. **Check SKU depth first.** If you sell a handful of products, catalog ads add little; a small supplement range, for example, rarely benefits. With hundreds of SKUs they can outperform standard ads.
2. **Run catalog ads in their own campaign**, separate from creative testing, so their bottom-of-funnel behaviour does not distort which concepts look like winners.
3. **Cap their share.** Catalog ads mostly serve warm, high-intent people. A modest share of spend is fine; when they take most of the budget, you are likely paying to re-reach buyers who would have purchased anyway. Check new-versus-existing breakdowns.
4. **Write the offer into catalog copy during sales.** Most catalogs carry no sale messaging, so a sale-period catalog ad can look unrelated to the promotion. Adding the offer to the catalog text or frames is a cheap congruency win.
5. **Use automated shopping campaigns as a holding pen,** not a magic upgrade. Moving winning ads into one typically lifts results by around 10-20%, not multiples.
6. **Control the setup if you need to.** Meta keeps nudging advertisers into the automated version. If you want the standard campaign type, setting a manual age range in the audience controls typically keeps you out of the fully automated setup. Leave automatic placements on.
7. **Prefer one consolidated campaign** over several parallel automated campaigns, so ads compete in one pool.

## Example
Say a furniture store with 400 SKUs runs its main creative campaign at $4,000/day and adds a catalog campaign at $600/day with sale-price frames during a spring promotion. The catalog campaign shows a 6x platform ROAS. Breaking it down by audience, most purchases come from people who visited the site in the last two weeks, so the store keeps it capped near 15% of spend and judges growth on the main campaign's new-customer results.

## Common mistakes
- Running catalog ads for a five-product brand.
- Letting catalog ads take most of the budget because their reported ROAS is high.
- Expecting an automated campaign type to multiply results; creative still decides the ceiling.
- Mixing catalog ads into concept tests.
- Running reach campaigns at small budgets.
