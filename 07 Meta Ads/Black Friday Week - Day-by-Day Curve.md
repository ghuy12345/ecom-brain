# Black Friday Week - Day-by-Day Curve

## What it is
Black Friday to Cyber Monday (BFCM) follows a fairly predictable shape across most ecommerce brands. Knowing the curve tells you when to push budget, when to hold, and when a slow morning is just the normal pattern rather than a crisis. The shape below is relative; your own last year's hourly data is always the better guide.

## When to use it
- When planning BFCM budgets and staffing.
- During the week itself, to judge whether today is behaving normally.
- When deciding whether to swap out sale ads after Friday.

## How (steps)
1. **Thursday:** a small bump. If results are good, start incremental scaling. Resist doubling budgets simply because Friday is close; set budgets and start tracking from Thursday.
2. **Friday:** typically the largest day for most brands. Scale aggressively on good periods. A quiet morning often recovers in the afternoon; avoid sweeping changes before midday.
3. **Saturday:** clearly lower. Revenue occasionally holds up, but efficiency and margin are usually lower. Trim budgets that are not earning.
4. **Sunday:** slower still.
5. **Cyber Monday:** close to a normal day until mid-afternoon US Eastern time, then a surge of last-minute shoppers that runs to midnight. The pace in those hours can rival Friday, but the day's total rarely beats Friday.
6. **Beat the timing trap.** Meta reacts about one to two hours late. If you raise budget only after you see the evening ROAS, delivery arrives after the rush. Raise budgets shortly before known peaks so delivery is already pacing when buyers arrive.
7. **Keep sale ads running** through the weekend, Cyber Monday and after. They hold momentum and delivery history; swapping in fresh ads on Monday and hoping delivery ramps again from scratch usually backfires. If you worry about brand consistency, label creatives "Black Friday, Cyber Monday sale" from the start.
8. **Cut after the peak.** Demand falls after the weekend, so pull budgets down the following day instead of keeping Friday-level spend.

## Profit, not revenue
The aim of the week is profit. If Friday's scaling went too far and ate margin, recover it over the weekend and on Monday evening; do not chase Friday's revenue figure. A modest day with healthy gross profit is better than a record day with almost none.

## The weekly pacing caveat
Meta has moved toward pacing budgets over a week rather than strictly per day, which can let it spend above a daily budget on some days. How this behaves over BFCM is not well established, so watch spend closely on each day. At $10-20k/day, a 100% budget bump is a big move; treat it with respect.

## Uplift expectations
Brands that ran deep discounts all year often see only a modest BFCM uplift, perhaps 1.5-2x a normal day. Brands that hold back their best offer for the event tend to see much larger lifts. Your offer strategy shapes the curve's height more than any media-buying trick.

## Example
Say a fashion brand normally spends $3,000/day. On Thursday it runs $3,500 at target. On Friday it raises budget at 6am Eastern before the morning surge and doubles twice on strong periods, spending $11,000. Saturday reads below target by midday, so it trims 20% twice. On Monday it holds a normal budget until mid-afternoon, then raises budget an hour before the evening rally and spends most of the day's budget after 4pm. Tuesday it resets to half of Monday's actual spend.

## Common mistakes
- Waiting to see the evening peak before raising budgets.
- Doubling everything on Friday morning regardless of data.
- Replacing working sale ads on Monday.
- Holding Friday budgets into a quiet week.
- Measuring success by revenue instead of profit.
