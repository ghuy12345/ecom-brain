# Meta Scaling Decision Tree

## What it is
Scaling is a daily routine, not a mood. Once a day you check the whole account against your target KPI and follow one of two branches: scale up or scale down. The tree stops you from chasing good days, panicking on bad ones, and fiddling with settings when the real fix is a better ad. Step sizes are covered in [[Budget Increase Rules for Evergreen Scaling]].

## When to use it
- Every 24 hours on evergreen campaigns once you have a target KPI and a minimum daily spend.
- Not during a sale; use [[Intraday Budget Scaling for Sale Periods]] instead.

## How (steps)
**Before you start:** set your break-even ROAS, a target above it, and a minimum daily spend (a floor you keep regardless of results, based on cash flow; beginners often use $50-200/day, larger brands sometimes about half their usual spend).

**Scale-up branch**
1. Is the account as a whole at target or better? If not, go to the scale-down branch.
2. Have you been above target consistently for 48-72 hours (the "scaling protocol")? If not, check again tomorrow. Two exceptions let you go sooner: a limited-time promotion with a hard end date, and a genuine change, like a newly winning ad, offer or page that is clearly lifting results (even then, give it a day or so to confirm).
3. Is the result driven by clicks? You want roughly 60% or more of purchases credited to clicks, or click-only results that meet KPI on their own. If not, wait another day and think about moving to a 7-day click window.
4. Scale by the step size for your level, then leave it 24 hours.

**Scale-down branch**
1. Has the account been under break-even for one to two days? If you are above your minimum daily spend, cut about 20% (the 20% step here and elsewhere in this note is a rough estimate, not a benchmark).
2. Between break-even and target? Cut only once the 7-day click result has stayed under target for two to three days running.
3. Do not swing budgets up and down daily; give delivery time to settle.

**While cutting back or sitting at your floor, put the hours outside Ads Manager:**
1. Ship new creative tests.
2. Inspect the store every day for anything broken: removed discount codes, product page edits, stock-outs.
3. Study which ads, angles, avatars and styles earn spend, and turn that into the next batch.
4. Research fresh avatars, mechanisms and positioning, and revisit old winners.
5. After a months-long plateau, try new offers in the main campaign using new ads.

## Why scale-ups fail
Your ads convert a core pool of people well. Raising the budget forces delivery further out to colder people who need more information. If the ad and page cannot persuade them, ROAS falls. That is why the fix is nearly always creative, offer or funnel, not settings.

## Structure by spend level (bands overlap)
- Up to around $3k/day: one main campaign; whether it is a standard budget-optimised or automated shopping campaign barely matters.
- About $3-10k/day: optionally add a small creator-vs-creator test campaign; otherwise creator content tests in the main campaign.
- About $10-50k/day: add an automated shopping campaign for profitable ads and whitelisted creator ads.
- $50k+/day: segment by product line or avatar.
Earn your structure; running several ad accounts has not been shown to improve performance.

## Example
Say your target is 2.5x and break-even is 1.6x. Monday through Wednesday the account runs 2.9x, 3.0x and 2.8x, and 70% of purchases are click-based. You raise budget 20% on Thursday. On Saturday the account drops to 1.4x for two days; you cut 20% and spend Monday launching two new concepts and checking that the checkout discount still works.

## Common mistakes
- Scaling one campaign because it looks good while the account is below target.
- Scaling on view-through-inflated data.
- Scaling after one good day that nothing explains.
- Responding to a stall with more settings changes.
- Keeping no list of past big winners to remind you what good looks like.
