# Giveaway Draw, Winners and ROI

## What it is
The closing stage of a customer survey giveaway: picking winners fairly, checking they took part properly, telling the right people, actually sending the prizes, and working out whether the exercise paid for itself. Done carelessly, this stage can undo the trust the giveaway was meant to build. Done well, it produces happy winners, a warm list and a clear view of the return.

## When to use it
- The day after a survey giveaway closes, before the winner-announcement email goes out.
- About a week later, once the founder's thank-you email has been sent and you can total the revenue.

## How (steps)
1. **Draw winners in your giveaway app.** Open the ended promotion, set the number of winners to match your prize structure, include entries from the whole period and draw.
2. **Vet before accepting.** Check each winner's name field and survey answers. Find their address in the entrant export and make sure the survey questions were really answered. If someone left only a name and email, gave joke answers or entered an unusable name, drop them and redraw. A real name visible in the email address is acceptable. Check suspected employee entries the same way.
3. **Make vetting defensible.** Say in the promotion copy that every response is read and only genuine answers earn bonus entries, and make sure your official rules permit replacing a drawn winner. Bonus-entry wording alone is narrower than a right to disqualify, so check the rules match what you do.
4. **Fill in the announcement email.** Show each winner by first name and surname initial to protect privacy, with the prize beside each name. When prize rank follows draw order, keep that order.
5. **Send it only to entrants.** Create a dedicated entrants list (fed by your giveaway app's integration) and select it explicitly as the recipient. Most of the wider past-purchaser list never entered, has no context and has already received several emails; announcing a contest they skipped confuses them and invites unsubscribes. Forgetting to switch recipients from the earlier segment is the most common error.
6. **Preview and schedule.** Check the mobile view, send yourself a test using a real customer profile so personalised fields fill in, open it on your phone, and schedule it at a fixed time on the day after the giveaway closes.
7. **Contact winners individually.** Have the owner or customer-service team email each winner from a normal branded address, congratulates them, confirms which prize they won and asks for shipping details and sizes where relevant.
8. **Always deliver the prizes.** Customers gave you candid feedback; failing to send prizes betrays that and damages trust. Only promise prizes you can afford to ship.
9. **Measure the return.** Once every email, including the founder's discount email, has gone out, filter the email platform's campaigns by the giveaway tag, set the metric to placed orders, and total the revenue in a sheet. Use the real prize cost (cost of goods plus shipping, not retail value). Return = sequence revenue / prize cost.

## Example
Say a coffee brand gives away three bundles with a retail value of $450 but a cost of goods and shipping of $150. Two drawn winners left blank survey answers, so they are replaced. The announcement goes to the 1,200 entrants only, and each winner gets a personal email asking for a delivery address. A week after the founder's discount email, the tagged campaigns show a hypothetical $1,800 in placed orders, a 12x return on prize cost. The team records that figure as an upper bound, because many of those engaged customers might have bought anyway. For comparison, one reported giveaway sequence brought in $3,102 in placed orders against a prize costing about $200, roughly a 15x return.

## Common mistakes
- Accepting winners without checking their answers, so prizes go to people who gave nothing.
- Sending the announcement to the past-purchaser segment instead of entrants.
- Publishing full names of winners.
- Using retail value as prize cost, which understates the return; or treating attributed revenue as incremental, which overstates it. Without a holdout or baseline, attributed revenue is a ceiling, not a lift figure. Attribution defaults in email platforms also change over time, so note the settings used.
- Delaying or quietly skipping prize delivery.
