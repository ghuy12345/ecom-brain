# Pre-Sale VIP Lead Generation

## What it is
Instead of asking cold traffic to buy today, you run ads that ask people to join a VIP list for something genuinely better than the public sale: earlier access, a deeper discount, or first pick of products that sell out. When the sale opens, you email and text that list, and the first send can create a large spike in orders. Anyone who does not buy stays on the list for future marketing.

## When to use it
Run it only if both conditions hold:
- **You have real owned-channel skill.** How you email and text these leads decides the result. Without a competent email/SMS operation the leads go cold.
- **Your economics reward a list.** At least one of: substantial recurring revenue, roughly 30% or more of customers coming back, a strong share of genuine word-of-mouth, or unusually distinctive positioning.

If neither is true, keep the budget on direct-response sales campaigns. The tactic suits sale periods such as Black Friday, where the VIP promise is believable and time-bound (see [[Black Friday and Q4 Offer Strategy]]).

## How (steps)
1. **Give a real reason to sign up.** For example, VIPs get 35% off while the public sale is 30%, or VIPs get 24 hours of early access to limited stock. A vague "join our list" ad converts poorly and attracts low-intent sign-ups.
2. **Project the outcome from five inputs:** lead-ad spend, cost per lead, average order value at the VIP price, the share of leads who buy, and offer margin.
3. **Use offer margin, not normal margin.** A product at 30% off has much thinner margin than your everyday COGS figure suggests; work from what is left once the VIP discount is applied.
4. **Work out the results:** expected leads, revenue, profit and the ROAS needed to break even. Then run a worse case (higher cost per lead, lower conversion) before you commit.
5. **Underestimate every input.** Spending more pushes your cost per lead upward, just as ROAS slides when you scale. As rough planning ranges, expect around 2-4% lead-to-customer conversion when AOV is above about $200 and around 6-10% when it is below. Treat higher claims as optimistic, and never copy someone else's conversion assumption.
6. **Time it.** Start the lead campaign roughly a fortnight ahead of the VIP window, then release the offer to the list. Some brands run a shorter lead-gen burst one or two days before the sale as one of their sale-period ad formats.
7. **Make the creative specific.** Ads that name a strong offer and speak to a clear audience produce more buyers than generic "join the list" ads shown to everyone.
8. **Measure with a long window.** Lead ads convert later than purchase ads, so use a third-party attribution tool with a long or lifetime window alongside platform data, and track how many purchases came from new customers.

## Example
Say an outdoor-gear brand with an AOV of about $300 tests the idea before Black Friday. It plans $2,000 of lead spend at a hypothetical $3 per lead, giving roughly 660 leads. Using the conservative 3% conversion for a higher-AOV product, that is about 20 orders, or around $6,000 of revenue at the VIP price. Using the post-discount margin, it checks whether that clears break-even, then reruns the sheet at $4 per lead and 2% conversion. Only if the worse case is still acceptable does it scale, and when it does, it assumes a higher cost per lead and a lower conversion rate than the test showed, because small samples flatter.

For scale, one reported small test spent about $1,400 over two weeks at roughly $2.78 per lead, collecting around 490 leads; at a $370 AOV and about 4% conversion that came to some 20 purchases, mostly from new customers, at close to 5x ROAS.

## Common mistakes
- Running lead gen without the email and SMS capability to convert the list.
- Projecting with standard margin rather than post-discount margin.
- Borrowing an optimistic conversion rate (for example planning 10% and getting 4%).
- Assuming cost per lead stays flat as spend multiplies.
- Judging the campaign on platform purchase attribution alone, which misses delayed conversions.
- Offering VIPs nothing more than the public gets, so the sign-up has no pull.
