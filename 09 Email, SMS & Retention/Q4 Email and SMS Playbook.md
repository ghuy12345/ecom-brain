# Q4 Email and SMS Playbook

## What it is
A working plan for owned channels during the peak season, from the pre-sale warm-up to the last campaign of the year. In this window almost every shopper is most aware: they know what they want and are waiting for the price. Email and SMS do the most work when they are planned early, sent to the right segments and kept clean enough that inbox placement survives the volume.

## When to use it
- From late summer onward, for any brand with a meaningful list, whether you run one simple sale or several offer funnels.
- Adapt the dates for your market: Asia-heavy lists peak around Singles' Day (11.11), North America around Black Friday and Cyber Monday plus the mid-December shopping weekends, and the UK, Australia, New Zealand and Canada see a strong Boxing Day.

## How (steps)
1. **Lock the timeline.** Finalise offers and the sign-up pop-up two to three weeks out, fix the send calendar and test the flows about a week later, then run a warm-up of around two weeks.
2. **Map the phases.** Warm-up; the Black Friday to Cyber Monday stretch; gifting; last orders before delivery cut-offs; year-end clearance; a final send of the year. Give each phase its own creative: teasers and VIP framing for warm-up; the percentage off as the headline at peak; extra savings and urgency on Cyber Monday; gift imagery and ideas below a set price; shipping deadlines and express options for last chance (often the strongest lever); clearance and new-year themes after.
3. **Collect VIP early-access sign-ups.** Use the pop-up to collect sign-ups, then run a short warm-up flow: instant confirmation, a teaser a couple of days later, an optional founder note (more useful for newer brands), early access opening, and an "ends tonight" reminder. See [[Pre-Sale VIP Lead Generation]] for buying these leads with ads.
4. **Size the structure to your stage.** An emerging brand runs one funnel and keeps the offer simple. A growing brand can add a second or third (site-wide, tiered "up to" savings, subscription bundles). A large brand runs funnels across everything with a dedicated team. Lead with your strongest offer on Black Friday and follow with a different offer on Cyber Monday for late deciders.
5. **Segment by engagement.** Your primary audience is recently engaged (opened or clicked in about 30 days, or bought in about 60). Widen to a 90-day engaged segment only for the biggest offers. Hold back long-dormant contacts until year-end clearance, and exclude loyalty or subscription customers from discounts they do not need.
6. **Control volume.** Most brands send one email a day at peak with three or four warm-up emails; only very large senders with strong placement go to two a day. SMS stays scarce: at most one a day, two (possibly three) on the final day, reserved for launches, stock alerts, shipping cut-offs and VIP confirmations. Keep texts under 160 characters, put the discount in the opening words and use a branded short link.
7. **Protect deliverability.** Narrow to engaged segments as soon as open rates drop or complaints climb, ramp a rarely used sending domain slowly, monitor bounce and unsubscribe rates in the hours after each send, and resend "ends tonight" to non-openers as plain text.
8. **Run QA.** Before each send: subject and preheader length, buttons and images that work, discounts that apply automatically, stacked-offer maths, mobile layout, a plain-text version, and a footer with unsubscribe and address. Align operations on cut-offs, stock and support replies.
9. **For fashion and hype drops, lean on brand.** Warm up two to four weeks with a three-step teaser (announce with silhouetted product, add detail, then say who it is for) and write in your avatar's own register. Brand is said to carry about 80% of a drop and wording 20%; a cited study reported roughly triple the close rate on sales calls when an attractive person merely stood in the background, so use good-looking models who match your customer.

## Example
Say a home-fragrance brand plans a single funnel. Offers lock in late October, the pop-up promises VIPs early access, and the warm-up flow runs for two weeks. On Black Friday the engaged-30 segment gets one email a day with the percentage as the hero, and VIP subscribers get one text when early access opens. Cyber Monday adds a small extra incentive. December shifts to gift ideas, then delivery deadlines. Dormant contacts are only mailed for the late-December clearance.

## Common mistakes
- Validating a new offer by email alone. Your list is mostly past buyers, who behave differently from cold traffic; confirm offers with paid tests too (see [[Black Friday and Q4 Offer Strategy]]).
- Assuming "up to X% off" framing works the same everywhere. It can earn clicks in email when a high-margin item carries the headline number, but judge each channel on its own results.
- Discounting on minor holidays unrelated to your product just before the main sale; use engagement content such as a quiz instead.
- Broken buttons and wrong codes, the most common and costly peak-season errors.
