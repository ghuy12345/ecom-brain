# Awareness Levels in the Search and Marketplace Era

## What it is
The five awareness levels still hold, but the speed of movement between them has changed. Someone who notices a problem today can search it, scroll short videos, read review threads or ask an AI assistant and be looking at a dozen products within minutes. They can go from first noticing a problem to ordering a product in under half an hour, receive it the next day and form an opinion within a week.

That compression shifts the definitions and, more importantly, makes the **product-aware** level far larger and more skeptical than older playbooks assume. This note covers the update; the five levels themselves are explained in [[Market Awareness Levels]].

## When to use it
- When an "unaware" or "problem aware" ad underperforms and you suspect the audience knows more than you assumed.
- When your category is crowded on marketplaces and many prospects have already bought a competitor.
- When you want to unlock scale beyond buyers who respond to discounts.

## How (steps)
1. **Use the sharper definitions.**
   - *Unaware:* not thinking about the problem at all. Your job is to bring a problem or desire to mind (say, the long-term cost of sitting all day, shown to someone with no pain yet).
   - *Problem aware:* already recognises the problem yet has not made fixing it a priority. Your job is no longer to name it, it is to dramatise why it is worth solving now.
   - *Solution aware:* weighing which *type* of solution to use. You can start here without challenging beliefs about any particular product.
   - *Product aware:* has picked the type and is comparing products, unconvinced yours will work or fit.
   - *Most aware:* has seen your brand, but either lacks trust in you or is still missing an offer worth acting on. It is not only "waiting for a sale".
2. **Map the real journey.** Write out the path a buyer takes from noticing the problem to purchase, including searches and marketplace listings. Note where they likely bought a competing product.
3. **Account for failed purchases.** A buyer who tried a product in your category and it failed is now product aware *and* now carries a hostile belief ("these never work"), plus full awareness of whichever brand let them down. Showing them the same product type with the same claim makes them scroll on.
4. **Win the burned buyer with a new angle.** Either change the approach (a different mechanism, or targeting a cause the category ignores) or explain where the usual category products go wrong and show what you do differently, supported by authority.
5. **Place your offers correctly.** Discounts mainly convert most-aware buyers and the part of the product-aware group happy to gamble on a good price. They do not solve the skepticism of burned buyers.

## Example
Picture a pet brand selling an indoor-accident solution for dogs left home alone. The typical journey: the owner searches the problem, sees pads, medication, training and outdoor routines, picks pads, orders the best-reviewed pad from a big online marketplace, and it fails. That owner now believes "pads don't work" while still being product aware of pads. An ad that just says "the best pads" bounces off. Two angles that can land: a calming product that addresses the anxiety behind the accidents (new approach), or an ad explaining why most pads fail (wrong placement, wrong size, unappealing to the dog) followed by why this one is different and who recommends it (new information plus authority).

## Common mistakes
- **Treating "describe the problem" as a problem-aware tactic.** In the search era, naming a problem nobody was thinking about is unaware work; problem-aware work is amplifying a problem people already know.
- **Calling most-aware buyers purely price sensitive.** Many are held back by trust, not price.
- **Ignoring the size of product aware.** If you cannot win here, you cap your scale. The usual blocker is weak positioning: no fresh mechanism, no authority, and only a surface-level grasp of the customer.
- **Assuming older, longer copy rules always apply.** Unaware copy still has to carry the whole journey and runs longest, but buyers arrive at higher levels faster, so test shorter entry points too.

Both definitions of problem aware are used in practice. Use the older one (spell out the problem until they recognise themselves) when the audience genuinely has not connected the dots; use the updated one (amplify a known problem) when search and reviews have already done that work.
