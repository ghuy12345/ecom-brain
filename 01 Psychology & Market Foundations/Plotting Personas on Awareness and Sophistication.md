# Plotting Personas on Awareness and Sophistication

## What it is
Awareness and sophistication are often drawn as one funnel line, which leads to generic ads. They are two separate scales, and every persona sits somewhere on both at once:

- **Awareness** measures how near the person is to a purchase. It chooses your **angle and hook**: where the conversation starts.
- **Sophistication** measures how much the person doubts the promised result can really happen. It chooses your **persuasion load**: how much and what kind of proof you need.

The two do not rise together. A person can be barely aware of the problem yet deeply skeptical of ads in general, or fully solution aware yet optimistic. Plotting both prevents mismatches. The scales are explained in [[Market Awareness Levels]] and [[Market Sophistication Stages]]; this note is about using them together.

## When to use it
- Before briefing or writing ads for a new persona.
- When an ad has the right opening but fails to convince, or convinces but nobody stops to watch.
- As practice for sharpening your judgement on ads, whether your own or a rival's.

## How (steps)
**Plot the persona**
1. Find where the persona sits for awareness and choose the opening:
   - Unaware: lead with a deeper hope or worry already on their mind rather than the problem itself.
   - Problem aware: open with the problem.
   - Solution aware: stand out within the category (quicker, safer, more durable).
   - Product aware: answer the remaining questions and objections.
   - Most aware: give a final push with a strong offer, urgency or genuine scarcity.
2. Separately, place them on a skepticism ladder and pick the persuasion load:
   - *Beginner* (little skepticism): one bold, clear promise will do.
   - *Explorer* (has come across a few products): needs differentiators and amplifiers.
   - *Researcher* (wants the ingredients and the how): needs a clearly explained unique mechanism.
   - *Skeptic* (let down before): needs a mechanism backed by proof such as studies, demonstrations and testimonials.
   - *Hopeless* (failed repeatedly and blames themselves): needs hope and an identity reframe, not more proof.
3. Check unusual combinations explicitly. Unaware but skeptical means the doubt is about ads, not the problem, so lower the ad feel. Solution aware but optimistic means they have already bought into the category and mostly want a credible, well-reviewed option.

**Score existing ads (practice drill)**
1. Choose an ad: either a performer from your account or one from an admired brand found in an ad library.
2. Watch it several times. Ignore hook and editing quality; you are judging strategy, not execution.
3. Name the desire it is aimed at, the awareness level it speaks to and the sophistication stage it assumes.
4. Record spend, revenue, the date it went live and a saved copy of the creative.
5. Do this across many ads until patterns show which desires deserve more investment and which levels or stages remain open.

## Example
Say a water-bottle brand (hypothetical) runs an ad saying its cap cleans itself, unlike caps that trap grime. Desire: easy cleaning, laddered up by asking what lies behind it, to feeling safe about what you drink, then better health. Ask which rung customers mention most in reviews; that is the operative desire, with health as the permanent desire above it. Awareness: product aware, because it argues superiority over other bottles. Sophistication: it does not inflate the claim, it explains a better mechanism, so roughly stage 4. Stage 3 versus 4 is often a judgement call; pick one and move on.

## Common mistakes
- Collapsing both dimensions into a single funnel label.
- Loading heavy proof onto an optimistic beginner, or adding more proof for a hopeless persona who needs renewed hope instead.
- Scoring the execution (hook, editing) rather than the strategy.
- Expecting your scores to be perfectly repeatable. Early scoring is awkward; reps fix it.
- Sourcing the desire from guesswork instead of reviews and research.
- Letting the framework cause paralysis. Most competitive categories sit around stage 3 or 4; plot quickly and test.
