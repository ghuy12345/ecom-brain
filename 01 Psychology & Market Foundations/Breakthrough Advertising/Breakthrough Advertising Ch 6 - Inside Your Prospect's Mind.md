# Breakthrough Advertising Ch 6 - Inside Your Prospect's Mind

## Core Argument & Structure

This chapter is the pivot between the book's two halves. It closes the headline section and sets up the persuasion framework for the rest.

The headline has one job: flag down the right prospect and get the first paragraph read. After that, the body does the selling, and it does so not by arguing about the product but by changing how the prospect sees reality. To do that it must widen or shift one or more of three dimensions of the world already in their head.

That gives Schwartz a way to decide copy length. Length isn't a style or format choice; it's the sum of three workloads:

1. Build desire for the product, and for everything it can do, to full strength.
2. Make the prospect feel comfortable and flattered, able to picture the product fitting into the life they are building.
3. Make them believe, overcoming the prejudices and beliefs they already hold.

Your answers to these three set not only length but how the ad is built, how it unfolds, its style and its speed.

## Frameworks & Mechanics

**1. The three dimensions of the mind.**
1. *Desires:* the hungers and urges that drive the prospect day to day.
2. *Identifications:* the roles they want to play and the traits they want a product to help them build or show.
3. *Beliefs:* the views, assumptions, biases, half-knowledge and mental models they live by. Schwartz calls this emotionalised reason.

**2. Types of desire.**
1. *Physical:* slim, strong, healthy, free of skin problems or bad breath.
2. *Material:* money, a better car, a beautiful dress.
3. *Sensual:* a cold drink, a soft bed after a long day.

**3. What copy can and can't do with desire.** It can't create, shrink or fight it. It can widen it, sharpen it, channel it and give it a goal. In some ads the first task is simply to make the prospect want: let them experience the fulfilment through the senses (picturing it, touching it, trying it, hearing friends admire it) before you offer the product. The method is intensification.

**4. Identification.** Every purchase does double duty: a physical gain plus a boost to self-image. A low-calorie food delivers slimness and also a youthful, attractive personality. Unspoken ambitions, envies and fantasies are powerful sales forces. Put them behind the product, make the select group feel tangible, and show the kind of people who already live in the product's world.

**5. Belief.** Believing is a process of fitting new facts into existing patterns of thought. Beliefs act as filters, and their habitual paths are the only channels your argument can travel along. Advertising isn't education and can't argue with them head-on. It changes reality by working with existing tendencies and giving their energy a direction. Build with the prospect's logic, not yours.

**6. Four things to prove** (a proof checklist for any copy):
1. The product satisfies their desires.
2. The product works.
3. People like them trust it.
4. No other product meets their need as well.

**7. Study separately, write together.** The three dimensions are analysed one at a time but written as a single smooth line of thought from start to finish.

## Examples & Evidence

No reproduced ads. Two miniatures and an analogy:

- The low-calorie food buyer shows double duty: physical want plus self-image.
- The car buyer wants power, speed and transport, and equally the signal of success and spare cash it sends to the neighbours. Identification as outward status.
- Cold beer and a soft bed show the desire map reaches down to bodily sensation.

## Modern Validity 2026

**Still works as-is:** stop with the headline, sell with the body, exactly as on Meta. The three dimensions apply to any format; even a 30-second creator video must serve want, identity and belief. AI can't manufacture desire; it just speeds up variations. The belief-filter model bites harder than ever.

**Needs translating:** the "ad" is now a funnel, so the three workloads spread across ad, advertorial and landing page, and length becomes funnel depth. Cold traffic has high belief needs, so use an advertorial; warm traffic can take a short offer ad. Working in "their logic" means mining customer language and reviews before you write. Show the select group through community, creator content and social proof. Belief now costs more because of AI content and ad scepticism, so budget for more proof; never attack beliefs head-on.


## Swipe-Worthy Language Patterns

- **Three prohibitions, four permissions:** list what you can't do, then what you can, getting more concrete each time.
- **Sensory verb cascade** (picture, touch, try, hear the praise) to build desire in advertorials.
- **"Not by smashing into it, but by steering it"** as a contrast for positioning against resistance.
