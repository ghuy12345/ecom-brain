# Breakthrough Advertising - Introduction

## Core Argument & Structure

The introduction does two jobs: it establishes why Schwartz should be listened to, and it sets out the thesis of the whole book.

1. **Credibility.** He writes as someone paid purely on whether his mail-order ads sold. Running his own mail-order companies for over a decade let him test freely, and he calls direct response the best possible school for copywriters.
2. **Goal.** The book is not about small optimisations. It is about the headline and ad that open an entirely new market for a product, which is the "breakthrough" of the title.
3. **Worldview.** Copywriting, investing and science all work with huge natural forces that nobody can produce or turn off. In advertising those forces are the hopes, fears and desires of millions of people. You can't make them, but you can harness them.
4. **Consequence.** Since every force is different, formulas fail. The book's line: "no formula works twice." In their place comes analysis: markers and questions revealing which way a force is moving so you can arrive first, and which can be learned like grammar or arithmetic.
5. **Thesis.** Every new market, product and ad is a brand-new problem that has never existed before.

The closing pages survive only in a later reprint scan, so treat fine wording there as approximate.

## Frameworks & Mechanics

1. **The harness principle.** Scientists, speculators and copywriters all use forces they did not create and cannot increase or reduce. The mass desire to lose weight, channelled onto one product, is the copywriting example. In copywriting terms, you steer desire; you never make it.
2. **Three things a breakthrough ad should do:** make a new product profitable straight away; give an old product a fresh slant; give a product battered by competitors a new weapon, not just to defend itself but to break the loyalty of the rivals' customers.
3. **The anti-formula rule.** A formula is just a recorded solution to an old problem. Change one part of the problem and you need a new answer. The replacement is continuous innovation driven by analysis.
4. **What analysis is,** in four parts: signals of where a force is heading so you get there before rivals; educated guesses based on past wins that cut beneath the surface; skill at posing good questions so the problem itself reveals the answer; and a learnable technique for breakthroughs.
5. **What past successes are for.** Starting points and rough measuring sticks, never answers. The right headline is hidden inside the problem and surfaces once you ask the right questions.
6. **Two halves, in every ad and in the book.** Part one is everything you ask and answer before writing, ending in the headline: the few words that jolt a prospect into paying attention. Part two is body-copy technique. Each needs the other.
7. **The body copy sequence.** Move the reader through four steps in fixed order: agreeing, wanting, seeing themselves in it, and finally being convinced they must have it.
8. **Transferability.** Mail-order methods carry over to general advertising; agencies such as Ted Bates and Ogilvy confirm it.

## Examples & Evidence

No ad reproductions; the evidence is analogy. Atomic energy versus rocket propulsion, and electronics versus uranium stocks, show a winning formula failing on the neighbouring problem. The key case is slimming versus health foods: the appeal that sells weight-loss products collapses on health foods, even to the same audience, because what decides the appeal is the problem, not only the audience.

## Modern Validity 2026

**Still works as-is:** desire channelling is still the base of all ad research, and weight loss is a live example again as new drug classes channel the same old desire. Direct-to-consumer brands on Meta are today's mail order: no salesperson, so the ad and landing page carry the whole sale. "Jolt into attention" is literally the job of the first one to three seconds of a video. The anti-formula point is stronger now: AI tools rewrite other people's headlines at scale, formulas burn out in weeks instead of years, and analysis before writing becomes the real edge.

**Needs translating:** results are now partly modelled rather than counted, because of privacy changes, so measurement stays the guide but with blur. Paid-social teams run frameworks as hypotheses to test at volume, which only squares with Schwartz if each one is a jumping-off point. The four-step body sequence now often spreads across ad, advertorial and landing page rather than living in one ad.


## Swipe-Worthy Language Patterns

- **Radical ownership opener:** state that you live or die by one measurable outcome.
- **Powerlessness, then a turn:** list what nobody can do with a force, then resolve it in a short line about what you can do.
- **Three-field parallel:** show one principle in three unrelated fields to prove it is universal.
- **One-line maxim:** compress a doctrine into a few words for briefs or an anti-template hook.
