# Breakthrough Advertising Ch 5 - Creative Planning

## Core Argument & Structure

This chapter sums up Part 1 and doubles as Schwartz's method statement: creative planning is analysis, and it can't be handed off to formulas. He ranks three levels of creativity and says only the third works, because each ad depends on a one-off fit between product, market and timing. Copies turn into "echo ads" that remind people of someone else's product, and even your own past win can only be used once.

Research gives direction, never the idea itself. Three special headline problems follow: product personality, prevention, and splinter markets. The summary turns chapters one to five into a process ending in five to ten words that carry most of an ad's worth. The book offers, in its words, "not molds, but compasses," and hands the rest of the book its job: building conviction that the product will actually deliver.

## Frameworks & Mechanics

**1. Three levels of creativity.**
1. *Word substitution:* take a proven headline and swap in your product. It only works with the same product type, market and timing (ideally with a stronger promise). Otherwise you get echo ads.
2. *Formulas:* memorised templates poured into like a mould. Legitimate but narrow; they only help with phrasing the idea (see chapter 4).
3. *Analysis:* no answers, only signposts and questions. Each solution works once; there are no shortcuts. Push past your own wins every time.

**2. What research does.**
1. *Information:* hidden desires and their strength, taboos, market splits and the appeal for each, early reactions to wording.
2. *Testing hunches:* checking questions you've already formed.
Research points the way but never supplies the idea: first where not to go, then roughly where to look.

**3. The campaign feedback loop.** Market reactions are mostly negative and show the size of a problem, never its cause. Turn them into explicit "why" questions, let research answer them, and a new theme can emerge at any stage.

**4. Product personality.** List the bundle of traits. Pick the one that best sums up the whole (for that car, quality). Run a headline series on it, tying any secondary traits back to it.

**5. Prevention headlines.** People won't picture future disasters happening to themselves, but they readily picture them happening to family, friends or children, and will buy protection for them.

**6. Splinter markets.**
1. Markets split into sub-groups sharing an appeal but wanting different mechanisms (weight loss for looks wants speed and ease; weight loss for health wants safety and permanence).
2. Small budgets should avoid the core market and pick a segment by sociology rather than geography. The switch changes everything from headline to media and distribution.
3. Later, invade the main market and turn your niche track record into your differentiator.

**7. The planning process.**
1. *Market analysis:* size and depth, the emotional forces, focus on one desire, steer it toward the product.
2. *Product study:* what you physically ship versus what it does for the buyer, narrowed to one claim. Together these form the theme.
3. *Maturity check,* three questions: what the market knows about this product (awareness), what it has been told about rivals (sophistication), and how strongly it feels about both (desire intensity).
4. *Point of entry:* where the headline starts. Product, price, performance, need or the market itself. Here planning stops and writing begins.

**8. The 90% rule.** The headline's few words carry most of the value; at the idea level, copy is all or nothing. Look for the one element that makes this product and market unique; no outside rule supplies it. Guideposts come in two kinds: analytical (split the problem up, identify the piece that matters most, begin with it) and building up (remove limiting factors, add reinforcers).

## Examples & Evidence

A luxury car anchored on quality carries a whole series. A toothpaste sells cavity prevention through the children, and life insurance sells the family's security rather than the buyer's death. A weight-loss product splits looks from health, enters through doctors, then invades the mass market with its niche track record.

## Modern Validity 2026

**Still works as-is:** the Ad Library and AI generation have industrialised word substitution, so echo ads are the default and lose force. The 90% rule maps to the hook. Dashboards show what, never why. In a flood of AI content, belief, more than attention, is the constraint. Beachhead then invasion is the classic direct-to-consumer path.

**Needs translating:** research now covers post-purchase surveys, review mining, comments and interviews. Live tests measure but don't explain. Under broad targeting the creative addresses the segment. The dominant trait becomes a brand asset held across every variant.

**Shifted:** skincare now sells prevention directly as routine; disaster framing still works best through others. Disease-prevention claims are tightly limited.

## Swipe-Worthy Language Patterns

- **"Not templates, but compasses"** as a positioning line for systems and consulting.
- **Argue with your own success** as an internal rule against cloning winners.
