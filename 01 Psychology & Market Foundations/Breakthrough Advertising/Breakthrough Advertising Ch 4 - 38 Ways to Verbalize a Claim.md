# Breakthrough Advertising Ch 4 - 38 Ways to Verbalize a Claim

## Core Argument & Structure

Verbalization is Schwartz's first writing technique: raising a headline's impact through phrasing. Chapters 1 to 3 settled what to say; this chapter is how to say it, the step between choosing the appeal and writing the body. A bare claim is enough only when you're first in your field; otherwise bind extra images to it through the sentence itself. The 38 patterns are open-ended generators, not a checklist.

## Frameworks & Mechanics

### Three purposes of verbalization

1. **Strengthen the claim:** enlarge it, measure it, make it vivid.
2. **Make it fresh again:** twist it, come at it from a new angle, turn it into a story, challenge the reader.
3. **Pull the reader into the body:** promise information, ask a question, partly reveal the mechanism.

### The 38 ways, with modern examples

1. **Quantify size:** "Formulated from 1,200 customer reviews."
2. **Quantify speed:** "Visible difference in 14 days."
3. **Compare** it: "Costs less per wear than one dry-cleaning bill."
4. **Metaphor:** "Hits reset on dull skin."
5. **Make it sensory:** "Feels like fresh linen."
6. **Extreme-case proof:** the bag surviving a drop test.
7. **Dramatise the result:** "My sister asked what I'd changed."
8. **Paradox:** "The lazy routine that finally fixed my skin."
9. **Remove a limitation:** "Results without the 10-step routine."
10. **Admired people or values:** "The bottle our trainers refill."
11. **Detail the work it does:** "Covers all five steps of a morning routine."
12. **Question:** "Who else wants to stop re-buying chargers?"
13. **How-to:** "How to keep cut flowers alive for two weeks."
14. **Attach authority:** "What groomers use on their own dogs."
15. **Before and after** framing of one fact.
16. **Stress newness:** "New this week: the refillable version."
17. **Stress exclusivity:** "Only made with our cold-press process."
18. **Challenge the reader:** "Can you tell which photo is unedited?"
19. **Customer quote:** "Finally, a bra I forget I'm wearing."
20. **Condense** (product replaces something): "Your whole spice rack in one jar."
21. **Symbolise:** "Starting Monday, your hallway gets twice as big."
22. **Mechanism plus claim:** "Wicks sweat out through the seams."
23. **Contradict expectations:** "Wash your hair less to get less grease."
24. **Need plus claim:** "One fix for frizz: seal the cuticle."
25. **Information in the ad:** "Why your plants die in week three."
26. **Case history:** one customer's month.
27. **Name the problem:** "tech neck".
28. **Warn of pitfalls:** "Check these four things before buying a mattress."
29. **Emphasis by repetition:** "Nobody, and we mean nobody, ships faster."
30. **Ease via a limit everyone passed:** "If you can boil water, you can make this."
31. **State the difference:** "The difference is in the stitching."
32. **Old limit gone:** "Dropped it in the pool. Still works."
33. **Address non-buyers:** "Already booked your summer trip? Skip this."
34. **Direct address:** "For the runner who won't settle for sore knees."
35. **How hard it was to make:** "We threw out 41 samples before this one."
36. **Almost too good:** "Is it unfair that this is this easy?"
37. **Challenge a limiting belief:** "Your skin isn't the problem. Your cleanser is."
38. **Question and answer:** their question, answered by the product.

### Grouping by purpose

- **Strengthen:** 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 10, 11, 14, 15, 17, 30, 31, 32, 35
- **Make fresh:** 4, 5, 7, 8, 16, 19, 20, 21, 23, 26, 27, 29, 33, 36
- **Pull into body:** 12, 13, 18, 22, 24, 25, 28, 34, 37, 38

## Examples & Evidence

The book backs each way with real headlines: the Rolls-Royce clock line (proof by extreme case), the piano-course ad (dramatised result), and a cold-remedy pair stating the same five-day recovery once as suffering and once as a promise, showing reinforcement can be pure reframing.

## Modern Validity 2026

**Still works as-is:** AI churns out bare claims, so phrasing is the anti-commodity lever. "Headline" now covers the hook, the opening line of an ad's text, subject lines and page headlines. Questions, how-to, direct address, paradox, negative qualifiers and Q&A transfer directly.

**Needs translating and risky:** measured, comparative and testimonial claims need evidence and disclosure; keep beauty problem-naming cosmetic, not medical. Easy-money frames break financial and Meta rules.

## Swipe-Worthy Language Patterns

- **"For the [person] who won't settle for [less]"** self-selects the right buyer.
- **"Who else wants [result]?"** assumes others already use it.
- **"If you can [trivial skill], you can [result]"** proves ease.
- **"Already [did X]? Don't read this"** uses exclusion to signal value.
