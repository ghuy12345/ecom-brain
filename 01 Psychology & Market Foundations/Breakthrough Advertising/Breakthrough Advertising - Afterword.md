# Breakthrough Advertising - Afterword

## Core Argument & Structure

This section isn't Schwartz's own text. It's Brian Kurtz's afterword to the 2017 reprint, plus an appendix of four reproduced Schwartz promotions.

Kurtz's argument has three steps:

1. **It's a book about human behaviour, not copywriting.** His line in the reprint of Breakthrough Advertising: "Human behavior hasn't changed since 1966."
2. **Schwartz never tried to create demand.** Marketers go most wrong when they try to manufacture demand; Schwartz's skill was channelling and directing mass desire.
3. **The text is untouched.** The reprint keeps the 1966 manuscript word for word, because altering a single word would have been unforgivable.

Three short biographical sections follow, each with a lesson. Schwartz played the long game, writing copy in exchange for customer lists rather than fees: it isn't always about the money. He was a sociologist who read everything, including supermarket tabloids. And he was a serious art collector, proof that passion is a long-term condition. Kurtz closes on the idea that creativity lives in the market and the product, and the writer's job is to join the two.

## Frameworks & Mechanics

**Five meta-rules Kurtz draws out:**

1. **Channel, don't create.** Point existing mass desire at your product rather than inventing demand.
2. **Trade talent for the scarcest asset.** Schwartz swapped world-class copy for publishers' mailing lists. Money followed rather than led, and long partnerships beat a string of one-off paydays.
3. **Market immersion.** Put in a minimum of two hours every week learning where your market stands today; skip it and you fall behind for good. Kurtz adds that Schwartz also scanned for markets that didn't exist yet but soon would.
4. **Study broadly.** Look past the product and the buyer to the wider culture: academic journals, business books, fiction, everything.
5. **Commitment shows.** Sooner or later the market can tell when you don't care.

**Boardroom Reports launch letter mechanics:** a compression promise (hundreds of business magazines read for you in half an hour); the generalist-executive self-image; one concrete example instead of abstract claims; escalation by listing fields and sources; an insider layer the mainstream press never prints; an authority stack; a price anchor against a pricier format; a free first issue with cancel-by-invoice; a limited print run and soft close; fascination subheads and an insider-metaphor insert slip.

**Health-book letter mechanics:** a headline that names the mechanism and demonstrates it in the same line; an open-mind qualifier as a pattern interrupt; framing an ancient practice as new; a named practitioner as authority; a believability analogy (a once-mocked therapy now widely used); and reversing the burden of proof (prove or disprove it at our risk) with a bonus kept even on return.

**Psychology-book ad mechanics:** a named typology of difficult people; fascinations pointing to specific pages of the book; an identity split between givers and takers; renaming the problem as robotic living; an anti-willpower mechanism (unlearn rather than force or dig up the past); and a paradoxical warning to raise curiosity, with a low price and short guarantee. Its reproduced text has scanning errors.

**Advertorial mechanics:** a highly specific first-person survival story; numbered "puzzles" as fascinations; instructions sending you to specific pages; anti-selling framing; an unlimited guarantee.

## Examples & Evidence

- **The publisher alliance:** Kurtz says Schwartz's partnerships with Boardroom and two health publishers produced very large sums and millions of books sold, with control packages that kept running long after he died.
- **The four promotions** show information compression as the product promise, mechanism plus authority plus risk reversal in health mail, an enemy typology with fascinations, and a self-promotional testimonial advertorial.

## Modern Validity 2026

**Still works as-is:** "channel, don't create" is the tightest summary of Meta in 2026. Broad targeting works, with the creative doing the targeting, because the auction finds and rewards existing desire; ads that try to educate demand first lose on price. The two-hour weekly immersion habit is worth more than ever: primary sources (forums, comments, reviews, customer calls) are what generic AI research can't replace, and reading the feeds your audience actually reads is today's tabloid habit. The appendix mechanics work on any platform.

**Needs translating:** swapping customer lists no longer fits modern practice. The modern versions are newsletter cross-promotions, co-marketing, affiliate deals and content-for-distribution, and the strategy behind them still holds. AI summarisation has commoditised "we read thousands of sources for you"; what stays defensible is access, curation and judgement. Virality activates latent desire faster but still doesn't create it.

**Risky:** Cash-on-delivery coupons and tax-deductible promises are dated, and a "limited print run" claim must be true.

## Swipe-Worthy Language Patterns

- **Compression headline:** [huge volume] in [tiny time].
- **Insider metaphor:** reading this is like having a relative inside [the institution].
- **Format price anchor:** in [premium format] it would cost [big number]; in this format, a fraction.
- **"If you don't have an open mind, stop reading here."**
- **"Prove it or disprove it at our risk."**
