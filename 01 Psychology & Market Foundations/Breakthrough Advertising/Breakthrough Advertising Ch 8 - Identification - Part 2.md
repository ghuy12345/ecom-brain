# Breakthrough Advertising Ch 8 - Identification - Part 2

Continues [[Breakthrough Advertising Ch 8 - Identification - Part 1]].

## Frameworks & Mechanics (continued)

**11. Working with the primary image.** First diagnose it (a cigarette reads as masculine, a piston ring as precision-made, an appliance as time-saving). Then either change its intensity, dramatising it if favourable and dimming it if negative or neutral, or treat it as a connecting step toward more appealing images. Never try to replace it; accepted images can't be contradicted.

**12. Stacking images consistently.** The only limit on adding images is that the primary image stays the base and every new image is consistent with its broadest meaning. Multi-image symbols reach fringe buyers and deepen everyone's bond.

**13. Making a dull, parity product glamorous.**
1. Use up the practical benefits before anything else.
2. List the existing images and the main situation the product is used in, which supplies imagery of its own.
3. Keep every required element but glamorise and dramatise each one as far as it will go.
4. Reframe the situation: move the same action from a problem context (repair) to an aspiration context (performance upgrade).

**14. The "corny" test.** Spelled out in words, role images trigger disbelief, the feeling that something is corny. Shown visually and implied, they slip in unnoticed and are accepted. Imply roles; don't state them.

**15. The believability test.**
- *Question 1:* Do people already believe the product has these traits, and can they see themselves, their life, where they stand, or the rung just above, in it? If both are yes, use the image directly.
- *Question 2:* If not, which other accepted image can serve as a bridge from what they believe now to the target image?
- *Disbelief spreads:* an unbelievable identity also poisons the performance claims.
- *The chasm rule:* ask the prospect to leap a believability gap and the ad fails; start the bridge on their side and they'll follow you almost anywhere.

**16. Identity in the physical product.** Look at appearance, components and technical background. Borrow dramatic images from the product's extreme forms (race-car styling on a family car) or from outside the category; for liquids and powders every image is borrowed. Where origins or parts carry drama, quality or credibility, show them in the product, pack or ad.

## Examples & Evidence

- **Marlboro:** the core case for intensifying and then stacking. Masculinity shown three ways (rugged men, demanding situations, the tattoo as a creative gamble), then other images layered on that base: success, adventure, romance, mystery, culture. Formal or yachting imagery only worked after the masculine base was established.
- **Chesterfield's women's campaign:** the brand's accepted masculine image was kept and acknowledged, then used as a bridge into a shared scene that carried acceptance through to the call to action.
- **A deodorant soap** pitched elite-society imagery to a mass market; doubt leaked into its product claims and killed sales.
- **A piston-ring thought experiment:** a full glamorisation of a dull product via a sports-car workshop and an expert-advises-expert frame, recast as an upgrade instead of a repair.
- **Micrin mouthwash:** packaging shaped like medical glassware with an icy-blue liquid made the product read as clinical and effective; the container itself was the ad's best illustration.

## Modern Validity 2026

**Still works as-is:** the identity economy has sharpened the two-dimensions idea. Parity is the norm in direct-to-consumer beauty (same actives, same manufacturers), so the role settles the choice. Bridging from the existing image explains why matching creator content beats polished leaps. The belief check and the one-rung-up limit work as a rule for casting creators; the way doubt spreads explains why relatable creators beat celebrities.

**Needs translating:** under broad targeting the creative is the targeting, so each role becomes its own test angle, and niche segments are now the norm. Discovery runs on review and comment mining. Status has moved from owning things to showing experiences. Refresh the symbol vocabulary (AI, biotech, "powered by" components). The corny test is a good QA filter against AI copy that states role claims outright, and overly perfect AI imagery can break the believability bridge.

**Dated:** tobacco cases are structural demos only. The 1966 role catalogue, especially "wife" as the main female role and a strict masculine/feminine axis, is unusable; keep the mechanics, re-survey the content for each audience.

**Risky:** implied image claims still count toward the overall claim and need support under cosmetic-claim rules; a medical look for a cosmetic or over-the-counter product invites challenges. Gender stereotypes as positioning draw complaints.

## Swipe-Worthy Language Patterns

- **"Not X, but Y" definitions:** not care, but self-respect.
- **Prison walls and the bridge** as pitch images against wishful positioning.
- **Three-beat staccato verdict** from concrete to conclusion, ideal for pack-shot overlays.
- **"It came from [Y], not [X]"** origin contrast.
