# Breakthrough Advertising Ch 7 - Intensification - Part 1

Part 2 covers campaign-level intensification, examples and modern use: [[Breakthrough Advertising Ch 7 - Intensification - Part 2]].

## Core Argument & Structure

This chapter opens the technique half of the book and names intensification as the first process of persuasion. Mass desire powers every sale, and selling means expanding it: wider (more people) and deeper (sharper, bigger) until it beats scepticism, inertia and price. Advertising, for Schwartz, is the literature of desire; wishes are only hazy feelings inside the prospect, and you give them shape, goal and strength. You write the script for their daydream.

Two guardrails run through everything:

1. The reader takes away one main idea and one dominant image per ad. Intensity comes from new perspectives on that one idea, not more messages.
2. Each fresh presentation of the same promise adds weight; literal repetition bores. In Breakthrough Advertising: "You cannot repeat, but you can reinforce."

Intensification is presenting a series of fresh and different fulfilments of the prospect's dominant desire. The chapter covers scope and media, then 13 techniques each shown with full mail-order copy (mail order must close the sale in one ad, so the techniques are easiest to see there), then applies it all to national campaigns.

## Frameworks & Mechanics

**Scope and medium.** How far you can intensify depends on space and on how many angles you have before it feels repetitive. Cramped media (catalogue, small space, radio, TV) force compression into key words, or a campaign that repeats key words across varied ads. Two forces limit every ad: competitors' ads (sophistication applies to body copy too) and your own wording (anything you say a second time needs a different angle, or you lose the reader mid-ad).

**The 13 techniques.**

1. **Present the claim directly:** a thorough, concrete description of how it looks or what it achieves.
2. **Put it in action:** go beyond appearance and results to the precise way it works.
3. **Bring in the reader:** place them inside the action and walk them through their first day of ownership, with concrete time anchors.
4. **Show how to test it:** turn the demo into a test the reader imagines running: a baseline, an action, the expected change, a deadline.
5. **Stretch benefits over time:** follow the product across weeks or months, a continuous flow of benefits building to a payoff.
6. **Bring in an audience:** other people supply fresh viewpoints, through testimonials, narration or case histories with varied, specific results.
7. **Show experts approving:** nothing persuades like experts being surprised, ideally including them admitting they could hardly believe it.
8. **Compare and contrast:** set the old option's drawbacks next to the new one's advantages, often with "instead of" triples.
9. **Show the dark side too:** irritate the wound, then apply the salve. Two streams of motivation, pushing away from the old problem and pulling toward your solution, through story, mockery of the old product, or a side-by-side comparison point by point.
10. **Show the ease:** every point where the product touches the buyer's life (cost, where to get it, how simple it is, how long it lasts, how portable, upkeep, even unboxing) is another fresh angle.
11. **Use metaphor, analogy, imagination:** go beyond raw fact. A classic English-course ad personified the product as a patient mentor who never laughs at you.
12. **Summarise with a catalogue:** a fast list of performances or uses without elaboration. Horizontal catalogues list application after application; vertical ones pile desire upon desire. The body is a rifle shot at one appeal; the closing catalogue is a legitimate last-minute hedge.
13. **Put the guarantee to work:** make it the climax, a final summary of performances, each backed by the guarantee. Two shapes: an escalating, numbered conditional guarantee, or the guarantee written as a usage scenario, plus reframing price per unit.

**When to stop.** The list is open. Keep adding perspectives as long as each one builds the dominant desire; drop any that isn't different or dramatic enough. Reread the ad days later to find where reinforcement turns into repetition.
