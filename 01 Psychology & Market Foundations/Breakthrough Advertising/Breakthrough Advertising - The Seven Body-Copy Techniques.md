# Breakthrough Advertising - The Seven Body-Copy Techniques

The second half of Breakthrough Advertising is a body-copy toolkit of seven techniques. They're organised by the three dimensions of the prospect's mind from chapter 6: desires, identifications and beliefs. Desire and identity can't close a sale without belief.

**Mapping:** intensification serves desire; identification serves identity; gradualization, redefinition, mechanization, concentration and camouflage all serve belief. Intensification is the first process of persuasion and camouflage the last.

## 1. Intensification

**What it is:** presenting a series of fresh, different fulfilments of the prospect's dominant desire. One main idea and one dominant image per ad; you can't repeat, but you can reinforce.

**How:** 13 techniques, from direct description and demonstration through future pacing, tests, experts and contrast to a closing catalogue and a guarantee as climax.

**When:** at all awareness levels apart from Most Aware. Sophistication applies inside the body too.

**2026:** fixed core message, rotating hooks.

## 2. Identification

**What it is:** a second, quieter longing, for self-expression rather than satisfaction, which the writer actively builds into the product.

**How:** discover which roles the market accepts, rejects and finds most compelling; magnify the chosen role until it's nearly irresistible; diagnose the product's primary image and intensify, dim or bridge from it, but never contradict it; then test believability (is it believed, and do buyers see themselves in it?) and find a bridge image if not.

**When:** the main weapon when products are at parity, and the strategy for the final sophistication stage.

**2026:** cast creators who match the current image rather than a glossy leap, and use the "corny" test to catch AI copy that states role claims outright.

## 3. Gradualization

**What it is:** starting from facts the prospect already accepts and leading them step by step to more distant ones. It shapes structure, not content.

**How:** strengthen a statement through its content or its position, then use six devices: the inclusion question, detailed identification, contradicting a false belief (only with authority), the language of logic, syllogistic chains, and other belief forms such as if-then and echoed proof.

**When:** wherever belief is low. Awareness becomes readiness to accept; even a slight breach of an existing belief kills the ad.

**2026:** advertorials as belief bridges, quiz funnels as mechanised yes-ladders.

## 4. Redefinition

**What it is:** giving the product a new definition (this, not that) to remove an objection before the prospect notices it.

**How:** three obstacles with three answers: too complicated → simplify; not important enough → escalate (shift the benefit, hinge a big result on it, show how often it's needed); too expensive → switch the comparison to a pricier standard. The fastest route is flipping the flaw into proof of strength.

**When:** wherever a specific product drawback exists, at any stage. Redefine first, then show the mechanism of the new simplicity.

**2026:** flip one drawback into an asset for each hero product; use real comparison values, never invented "was" prices.

## 5. Mechanization

**What it is:** verbal proof of how the product works, answering "how does it work?"

**How:** name the mechanism (known), describe it as promise then reason why (unknown), or feature it in the headline (saturated market). Known means a word is enough; unsure means describe; strong means sell it hard.

**When:** awareness sets the dose; high sophistication pushes the mechanism up into the headline, matching the new-mechanism stage.

**2026:** mechanism headlines are standard in crowded direct-to-consumer markets. Find the real mechanism; don't invent one.

## 6. Concentration

**What it is:** pointing out competitors' weaknesses and proving your product delivers without them. Copy sells a route to satisfying a desire, not a product.

**How:** never name a weakness without the fix. Two structures: interwoven bad-good pairs, or a time sequence of what happens now versus after switching. It compresses to "[result] without [drawback]."

**When:** only as the underdog with a smaller budget whose prospects already buy from the rival; never as the market leader.

**2026:** comparison tables and "I tried everything" sequences. Attacking the category is legally safer than naming brands.

## 7. Camouflage

**What it is:** borrowing believability from places where trust is already stored so the ad blends in.

**How:** adopt the host's format, adopt its phrasing (date, place, byline), or adopt a mood (understatement, or deadly sincerity that names your own flaws).

**When:** grows in value with ad blindness, roughly at the final sophistication stage.

**2026:** advertorials, creator content and whitelisting. Full disguise is hidden advertising and breaks labelling rules; deadly sincerity is the one variant fully compatible with disclosure.

## Final Touches: weaving it together

The closing chapter binds the seven into one ad: **verification** (proof where the reader asks for it), **reinforcement** (coupled images and damaging admissions), **interweaving** (promise, image and logic in every sentence), **sensitivity** (turn the copy when the reader has had enough), **momentum** (linking phrases and open loops) and **mood** (invisible emotion words, read-aloud rhythm).
