# Seasonal Shifts in Dominant Desires

## What it is
The desires on people's minds change through the year. **Seasonal desire planning** notes which two or three desires tend to dominate at different times of year, which one to open with, which to blend in, and any spending patterns behind it. It is a diagnostic lens, not a targeting rule or a posting schedule. Your core evergreen desires stay the foundation; the seasonal view tells you how the mood around them is shifting.

A loose hypothesis behind some of the pattern: longer, sunnier months tend to bring fewer, bigger purchases (trips, cars), while darker months bring more small "pick-me-up" purchases and gifts. Treat this as a lens, not a law.

Q4 sale tactics are covered in [[Q4 Planning Timeline and Checklist]]; this note is about the underlying desires.

## When to use it
- When a month underperforms and nothing in the account explains it (a strong December followed by a soft January, or a slump in late summer).
- When planning variations of existing winners or reviving old ads in a seasonal tone.
- When deciding how early to start event-driven messaging.

## How (steps)
1. **Check the seasonal map before blaming creative.** If performance drops, consider what is going on for your customers this month and whether your message moved with them.
2. **Lead with the month's dominant desire, layer a second.** Use the outline below as a starting point.
   - *Late summer:* a spending hangover. People spend freely through summer, then see their bank statements at the end of August and cut back. Softer demand is normal.
   - *September:* another new-beginning month. Open with control (structure, routine, learning), blend in status (the parent or professional others admire). Parents feel relief as school resumes; some feel the empty-nest moment, which opens "now it's your turn" messaging. Reported US spending runs to about $127 billion in September, with around $88 billion on back-to-college. A reported 67% of people buying for the new school year have begun by early July, so run that theme in July rather than at the start of September.
   - *October:* the leading desires are status, belonging and control. Halloween behaves like a full season starting in late September. Even unrelated brands can speak to looking good at events, self-expression and dressing the home. Enjoying a safe scare is really about comfort. Reported figures: $12.2 billion of Halloween spending in 2023, 77% of people buying decorations, 69% buying costumes (about $3.8 billion), 47% starting before October, and spend on decorations 42% higher in 2024 than in 2019.
   - *November:* belonging, comfort and control lead, plus value-seeking as deal hunting begins and gift lists form. Cold messaging like "add this to your list" gets ahead of the sale rush.
   - *December:* togetherness and festivity; the emotional high point of spending (a reported $240 billion), mostly on gifts for others.
   - *January:* control, health and status take over. Fresh-start energy, financial discipline after holiday overspending and a sharp rise in health spending, a reported 35-40% increase across vitamins, supplements and prevention. Health and supplement brands should keep pressure on through December and into January.
   - *February:* belonging around Valentine's Day. Gifts must ship, so start around mid-January. Non-romantic products can pivot to self-appreciation or appreciating a friend. Reported: 56% of people intend to mark the day, spending $188 each on average; the 2025 total reached $27.5 billion, a new high (from $25.8 billion), and a record 32% plan gifts for friends and 19% for coworkers.
   - *Spring to early summer:* action and renewal, remembrance, and achievement and parent-themed occasions.
3. **Consider the inverse.** When the season pushes routine, some people resist it ("we miss summer too"). That resistance is a marketable feeling as well.
4. **Apply it lightly.** Rework current winners, or bring back retired ads, in the new seasonal mood, rather than building a seasonal campaign from scratch.
5. **Start early for shipped events.** Start event messaging at least a month early wherever shipping is involved. Gifts are bought ahead *for* the occasion, not *on* the day itself.
6. **Verify the data.** If you build your own seasonal map with AI research, check its sources and figures before relying on them.

## Example
Say a supplement brand has a strong December and assumes January will be quieter, so it trims spend. Under the seasonal view, January is one of its best months: health and fresh-start desires peak. A better plan is to prepare January variations of December winners that swap gifting language for new-year control and health framing. Meanwhile, a jewellery brand facing a soft October could lean into "looking your best at every event this season" rather than a costume theme.

## Common mistakes
- Slapping a generic seasonal label on an ad and expecting results; a weak ad stays weak whatever the season.
- Abandoning evergreen desires in favour of a seasonal one.
- Running seasonal pushes at the last minute.
- Letting the seasonal map overcomplicate things for a beginner; it is a secondary layer.
