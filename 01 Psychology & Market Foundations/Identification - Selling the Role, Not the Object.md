# Identification - Selling the Role, Not the Object

## What it is
Identification shows customers the person they would like to be, or would like others to see. Instead of relieving a problem, it feeds the need for recognition and self-expression. Products are bought as parts to play, not only as things. It is the dominant strategy at the top stage of market sophistication, and it amplifies desire for every other kind of product too.

Two observations make it work:
- People push back when you try to change their beliefs, yet happily step into an identity they admire. When the identity moves, the beliefs move with it.
- Identity is communicated mostly without words: environment, casting, styling. A luxury car already suggests who drives it; cast the wrong person at the wheel and viewers reject the scene instantly.

Inside an ad, desire decides the subject, identity decides the imagery, and belief decides how the case is built. Fashion, accessories and jewellery depend on it, since there is nothing in them to "fix". Identity strategy for underserved avatars is covered in [[Underserved Avatars and Identity Positioning]].

## When to use it
- Your category has nothing to fix, or buyers have stopped trusting claims.
- Planning a shoot, choosing creators or writing hooks.
- A product-aware ad needs extra pull: "if this is what you believe, this was made for you."

## How (steps)
**Ask four questions before shooting**
1. Where does the ideal customer want to be? That sets the environment.
2. How do they want to look? That decides who is on screen.
3. Which feeling are they after (nostalgia, pride, success)? That decides the story.
4. How do they want others to see them? That decides the expression and role.

**Pick an identity lever** (the strongest ads stack several)
- *Aspiration:* the future self. Show who they could become first, with the product as the way there.
- *Tribe:* the group they belong to. Centre the message on a movement or community the buyer can join.
- *Enemy:* what the group refuses. Name the thing the tribe stands against, such as chemical-heavy products or wasteful consumerism.
- *Threat or status:* a risk to the group, or a marker of rank within it.

**Write identity headlines**
1. Identity plus emotion: a group and its reaction.
2. Identity plus threat: a group and a danger or loss.
3. Identity plus betrayal: a trusted party turning on the group.
4. Identity plus curiosity: "why more [group] are quietly switching to [new way]".
Combined, identity plus conflict plus emotion plus curiosity is very potent. Take a plain benefit hook such as "this ingredient is great for skin". An identity version speaks to a group (men who take their appearance seriously) choosing it; a movement version frames a wider shift away from harsh chemical products. A stacked ad can open on aspiration, introduce the enemy midway, invite the viewer into the group and finish on the transformation.

**Keep the stretch realistic.** What the product genuinely offers today and the role you promise must be on a believable level. Tie it to an identity buyers already hold, then to the one they aspire to.

## Example
A classic case: an early instant coffee was pitched as a time-saver and stalled, because its intended buyers took pride in careful home-making and saw the shortcut as lazy. Reframed as a way to look after the family better and free time for what matters, the same product grew strongly. A supplement brand stacks quieter cues: doctor-designed (credible), filmed in a calm, modern home (health-conscious), with a cheerful family around it (caring parents), while the actual benefit is just a simple daily capsule. A men's natural soap brand shoots its presenter outdoors among pine trees to match its all-natural positioning.

## Common mistakes
- **Clashing with how the audience sees itself,** as in the coffee case.
- **Absurd over-stretch:** a mass-market deodorant linked to billionaires, or a luxury-car driver placed in a minivan.
- **Casting someone the audience cannot see themselves in.** A creator who looks, dresses and talks like the target, filmed in their environment, beats a polished but mismatched presenter.
- **Assuming premium means flashy.** Understated, well-made and quiet products serve a distinct identity of their own.
- Treating identity as a slogan rather than something shown.
