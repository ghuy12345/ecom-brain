# Forces Behind Mass Desire - Instincts, Technology Problems and Change

## What it is
Mass desire (see [[Mass Desire - Channel It, Don't Create It]]) does not appear from nowhere. Two families of force produce it:

**Permanent forces** - apply to anyone, in any era.
- *Six mass instincts:* health and survival; sex (being attractive, finding a partner, feeling wanted); status (respect, importance, being ahead); belonging (fitting in, not being rejected); control (predictability, being in charge); comfort (an easier life, less pain and effort). People can feel instincts on behalf of others, such as parents and pet owners.
- *Six mass technology problems:* every advance creates new problems, and each comes with a feeling you can write to:

| Problem | Feeling it creates |
|---|---|
| Complexity | feeling stupid |
| Overwhelm (too many options or distractions) | anxious |
| Fragility (fails when needed) | frustrated |
| Maintenance (constant fixing, updating, paying) | worn out |
| Incompatibility | trapped |
| Obsolescence | cheated |

**Forces of change** - change the *route* people take to satisfy permanent desires.
- *Style trends:* slow shifts over years, not short spikes. Status used to be earned in your neighbourhood; today it can be earned in front of millions online.
- *Mass education:* what society has newly learned. Teaching an entire market something new is a huge job; teaching one small group about one specific desire is achievable. Instinct crossed with new education often produces the biggest breakthroughs.

The instinct stays constant. Only the route to satisfying it changes.

## When to use it
- Building avatars: start from desires and instincts, not demographics. People decades apart in age can want exactly the same thing, while a label like "mothers aged 30 to 45" gives you nothing to write to.
- Diagnosing why one headline beat a near-identical one.
- Repositioning a product in a crowded category.

## How (steps)
1. **Ladder up to the instinct.** For any stated want, keep asking what is behind it until you reach a feeling (call friends, then stay in touch, then belong).
2. **Build a desire sheet** for the product: relevant instincts, technology problems, trends and pieces of mass education.
3. **Pair one driver with one force.** Combine one instinct or technology problem with one trend or piece of education, then draft a headline that carries both at once. Do not grind through every combination.
4. **Work backward more than forward.** Take headlines that already win and name the instinct and trend each one rides. Patterns across winners teach more than a filled matrix.
5. **Test elevation.** Rewrite the same product at each instinct level and test which converts. Elevation means moving the product onto a more powerful desire than the one the category usually sells, such as selling on health or status where everyone else sells on control.
6. **Consider moving down instead.** If a category is packed with health claims, positioning on comfort, control or attraction may face fewer rivals. You trade some urgency and possibly price, but being big in a quieter desire can beat being tiny in the loudest one.
7. **Diagnose by feeling.** For each headline ask which instinct it triggers, then record results, including failures.

A rough working order of instinct strength is health first, then status and sex close together, then comfort, control and belonging. Treat it as a heuristic; a lower rank does not make an instinct weak. Money is not itself an instinct, yet it serves almost all of them.

## Example
Imagine a dog-harness brand writing to every level. Belonging: a label like "responsible owners". Comfort: stress-free walks. Control: take charge of every walk. Status: the harness few owners know about. Health: the education that pulling on a collar can damage a dog's windpipe, framed as a pointed question about why owners still use collars A near-identical line calling collars "outdated" leans on status instead of health, and could perform quite differently; knowing why is the lesson. A blackout-blind brand could pair complexity with a no-tools install, or health with education about light and sleep.

## Common mistakes
- Describing the avatar only by age and gender.
- Forced elevation that feels manipulative (printer paper pitched as a way to find romance) or elevation in the wrong direction (selling a prestige watch on how handy it is for telling the time cheapens it).
- Claiming expert, vet or celebrity endorsement you do not have. Reporting a real category association is fine; inventing one is a legal risk.
- Analysis paralysis: filling the matrix mechanically instead of feeling how the words land.
- Chasing a desire people cannot actually satisfy.
