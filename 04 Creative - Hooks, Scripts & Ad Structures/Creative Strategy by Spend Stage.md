# Creative Strategy by Spend Stage

## What it is
A staged model for what your creative work should be solving at each level of monthly spend. Brands that stall usually have a stage problem rather than a people problem: they are running the system that suits a different spend level. Adding headcount does not fix that; changing the system does.

Any ad account can be viewed as four stacked layers, each a bigger unlock with more risk than the one before:

| Level | Size of unlock | Risk | Typical lifespan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ad | smallest | lowest | shortest |
| Angle | medium | medium | medium |
| Audience | large | high | longest |
| Offer | biggest | highest | — |

The governing rule: crack the lower level before working on the next. Don't test audiences or angles before individual ads are winning, and don't open new offers before audiences are solved. Working out of order is one of the most expensive mistakes a brand can make. The exception is the very first stage, where the offer itself must be found.

Monthly figures below are rough boundaries, not laws.

## When to use it
- Deciding what the creative team should focus on this quarter.
- Planning hires and test budgets.

## How (steps)
**Stage 1, up to roughly $10K a month: find the hero offer.** Test packaging and presentation (bundles, price points, buy-one-get-one, positioning), not audiences or angles, sending traffic to a simple product page; dedicated landing pages wait until an angle is proven and spend is meaningful. Know your unit economics; aim for steady daily sales at break-even first, profit second. Nearly all spend is testing. The founder usually does everything, using cheap formats: UGC, testimonials, before-and-afters, founder clips.

**Stage 2, roughly $10K to $100K: win at the ad level.** Commit to a single audience and angle. Build the habit of multiplying winners (variations, improved versions, fresh concepts around a proven idea), with clear rules for when an ad is cut, trimmed or promoted. Put roughly a fifth of spend into testing. Keep a record of failures so nothing is retested by accident, and use competitor research to save budget. Target: between one and five fresh winners a week.

**Stage 3, roughly $100K to $500K: open new angles.** Keep the first winning angle profitable while finding three to five more. Angles tend to fall into a few loose families: practical benefits (a concrete problem solved, few steps, little time), feelings (how you'll feel or look, closeness to people you love), who the buyer wants to be (belonging, status, "the kind of person who...") and objections or the cost of waiting (saving money, time lost by delaying). Most audiences lean toward one family; find it before diversifying. Testing budget drops to around 10 to 15 percent. Track performance by angle.

**Stage 4, roughly $500K to $1.5M: add audiences.** Expand horizontally: same offer, new buyer context, such as people buying the product as a gift for the existing customer. Keep the core audience on 80 to 90 percent of spend, track every audience separately, and recycle proven angles into the new ones. Only open a new audience once the first has three to five profitable angles running at once. Testing falls to 5 to 10 percent.

**Stage 5, beyond that: new offers and markets.** Run the same machine on a second hero offer, or pull three levers: new countries and languages, lifetime value (upsells, subscriptions, new products), and order value (bundles that justify a higher acquisition cost). Localise in order: launch existing winners unchanged, add translated voiceover or subtitles to the best ones, see what keeps winning per country, and reshoot with local creators last. 

**At every stage, design for headroom.** Ask whether each ad could plausibly carry very high daily spend, not just whether it converts. Ads built for a tiny slice put a ceiling on the account.

**Move up only when the current stage's win condition holds consistently.**

## Example
Say a pet-supplement brand spends about $60K a month and wants to test owners of senior dogs and a new "calm" angle simultaneously. The model says stage 2: one audience, one angle (joint mobility for active dogs), and the job is a weekly flow of new winning ads within it. Six months later at $250K a month with four winners a week, the team opens an emotional angle ("more years of walks together") and a critical one ("cheaper than one vet visit") while protecting the original. The older-dog audience waits until several angles are profitable at the same time.

## Common mistakes
- Hopping categories or chasing audiences before an offer sells consistently.
- Opening angles or audiences while the ad level is still unsolved.
- Changing angles mid audience test.
- Blending metrics across audiences or offers so one quietly subsidises another.
- Launching several new offers before the first runs like a system.
- Translating everything for a new market rather than just the winners.
- Costly productions before anyone has proven the offer sells.
