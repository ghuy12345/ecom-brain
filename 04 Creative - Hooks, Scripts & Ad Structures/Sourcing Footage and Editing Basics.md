# Sourcing Footage and Editing Basics

## What it is
The practical side of getting video ads made: finding clips, editing them and deciding who does the editing. The most common and often best-converting video formats are voiceover over B-roll and subtitles over B-roll, so most of your footage problem is supporting clips rather than people speaking to camera.

Speed matters as much as quality. Script-first UGC, where a creator must film every scene, is slow, and you should test a script before investing in it. As rough estimates only, not benchmarks, one team's internal cycle times per video ad were about 6 days with no creator (B-roll only), about 11 days with an in-house creator on loose direction, about 15 days with an in-house creator on a full script, and about 26 days with an external creator. Using footage that already exists gets tests out faster.

## When to use it
- You are about to start producing video and need a footage pipeline.
- Your team keeps waiting on creators and tests are stalling.

## How (steps)
**1. Source footage, in this order of preference:**
1. *Your own footage.* Use brand-owned clips wherever the product appears. Film it yourself whenever you can; it costs nothing.
2. *Licensed stock B-roll.* A subscription library cleared for paid social gets you generic clips quickest (someone walking in snow for a cold-feet script). Search it with the visual instruction from your script. Choose clips with no baked-in text, and vertical ones where you can.
3. *Creator content you pay for.* A creator marketplace gives authentic UGC; it is relatively expensive, so it suits a volume strategy, and good creators there can become future ambassadors. You can also approach creators directly on social platforms and pay them to shoot. A creator on a small monthly retainer can deliver around one video weekly.
4. *AI-generated B-roll.* Increasingly realistic and acceptable for neutral shots (a person pointing, a scene). Avoid AI people delivering "I tried this and loved it" testimonials; that is inauthentic.

Reserve outside footage for scenes without your product: competing solutions, abstract ideas, the problem in everyday life.

**Use your own, stock or purchased footage** rather than clips lifted from creators' posts or documentaries, even if it is only B-roll with no faces.

**2. Handle music properly.** Study trending sounds in your niche and identify unknown tracks with a music-recognition app, then pick a comparable track from a music library.

**3. Pick tools that are good enough.** There is no secret software. A beginner editor such as CapCut or a mid-level one such as Final Cut Pro can produce winning ads; Premiere Pro or After Effects offer more depth long term. A subscription marketplace of templates (zooms, transitions, animated captions), stock, music and graphics saves a great deal of time. Quality comes from repetitions: patterns appear after many edits, and finding the right clip for each line is a skill built the same way.

**4. Build an inspiration system:**
- Your own saved collections of great openers and ads, for days when ideas dry up.
- A shared team thread where people drop an ad with a line on why it's good, checked daily.
- An inspiration tool that lets you organise ads into boards and filter by niche.
- The public ad library for a brand's full set of live ads, since inspiration tools miss some; filter by status, date and media type.

**5. Decide who edits.** Below roughly $20K to $30K of monthly ad spend, a strong editor probably won't join you, so learn the basics yourself; basic software can take you a long way, and competent is enough. Buy back your time later, once someone can be hired for well under your own hourly value. Execution is a major reason ads fail, so whoever edits needs every brief with a visual direction for each line (see [[Hiring and Developing Video Editors]]).

## Example
Say you sell heated insoles and have a script about cold feet on winter commutes. You film the product yourself on a kitchen table and on your own feet. For the commute scenes, you search a licensed stock library for "waiting for bus in snow" and "rubbing cold hands". A voiceover in a free beginner editor, animated captions from a template pack, and a library upbeat track complete the ad in an afternoon.

## Common mistakes
- Waiting weeks for creator footage to test a script that existing clips could have tested.
- Lifting clips from creators or documentaries "because it's only B-roll".
- Using trending tracks lifted from social posts instead of picking one from a music library.
- AI avatars giving fake personal testimonials.
- Hiring an expensive editor before you know what a good ad looks like.
