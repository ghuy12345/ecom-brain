# Static Ad Concept Library and Quick Formats

## What it is
Of all creative types, statics cost least and are quickest to test, and a winning static can outperform most videos in an account. A static squeezes the three parts of a video ad into a single frame:

- **Hook**: stops the scroll and buys about two seconds, usually via the headline.
- **Body**: makes the case with the product, what's in it and evidence.
- **Close**: price anchor, button, real urgency.

Think of each concept below as a component rather than a complete design. Stack two or three in one image, each giving a different reason to buy, and the combinations become practically unlimited.

## When to use it
- Planning a static test batch and wanting variety across concepts, not ten versions of one.
- Finding desires cheaply before investing in video.
- Filling gaps across funnel stages: many accounts only test product-forward statics.

## How (steps)
1. **Decide traffic temperature first.** Cold and warm audiences suit different concepts.
2. **Pick one concept per layer**: one for the hook, one for proof, one for the offer.
3. **Build the image**, then build variations that change one component each.
4. **Cover all stages.** Include a format for each of the top, middle and bottom of the funnel.

**The concept library**
- *Hook-first*: a bold claim (one oversized, specific statement); a problem callout (name the feeling in personal words, up to three problems); a curiosity gap (give away most of the story but keep the payoff back); a pattern interrupt (warning-label or caution-tape visual language); a direct question that makes the right people self-select.
- *Proof-first*: before-and-after; timeline proof (lead with the fastest believable result, precise wording such as "day 10" beats "a couple of weeks"); a testimonial card written the way people actually message each other; a UGC-style static that looks like a story post; a guarantee headline that answers the main objection before it forms.
- *Offer-first* (mostly warm audiences): an authority badge strip (three or four logos at most); a price anchor with a large strikethrough; a bundle stack that signals abundance; urgency with a real reason; a free-gift stack.
- *Education-first*: a listicle (three items is the sweet spot, five the maximum); how-it-works diagram (one mechanism per ad); a "hack" framed as advice; an ingredient spotlight; us vs them (including against the material or habit people use now); a myth-buster.
- *Social-first* (blends in as ordinary content): a meme in an instantly recognisable format; native screenshots (text threads, notes app, raw reviews); an editorial "as featured" frame; a handwritten note (a sticky note, or writing on a relevant object); a deliberately "ugly" drawn ad that costs almost nothing to make.
- *Product and demand*: a variant grid showing many colours, shifting the question to "which one?"; a sell-out or restock story framed as news; a humble-brag apology for selling out.

**Temperature rules**: problem callouts, curiosity, UGC-style, guarantees, hacks and most education and social concepts work cold. Offer-first concepts, variant grids and "still thinking about it?" questions belong in retargeting.

**Quick formats by funnel stage**
- *Top* (problem aware): text-only statics with the persona and problem named very clearly, because with no product image the copy is all the algorithm can use to find people; listicles of warning signs; an open letter to the persona; a notes-app "note to self"; a forum or review screenshot about the problem, not the product.
- *Middle* (solution and product aware): "me vs me" before-and-after carrying an identity-level change; a timeline; a stats lineup; a strike-out list of pains or alternatives; a label-transparency static for buyers who compare ingredients; a Venn diagram with the product at the overlap; one benefit word repeated large behind the product.
- *Bottom* (offer): the product plus the offer word, a clean buy-one-get-one display, or a comment box with the product forward.

**Proven stacks**: press logos + a colour grid + a strikethrough price; problem callout + timeline + price anchor; before-and-after + guarantee headline + timeline; ingredient spotlight + myth-buster.

## Example
Say you sell a collagen powder to cold traffic. Stack: problem callout ("Crepey neck skin at 45?") on top, timeline proof in the middle ("Week 2: softer. Week 6: visibly firmer"), and a guarantee line at the bottom. Variation 2 swaps the hook for a myth-buster ("Why your collagen isn't working"). Variation 3 swaps the timeline for a label-transparency shot. Each test changes one block, so you learn which reason to buy carries the ad.

## Common mistakes
- One concept per image when two or three would give more reasons to buy.
- Polished testimonials that read like billboards.
- Fake or unexplained urgency, which trains buyers to ignore you.
- Crowding the image with logos or a long list.
- Mimic formats (story posts, text messages) with the wrong fonts or layout, which kills the authenticity.
- Leading a timeline with the slowest result.
