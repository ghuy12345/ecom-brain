# The Weekly Creative Production Loop

## What it is
Consistent winners come from a repeatable process, not inspiration. The weekly loop runs the same five steps in the same order every week, so finding a winning ad becomes a predictable output rather than a lucky one:

1. Analyse data
2. Script
3. Edit and design
4. Quality-check
5. Launch and test

Two beliefs hold it together. First, budget that buys a fair test is not lost: you get either sales or information. Second, doing everything right at too low a volume still fails; output matters, as long as each ad is made with intent.

## When to use it
- Any brand producing ads every week, from a solo founder to a full team.
- When winners arrive in random bursts and you cannot explain why.
- When one step (usually analysis or QA) keeps getting skipped.

## How (steps)
**1. Analyse.** Look at three groups of numbers:
- *Performance*: which angles and concepts get spend and hit target cost per purchase.
- *Video*: attention (three-second views divided by impressions), hold (completed or long views divided by impressions) and the retention curve in quarters.
- *Soft signals*: script and execution quality, comments.
Judge groups of ads rather than single ads, over a sensible window (a weak day doesn't make a weak ad), and allow for seasonality. Review as many ads as you can, losers included: why did it work, and did it hit the right desire and avatar? Keep a change log of account edits so you can explain swings, and after a scale-up give the account three or four days to settle before judging.

**2. Script.** Assemble proven message components in a set sequence (see [[Cold-Audience Video Ad Script Structure]]). Write at least four hooks per ad, because a good opener can be moved onto a new body if the first one flops. Include visual examples so the talent and editor know what you mean. Start cheap: use image ads to find which desires land, then invest in video for the ones that do. Keep product-aware videos short, around 15 to 30 seconds.

**3. Edit and design.** If the script mentions it, show it. Use variety and several faces, with transitions that do not distract. Thumbnails and audio both matter even though many people watch muted. Don't let lighting or set design scream "advert". Spend the most effort on the opening seconds.

**4. QA.** Check spelling, visual quality and brand compliance. QA prevents cheap-looking mistakes; it is not perfectionism, and a typo should never delay a launch by weeks.

**5. Launch and test.** Give each concept its own ad set so tests are fair, name everything so ads are recognisable without a preview, and launches on the same weekday. Don't read data too early (attribution lags; winners can appear as late as day six), don't kill ads on early numbers, and don't scale winners too fast.

**Then close the loop.** Feed what you learned into next week's analysis. Fix the weakest step first; a great scripting process cannot compensate for no analysis.

**Throughput benchmarks (rough):**
- A designer needs about 15 to 30 minutes per static; an editor 45 to 60 minutes per video.
- For everyday iterations, an editor can reasonably turn out three or four video concepts daily.
- Brands scaling into very high daily spend often aim for about one genuinely new concept a day, on top of iterations.
- Track volume and hit rate together; neither alone tells you whether the loop is healthy.

**Don't over-engineer structure.** On a budget of a few hundred dollars daily, choose one test method and focus on creative; a consistent weekly review rhythm matters more than campaign architecture.

## Example
Say a skincare brand runs the loop with one strategist, one editor and a freelance designer.
- *Monday*: review last week's batch. Two statics on "makeup sits better" took most of the spend; a retention dip at 40 percent shows a slow demo section.
- *Tuesday*: scripts for three new videos built around the winning desire, four hooks each, plus six new statics testing a second desire.
- *Wednesday and Thursday*: editing and design, using customer footage and real reviews.
- *Friday morning*: QA checks spelling, claims and brand colours. *Friday afternoon*: launch, one ad set per concept, named consistently.
Next Monday, the loop starts again with that batch's numbers.

## Common mistakes
- Judging individual ads instead of patterns across groups.
- Turning ads off within a day or two out of habit.
- Skipping analysis and producing more of whatever feels right.
- Treating QA as polishing and delaying launches.
- Volume for its own sake: mass-produced ads with no intent behind them.
- Changing several account settings at once with no change log.
