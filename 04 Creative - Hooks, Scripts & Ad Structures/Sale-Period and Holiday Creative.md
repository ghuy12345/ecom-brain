# Sale-Period and Holiday Creative

## What it is
During Black Friday, Cyber Monday and other sale windows, the offer does most of the persuading, and the creative's job shifts: make the deal impossible to miss, make the product obvious to first-time buyers, and reuse what is already proven. Sale creative is mostly about reuse plus clarity, not reinvention. The offers themselves are covered in [[Black Friday and Q4 Offer Strategy]].

## When to use it
- Planning creative for Black Friday, holiday gifting, or any major promotion.
- Deciding how much new production to commission for a sale (usually less than you think).

## How (steps)
**1. Build the evergreen bank first.** In the months before the sale, keep producing evergreen concepts that scale. Every evergreen winner becomes a ready-made sale ad later. Test offers ahead of time on a dedicated page without sale language.

**2. Prepare the main sale formats**, weighted toward reuse:
1. *Early-access lead generation*: ads that collect emails and phone numbers for a VIP sale a day or two before the main event.
2. *Offer callouts*: the product clearly shown with the offer big and bold. Statics often beat video here.
3. *Benefit callouts*: the deal paired with what the product does, for cooler audiences who don't yet know why they'd want it.
4. *Banners on top performers*: your best evergreen ads with scrolling banners or stickers added. Also revive ads that won in the same season last year and add fresh banners; that's a near-free winner. Long educational ads can take a banner too.
5. *Gifting UGC*: creators presenting the product as a gift.
6. *Custom studio content*: the costly one, and often the one with least lift. Heavy custom UGC production for the sale itself rarely pays back.
Other options worth a test: a static turned into a lightly animated video, genuine sell-out or limited-run framing, and partnership ads from creators.

**3. Word the offer clearly.**
- "X% off everything" is clearer than "up to X% off", which earns clicks but annoys shoppers when their chosen item isn't discounted much. "Site-wide" misleads if anything is excluded.
- Only claim "our biggest sale ever" when it genuinely is.
- Name "free" whenever something is free (gift, shipping).
- Make it obvious what the product is; lots of sale shoppers have never heard of you.
- Show variants for multi-product stores, and make the dynamic catalogue ads work.
- Give each bundle item a reason to be there, and make ads specifically for bundles; new customers often feel safer buying a bestseller set.
- Pre-sale scarcity ("limited early run because of demand") works only if the product really sells out.

**4. Put the sale on winners in two different ways and compare:** (a) keep the ad almost as it was, with a small sale badge so the hook still leads; (b) work the deal into the winning headline itself.

**5. Keep campaigns separate.** Don't switch off evergreen campaigns and don't drop sale creative into them; changing the offer inside evergreen disrupts it. Run the sale in its own campaign, launched early enough to gather a couple of weeks of data. Keep testing new creative right up to and during the sale; quality beats clever structure.

**6. Volume and variety.** There is no magic number, but three or four batches a week (roughly 9 to 12 statics) with genuinely different swings is a practical start, since delivery rewards diversity. Creator footage can be cut into several tests by starting each cut at a new sentence, so each opening works as a hook.

**7. Match the store to the ad.** Show total savings on product pages (evergreen plus sale discount), use automatic discounts, and repeat the saving in cart and checkout.

**8. QA every sale ad.** Check the price and percentage match the store, the dates are right, claims like "free" and "biggest" are true, the product is clearly visible, and every link points to your own site.

## Example
Say a candle brand has three evergreen winners. In October it adds two more evergreen concepts and an early-access lead ad. For the sale it overlays "30% off everything, ends Monday" on all five winners, revives last year's best gifting video with a new sticker, and makes a bundle static explaining each scent in the trio ("one for the bath, one for the desk, one to give away"). One headline test keeps the original hook with a small corner badge; another leads with the offer. Evergreen campaigns run untouched alongside.

## Common mistakes
- Spending heavily on new custom video for the sale instead of reusing winners.
- "Up to" percentages that buyers can't find on the product they want.
- Burying a proven hook under a giant sale banner without testing a subtler version.
- Mixing sale ads into evergreen campaigns.
- Engaging brand-story or street-interview ads that never get to the product; sale buyers want the deal and the item.
- Assuming last year's sale winners will still work; few do a year later.
