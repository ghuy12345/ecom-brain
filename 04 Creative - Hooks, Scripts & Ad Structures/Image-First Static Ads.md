# Image-First Static Ads

## What it is
A way to produce statics quickly without starting from a blank page. Instead of writing copy and then hunting for a design, you begin with a visual: a layout or style that catches your eye in a library of validated statics. You then pour copy into that structure, written for two or three specific sub-avatars you found in forums, and finish with a review pass after a break.

Two ideas sit underneath it:
- **Specific beats generic.** A headline naming a habit unique to this person outperforms a vague emotional state. "Chugging water so I don't snack" paints a picture; "struggling with cravings" does not.
- **Message beats polish.** Visual congruence helps, but what the ad says matters far more than how refined it looks.

## When to use it
- You need a static batch fast and have research but no design idea.
- You want to test several sub-avatars against the same layout.
- You have no designer, so copy is the main variable you can control.

## How (steps)
**1. Find sub-avatars and their words.**
- Start from one core desire (for example, managing cravings while dieting) and break it into behaviours.
- Search each behaviour in plain language with "reddit" added ("can't stop snacking at night reddit").
- From the threads, log behaviours, feelings, experiences, rough demographics and verbatim quotes. Rants and "same here" replies are the richest.
- Stop at about three sub-avatars; park the rest for later. Overlap between them is fine.
- Write as ideas arrive. Research and writing run side by side, and you can draft much of a batch from research before involving any AI.

**2. Pick a layout.** Browse an editable template library (not just an inspiration gallery) until a style grabs you. A style such as "comparison" is different from a format. Prefer layouts from outside your industry so you are not copying a direct competitor.

**3. Write into the structure.** Note how the reference's headline, subheading and bullets are built, then rewrite each part for your sub-avatars, for example a trio of headlines for each group plus one shared list of check-mark benefits, at a very easy reading level. An AI assistant can help if you give it the screenshot, product facts, sub-avatars and real quotes (see [[Building Static Ads With an LLM]]). Steer it by naming the lines you like and why.

**4. Apply the specificity test.** Would each headline only make sense to this one person? Rewrite lines like "motivation fades after a week" into concrete moments like "ordering a kids' meal so the portion looks normal". Choose vivid verbs.

**5. Check every claim.** Only claim what the product actually does. Verify any science and statistics against your product document, and avoid definitive "root cause" language or comparisons that would need studies to support.

**6. Build one ad completely first.** Headlines that read well in a doc may not fit the layout. Fix the template once, then produce the rest.

**7. Add congruent imagery if you have time.** An image tool can place your product in a scene that reinforces the headline (a tiny plate for a small-portions line). Ask the tool for the picture alone and assemble the ad yourself. Do not lose a day here; message testing comes first.

**8. Review after a break.** Draft the batch, step away for at least 20 to 30 minutes (overnight is better), then improve the ads you have rather than making more. One workable rhythm: draft briefs on Friday, rest, recheck on Monday before anything goes out. On review:
- Remove headlines too close to ones already in test.
- Reconsider provocative "X is easy" claims that invite comment fights.
- Add strong research quotes you skipped as extra variations.
- Update dates in notes-app or message-style mockups.

**9. Decide what to vary.** Same layout, different sub-avatar headlines is already a big enough swing. Only test different visuals if someone can make each visual match its headline.

## Example
Say you sell an appetite-support supplement. Core desire: get through a diet without constant cravings. Forum research gives three sub-avatars: people who drink water to fill up, people who eat tiny portions yet stall, and people who snack out of boredom. You pick a clean two-column layout from a skincare brand, write three headlines for each group ("Drinking two litres before lunch just to stay full?"), add four check-mark benefits, and generate one scene per group. After lunch you return, drop one headline that duplicates a live test, and add a real forum quote as a tenth variant.

## Common mistakes
- Generic emotional headlines that any brand could run.
- Letting AI-written science go out unchecked.
- Spending hours on image generation before the message is proven.
- Making more ads after the break instead of improving the batch.
- Concluding a sub-avatar is dead because one execution failed.
