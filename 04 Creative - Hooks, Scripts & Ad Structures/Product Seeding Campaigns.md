# Product Seeding Campaigns

## What it is
Seeding means giving product away to lots of creators in return for content you may use. Think of it as buying content with product, not as influencer marketing: you aren't renting anyone's audience, so follower numbers barely matter.

The real goal is easy to miss. It isn't mainly to get a winning ad. It's to get in front of many creators and spot the one or two worth working with for months. A strong creator replaces a writer, camera operator, photographer and editor at once. Any winner from the first round is a bonus.

Seeding suits smaller brands (roughly under $50K a month in revenue or under $10K in ad spend) that can't yet fund a creative team. It is also a product test: if creators won't take your product even at no cost, look hard at the product or offer.

## When to use it
- You need a steady supply of authentic UGC on a small budget.
- You want to find future paid creators or ambassadors.
- You want unprompted, customer-style reactions as research (see [[Talking to Customers - Calls, Immersion and Seeding Feedback]]).

## How (steps)
**1. Build the listing on a creator marketplace.** Seeding normally lives under a reach or awareness objective; some platforms need a paid brand plan.
- Lead the description with a hook, and include photos, the product page and the retail price. Leave automatic invites off.
- Allow plenty of creators, within what your stock and shipping can handle.
- Offer no commission yet; retainers come later, for proven creators.
- The product's value is the creator's whole reward, so items below about $30 draw fewer applicants.

**2. Filter roughly by specs** (country, gender, age bracket like your buyer's, language, content niche), then judge quality by eye.

**3. Ask screening questions** when authenticity matters:
1. In their own words, what angle or problem would their video tackle?
2. Are they happy to talk about the sensitive part of your category? (Saves a refusal later.)
3. Do they agree to their video being run as an ad? (Secure usage rights up front.)
Mark the answer you want for each so you can sort applicants quickly.

**4. Preview your direction.** Share a short taste of the concepts, rules and angles; send the full framework once they're hired (see [[Creator Briefs - Frameworks, Not Scripts]]). You may ask for a photo with the product, but seeding can't make it mandatory.

**5. Hire and confirm details.** Shortlist approved creators, hire them on the platform, then message each personally for a tracking email, their address read back to you (it catches typos and moves) and their pick of product:
- Large range: two favourites, so you have a backup.
- One product in several versions: their preferred colour or size.
- A single item: no choice needed.
People who chose what they received make warmer, more genuine videos.

**6. Put the terms in writing before anything ships.** Each creator confirms it is product only, unpaid, and that a video isn't guaranteed. Anything you later run as an ad must also follow the platform's and your country's rules on disclosing gifted endorsements.

**7. Keep a tracker, even as a one-person team.** Log every applicant, shortlisted and hired creator. Spend a few minutes daily answering messages, saving new videos and adding applicants; nudge quiet creators every two or three days.

**8. Know when to give up on someone.** Hired but nothing shipped, and ten days of silence after several nudges: mark them unresponsive. Shipped, then two weeks of silence despite nudges: raise it with marketplace support.

**9. Know the numbers.** A product creators like can pull dozens of applications in two days; niche items may get a few, and only some turn into hires. Cost per video is roughly the plan fee divided by videos received, plus product, postage and fees; compare that with paying creators per video.

**10. Keep the best.** Run what arrives as ads; usually one video takes most of the spend. Offer a small monthly retainer to anyone who delivers a winner or shows real promise (see [[Brand Ambassadors, Retainers and Influencer Deals]]).

## Example
Say a women's supplement brand seeds a $45 product to women aged 30 to 55 in two countries. Screening asks which life stage they'd speak about and whether they're comfortable discussing hormonal changes. Thirty people apply in two days; eight are hired and six deliver. One honest morning-routine clip becomes the account's top ad, and that creator moves onto a monthly retainer.

## Common mistakes
- Picking creators by audience size.
- Skipping the written no-payment confirmation.
- Copy-pasting the same message to everyone.
- Seeding a product nobody wants and blaming the creators.
- Scripting seeded creators so tightly they sound fake.
