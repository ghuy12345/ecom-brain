# Open Loops - Layering, Chaining and Closing

## What it is
An open loop is an unfinished question planted in the viewer's mind. Opening a loop earns attention; resolving it delivers a small hit of satisfaction that attaches that attention to your message. Each loop is a trade: keep watching and you will get the answer.

Stopping the scroll is not enough. If viewers are gone after the opening seconds, nothing later in the script gets a chance, and a pattern interrupt alone only stops someone briefly. Loops are what turn a stop into a watch. For reference, strong organic creators keep the large majority of viewers through the first six seconds; most ad accounts are far below that, which shows how much room loops leave.

The working system has five steps: generate, place, chain, close, audit.

## When to use it
- Any video script longer than about 15 seconds.
- When hook rate is fine but hold rate drops off a cliff after the hook.
- When viewers watch but landing-page drop-off is high, which often means a loop was over-teased and under-delivered.

## How (steps)
1. **Generate loops.**
   - *Juxtaposition*: list beliefs your avatar holds, then write a line pairing two things that break one of them (getting results by doing the reverse of what everyone recommends).
   - *Free inference*: pair things so the viewer draws the claim themselves ("a plant extract that behaves like a prescription cream"). Handy where ad rules restrict direct claims, but whatever the viewer infers must still be true.
   - *Low-intensity fear*: "three red flags when buying..." Keep it to a warning worth checking, not a prophecy of disaster.
   - *Do the opposite*: if the category shows glamorous models, show an unglamorous real person.
   - Warm-up drill: spend a few minutes force-pairing random nouns and inventing a story that connects them. It trains the reflex before you apply it to your brand.
2. **Place two loops in parallel.** Open the main loop (the big promise) in the hook. At the bridge, open a second loop (a mistake, the mechanism, an objection). While two threads stay open, viewers have no natural moment to leave.
3. **Chain.** Don't let a thread end with nothing left open. As one loop resolves, plant the next: "That's why it works, but before I show you the result, there's one thing almost everyone gets wrong."
4. **Close with a callback.** Toward the end, remind viewers of the opening tease, deliver the answer, and move straight into the call to action. The callback re-raises tension just before release. Placing a loop just before the product reveal lets the product itself be the answer.
5. **Audit with a loop ledger.** List every loop with three details: its content, the point where it opens, and the point where it closes. A loop left hanging leaves a nagging feeling that can quietly cost you the sale.

**Quality gate for every loop:** it sparks a real need to know, ties to something the viewer actually cares about, and sits naturally in the script. Loops bolted on for their own sake read as manipulation.

**Caps:** short ads of 30 to 60 seconds hold at most two loops; longer sales videos at most three. Heavier nesting is for experienced writers only.

**Payoff parity:** the reveal has to match the size of the setup.

## Example
Say you sell a shampoo for thinning hair. A 45-second script might run:
- Hook (main loop opens): "Watch out for this ingredient if your hair is getting thinner."
- Bridge (secondary loop opens): "And no, it's not sulphates. There's a second mistake almost everyone makes in the shower."
- Mechanism (main loop partly resolves): what the ingredient does to the scalp and why your shampoo leaves it out.
- Proof (secondary loop closes): the shower mistake, and how the routine fixes it, shown on a real customer.
- Close (callback): "So, that ingredient? Check your bottle tonight. If it's there, this is what to switch to," then the offer and call to action.

Both loops close, each before the call to action, and the release of tension runs straight into the offer.

## Common mistakes
- Orphaned loops that never close.
- Over-teasing: a giant setup and a tiny reveal erodes trust and appears later as landing-page drop-off.
- Stacking too many loops in a short ad until it feels manipulative.
- Loops unrelated to the viewer's actual interest.
- Using a hook you cannot connect to the product naturally. If the bridge from hook to product feels forced, drop the hook rather than force it.
