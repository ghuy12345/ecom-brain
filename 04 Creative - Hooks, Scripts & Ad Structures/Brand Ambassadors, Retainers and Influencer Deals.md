# Brand Ambassadors, Retainers and Influencer Deals

## What it is
Volume seeding finds creators; ambassador programmes keep the good ones. Many brands shift from sending product to dozens of strangers toward a handful of high-quality creators they train like in-house team members. The payoff is a standing creative capability: you no longer have to find, brief and pay new creators every month, and trained ambassadors start thinking like mini strategists.

## When to use it
- A seeded or paid creator has produced an ad that absorbed meaningful spend.
- You want to approach a larger influencer with a performance-based deal.

## How (steps)
**1. Choose who to sign.** Primarily creators whose ad became a genuine winner (spend absorbed at target, not just nice-looking content). A looser second route: creators whose craft impressed you enough to hire before a proven winner.

**2. Make the offer.**
- *Retainer only*: about one video a week, product supplied, flat monthly fee (a round $500 is a simple default; a rough estimate, not a benchmark), plus access to a team chat. You can leave the rate blank, ask for theirs and meet in the middle.
- *Retainer plus performance*: only for creators committing to two or three videos a week. Add a tiered share of the revenue or ad spend their content generates, stepping down as volume rises and capped monthly. Rough estimate only, not a benchmark: 10 percent of the first $10K, 5 percent of the next $20K, 2.5 percent of the next $40K, 1 percent beyond $70K, with a monthly cap of about $10K on top of the base. Don't keep a flat percentage forever.
- Decide consciously whether pay is based on revenue or on ad spend; depending on ROAS they are very different obligations. Define how attribution to their content is measured.
- Remove objections: they don't need to post on their own profile, you will share breakdowns of winning ads, and other creators are earning well (without overwhelming them with big numbers).
- A monthly bonus for the top ad keeps rising creators motivated; they are often better value than big names.

**3. Keep the agreement minimal and consistent.** State the video cadence and the pay basis, matching exactly what you offered in writing. Include usage and whitelisting or partnership-ad terms. Templates are a starting point only; get legal review.

**4. Onboard properly.** A short deck covers goals, the expected cadence, when they get paid and when they get a results report, how quickly you review and approve, a contact, guidance on openers and what to do first. Then send a welcome note with an analysis of today's best ads, the framework, where to upload, and the schedule. Make sure no template placeholder survives.

**5. Share data openly.** Share a saved analytics report of top ads from the last 30 days with a short walkthrough video, so every ambassador can see what is working.

**6. Communicate casually and stay hands-off.** Use one dedicated chat channel and talk like you would with a teammate. Messages are either a quick alert ("sale starts Friday") or an idea plus the buyer insight that makes it work. You hired creators so you wouldn't direct every step; send the idea and expect a finished video. Give scripts only to those who want them or act well. Assign different ideas to different people so the whole group doesn't make the same video.

**7. Give structured feedback.** Start with what performed, point out what lagged, propose a fix, use the numbers as motivation, and request a single tweak on the same product.

**8. Keep ideas flowing weekly** (sale videos, clearance items, reactions to a founder clip) and log every payment in a simple sheet.

**9. Approaching larger influencers.** Offer a share of revenue directly; roughly 30 percent to them is a typical opening (a rough estimate). What wins the deal is showing why it benefits their followers, not you. Run their content as partnership ads from their profile where possible; platforms increasingly favour these over plain whitelisting. Secure written usage rights for paid ads, because influencers who later dispute usage can file copyright claims and get ads pulled.

## Example
Say a kitchenware brand has one seeded creator whose recipe video absorbed most of last month's UGC spend. She's offered a retainer plus tiered performance pay for two videos a week, signs a one-page agreement covering usage rights and partnership ads, and receives the onboarding deck and a walkthrough of the top ads. In the team chat she gets "sale on Friday, 20 percent off pans" one week and a guided idea the next ("parents who meal-prep on Sundays"). Her second-month video becomes a new top spender, and her monthly payout rises with it.

## Common mistakes
- Signing creators on taste instead of on spend.
- Contracts that don't match the offer email.
- Hand-holding capable creators, or giving none of them data.
- A flat revenue share with no tiers or cap.
- Running influencer content without written usage rights.
