# Building a Creative Team - Strategist, In-House or Agency

## What it is
A staged approach to who makes your ads as the brand grows. An ecommerce brand is essentially a marketing company, so creative capability is the one function you can never fully outsource. The choices are when to stop doing strategy yourself, how to hire well, how to use agencies without depending on them, and how to keep a team learning.

## When to use it
- You are the bottleneck on ideas, scripts and reviews.
- A team exists but results are flat and meetings are passive.

## How (steps)
**1. Be your own strategist early.** Below roughly $1K to $2K of daily spend, handle research, ideas, scripts and reviews yourself, with an editor (and maybe a designer) executing. Nobody understands the brand better, and you can't assess a senior hire until you've learned what a strong ad looks like. Many founders stay in this seat up to a few hundred thousand dollars of monthly revenue, adding junior editors.

**2. Hire a strategist when you are truly maxed out**, often somewhere around $100K to $200K of monthly revenue, though temperament matters as much: some founders want to stay hands-on. Expectations:
- Early output may be around 60 percent of a senior's (your own) output; you're getting a large part of yourself back, not all of it. Rough estimate, not a benchmark.
- A rule-of-thumb salary range is roughly $5-10K per month (an estimate only; it varies by market and experience).
- You won't fully step away; many strong operators still join ideation and reviews.
- Handing off work isn't handing off focus. A badly managed hire still drains you; a good one generates ideas and reviews their own ads.
- Month one decides it: clear goals, a growth plan, a definition of success, frequent feedback.
Specialists command much higher pay than generalists, and lifting the advertised salary often fixes a stalled search fast.

**3. Recruit like you're selling.** Good people have choices.
- Competitors won't hand over their best people, so don't ask.
- Headhunt from large agencies with real training (tiny ones are hard to poach from), using an assistant and a professional-network search tool. Target people a few years in who've hit a ceiling.
- Pitch the opportunity with a personal video message.
- If top people won't come, hire juniors and train them; it takes a year or more, but they can become worth many times their pay.
- Top talent follows a compelling vision or exceptional pay.

**4. Run a structured process.** Posting, screening, a practical task, a final interview. Ideally three conversations: a screener, someone who does the same job, then you. Insist on one reference call with a past employer you pick. Repeated bad hires mean the process is weak; add a question that would have caught each one.

**5. Hire top-down at higher revenue.** Past roughly $1M a month, bring in senior leads (creative, conversion) who hire beneath themselves, so the founder isn't running every department. A small team of excellent people often outperforms a bigger one.

**6. Use agencies without depending on them.** Agencies add production quality and fresh eyes but still need skilled direction. Check team structure, accounts per team and fit. Pay in a way tied to value (a share of spend is common) that doesn't crush margin. Always keep at least one creative in-house for speed. No breakthrough within about three months is a fair reason to part ways.

**7. Run weekly learnings meetings.** One per brand per week. Focus on ads live for about a week, especially those that just began spending. A proper review takes 10 to 15 minutes, so cover three or four ads. Make it active: everyone presents an ad they analysed, guided by prompts such as "where does the credibility come from?" A team that stays passive after coaching likely lacks the skills.

**8. Set standards and values.** Define winners with data, not self-reported hit rates (see [[Defining Winning, Losing and Breakthrough Ads]]), and reward ads for the spend they help scale over a month rather than a week. Useful values: prove value before asking for the title, move fast, aim for world class. Tie income goals to spend and bonuses, set clear output targets, and keep strong people with promotion paths and juniors to delegate to. If KPIs are hit but the business isn't improving, change the KPIs.

## Example
Say a skincare brand at $60K a month has the founder writing every brief with one editor. At $150K she's the bottleneck, so she advertises a strategist role at a competitive salary and headhunts two juniors from a large agency. The hire starts at about half her output; a growth plan, check-ins and weekly learnings meetings close the gap by month three. An agency handles quarterly shoots.

## Common mistakes
- Hiring a strategist before you can recognise a good ad.
- Expecting the hire to run everything.
- Hiring cheap.
- Relying entirely on an agency.
- Passive learnings meetings.
- Rewarding "winners" that die within a week.
