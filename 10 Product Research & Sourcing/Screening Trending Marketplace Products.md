# Screening Trending Marketplace Products

## What it is
A repeatable screening process for products that are already selling fast on a social-commerce marketplace (such as TikTok Shop). Starting from a best-seller list gets a new store selling fast, since someone has already proven the demand. On its own, though, it is not a durable business: if everyone copies the same products, the race goes to whoever copies fastest. The screen exists to filter out legal and economic traps and, above all, to find a way to stand out before you enter.

## When to use it
- When you are starting out and need a product with proven demand.
- When you want a fresh product idea for an existing avatar.
- Any time you see a product spike and wonder whether it is worth running ads to your own store.

## How (steps)
1. **Build the list.** Pull candidates from a marketplace analytics tool; its cheapest plan is fine. Filter products to the past week, unit prices over roughly $15, and fast-rising sales.
2. **Drop big-brand and protected products.** Large brands and patented products can afford to litigate. Search the brand and check its social following; thin socials and little real branding mean the product is fair game.
3. **Run a trademark search** in every market you will sell in, using the free national database. A trademark protects the name and phrases, not the product. If the name is said aloud in every video, rename both product and brand.
4. **Estimate the competitor's own-site revenue.** Use a traffic estimator: visitors x conversion rate (assume a conservative 1%) x order value. A low figure does not rule a product out: plenty of marketplace sellers neglect their own website, and that gap can be your opportunity. Remember that this estimate stacks assumptions.
5. **Research the market.** Go through the best-performing marketplace videos and the comments beneath them, noting angles, audiences and complaints, then go to forums, Amazon reviews, short-video platforms and Google. See [[Review Mining - Your Reviews and Competitors' One-Star Reviews]] and [[Forum Research for Beliefs and Hidden Segments]].
6. **Review competitor ads** in the free Meta ad library to judge how sophisticated the market is. See [[Researching Competitor Ads in Ad Libraries]].
7. **Check niche health** on a five-year search trend. A steady or climbing line is acceptable; a falling or heavily seasonal one is a no. Viral ingredient trends can collapse quickly.
8. **Sanity-check price.** Take a supplier quote, triple the landed cost, and make sure that figure falls within what the market normally pays. A cheap, flimsy product cannot sustain a premium price.
9. **Find the angle.** Look for a new mechanism, or for an avatar nobody is serving well. Without either one, do not run it. See [[Product Differentiation - How Will You Stand Out]].
10. **Make original creative** based on what you learned. Analyse the highest-earning 10 to 20 videos for their structure and angles, then make original ads; do not re-upload other sellers' content.

## Thresholds
- Beginner price band: roughly $40 to $80. Say a product sells for $170 at a 2x ROAS target with about 20% margin: break-even CPA is about $85. At $100 a day you can see a day or two with zero purchases, which drains a small budget before the platform learns.
- Seasonal items (personalised gifts, fleece wear, car jump starters) surge in Q4 and collapse after it. Plan around that or skip them.
- Heavy and battery-powered items add shipping cost and defect risk for beginners.
- Keep competitor brand names out of your ads; position against "the pricier version" instead.

## Example
Say a self-cleaning litter box has strong marketplace sales, a protected product name, almost no brand presence, modest website traffic and a steady-to-rising trend. Forum threads surface angles: cleaning is a chore, the waste drawer is badly designed, and units die within a couple of years. One safety complaint is serious enough to warrant walking away if it applies to your supplier. A premium competitor sells at several times the price, which opens a product-aware comparison angle: the best-value version, easier to maintain, without naming the incumbent. A different case: comments on a pair of leggings asking for a non-see-through version point straight to a mechanism plus avatar.

## Common mistakes
- Copying a product one-for-one without your own research or angle.
- Running other sellers' videos instead of building creative, which dies quickly anyway.
- Ignoring trademarks because the product itself is generic.
- Treating revenue estimates as facts.
- Entering a seasonal spike without noticing the trend line.
