# Product Focus - When to Pivot or Double Down

## What it is
A decision framework for what to do once a product starts working, or stalls. There are two broad operating styles: a "product a day" machine that keeps testing new items, and a focused approach that turns one momentum product into a brand. Focus usually wins over time, because every improvement to creative, offer, landing page, mechanism and product quality compounds on the same base. Pivoting is a bet, and it should be reserved for specific conditions.

## When to use it
- You have a product getting traction and are tempted to start another.
- A product has stalled at a modest revenue ceiling and you are considering a new brand.
- You are running side stores alongside a main brand.
- You operate in a small market and need a way to keep growing.

## How (steps)
1. **Check the two pivot conditions.** Switch products only when the item is a gimmick (it fails to deliver, so reorders and referrals never come) or demand is shrinking (look at several years of search trend data). If neither applies, the ceiling is usually a skills or execution problem, not a product problem.
2. **Exhaust the current brand first.** Before pivoting, work through better creative, stronger offers, better pages, a sharper mechanism, improved ingredients or components, and supplier visits. Getting one brand properly profitable teaches you what it takes to push past a low ceiling; a struggling second brand mostly drains attention.
3. **Do the time maths on side projects.** Giving half your hours to other stores means the main brand takes about twice as long to work out. Ask yourself whether you can fund twelve months of effort on the main brand. If you can, wind the side stores down or hand them to an operator.
4. **Separate attention from activity.** A high hook rate or lots of engagement without spend is not a signal to switch products; the platform prioritises purchase intent over attention. Diagnose the ad-to-purchase path before blaming the product. See [[Diagnosing Clicks That Do Not Convert]].
5. **Grow through product expansion.** Once the first product is solid, extend the range with complementary items for the customers you already serve, instead of hunting for fresh avatars or countries. Breaking into a new market is typically tougher than launching another product to a market you know, particularly when your home country is small.
6. **Build repeat purchase in order.** Start with product quality, since a single poor experience loses the customer and an excellent one gets them recommending you; then offer related items under the brand they already trust.
7. **Sequence multiple brands.** Operators who run several brands usually push one to a solid seven-figure run, with an operator or marketing lead in place, before launching the next.

## Example
Say a pet-supplement brand is stuck around a few tens of thousands a month and the founder is eyeing a new kitchen-gadget store. The trend line for pet supplements is rising and customers reorder, so neither pivot condition applies. Instead of splitting focus, the founder rewrites the mechanism, adds a bundle offer, visits the factory to improve palatability, and later launches a dental chew for the same dog owners. Each step builds on the existing audience, reviews and creative learnings. By contrast, a novelty gadget with poor reviews and a falling trend line is a genuine pivot candidate, because no amount of marketing fixes a product that does not deliver.

Famous public examples of same-avatar expansion include a premium mattress brand adding sleep-related products, or a wallet brand moving into luggage and travel accessories.

## Common mistakes
- Launching fresh products before squeezing the full potential out of the one already gaining traction.
- Treating every plateau as a product problem.
- Running side stores "just in case" and slowing the main brand.
- Expanding into new countries before exhausting product expansion at home.
- Believing time spent on a product is the same as having earned success with it; without a correction loop, effort alone does not scale.
